First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.1° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, a quarter-second of patience is cheaper than a magazine of misses.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.3°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 125 damage up close, fading to 91 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.31 s, 8 chest shots ≈ 0.73 s up close. Headshots save 57% of the kill time at close range — and with a 200 ms first-shot delay added, the close chest kill takes 0.93 s against 0.51 s for headshots, still a 45% saving. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 15 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 60 m. The gold line would show an ammo option — this gun has none, so there is only the one line.

Rule of thumbInside 15 m you are at maximum power. Past 60 m every kill takes one or two extra bullets — close the distance or fire carefully instead of spraying.

Damage is sliding toward 91 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. Recoil climbs gently up and to the left (angle −12° to −10°), vertical 0.23 — the mildest kick of any full-auto pistol here. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

Aim: chest. Taps at exposed targets only.

At 91 damage you need 10 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The NS-61 Emissary has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 to kill up close versus 8 to the chest, in 0.31 s instead of 0.73 s. Aim at the neck and let the gentle climb carry shots into the skull. It is the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it is free.

  2. Short bursts, even on an automatic

    577 RPM empties 25 rounds in 2.6 seconds if you hold the trigger. Fire in bursts of 4–5 and release between them — the cone resets, the recoil never builds, and you still have rounds for the second enemy.

  3. Swap, don't reload, mid-fight

    Your primary's reload is the Emissary's job. Swapping to the pistol takes about 0.25 s; reloading the primary takes seconds. When the rifle clicks dry in a fight, the pistol is already the faster answer.

  4. Reload early — the penalty is real, the habit is not

    Reloading with rounds left takes 1.8 s; an empty gun takes 2.8 s — a full second more. Twenty-five rounds means you reload less often than any other pistol here — but when you do reload, do it behind cover.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: your aimed cone is 0.30° standing still and 0.30° strafing — moving costs you nothing in cone size on this gun, so strafe from the first shot.

  6. Hipfire is a weapon, not a fallback

    Your standing hip cone is 1.0° and the 0.14 m ring at 8 m fits inside his chest. Inside ~8 m, don't aim down sights at all: keep strafing and let the hipfire do the work. Past ~10 m the rings start leaking past him, and that is the moment to switch to the sight.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the NS-61 Emissary pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · 125 damage — 8 chest shots up close

Eight chest shots to kill in 0.73 s is slow for a finisher. A target who lands his own shots first will win the trade.

Play around it: Play around it: Don't trade chest shots. Headshots close the gap entirely — 4 to kill up close in 0.31 s — and 577 RPM forgives a miss (104 ms) better than any slower pistol forgives one.

2 · The cone grows fast if you hold the trigger

Hip bloom is 0.10° per shot and aimed bloom 0.05° — a full mag dump ends in a cone several times wider than the one it started in.

Play around it: Play around it: Bursts of 4–5, release, repeat. Every release resets the cone to 0.30° aimed.

3 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 40 m you are at 106 damage and need 10 chest shots — a long, loud advertisement of your position.

Play around it: Play around it: Tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You are a sidearm — the doorway and the stairwell are your address.

4 · It tempts you to open fights with it

Twenty-five rounds and full-auto feels like a primary. It is not: 125 damage loses to every real primary at every range.

Play around it: Play around it: Open with the primary. The Emissary comes out when the primary is dry, the target is nearly dead, or the range is under 10 m.

5 · Reload is long for a pistol

An empty Emissary takes 2.8 s to reload — longer than most primaries take to swap twice.

Play around it: Play around it: Reload early and only in cover (habit 4). With 25 rounds and a 150-round pool, you can afford to top up after every kill.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 15 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the pattern climb gently up and to the left — that is your gun's fingerprint. Repeat, pulling down-right, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 10–15 m, kill dummies with bursts of 4–5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Swap Drill 3 minutes · habit

    Empty your primary into a dummy, swap to the Emissary (don't reload), and finish the dummy with the pistol. Repeat until the swap is automatic — in real fights you will do it without thinking, which is the point.
