01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.08° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, a heartbeat of patience beats a wasted magazine.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.10°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 143 damage up close, fading to 112 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.26 s, 7 chest shots ≈ 0.52 s up close. At 698 RPM, full-damage output is 1,664 damage per second. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun. Note the mid band: you need 7 chest shots out to 10.2 m, and 8 beyond it — the switch happens almost as soon as your damage starts falling. Magazine: 40 rounds, with 240 in reserve.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The TRAC-5 S has tight hipfire for a carbine (1.5° standing, 2.0° moving), and the moving ring stays chest-sized out to about 14 m. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage to 10 m, a tight 0.10° aimed cone, and a 40-round magazine behind it. Start your crosshair at the neck — the climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in about a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding from 134 toward 118 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil drifts up and to the right (23–25°), so pull your mouse gently down and to the left. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 112 damage you need 9 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The TRAC-5 S has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

Stances — the cone you start with, before the first shot

StanceStarting coneWhat it means for you
Sprinting7.0°Maxed out — the widest the cone can be. This is the section 01 disaster: never start firing from a sprint.
Hipfire, moving2.0°Walking or strafing from the hip. Tight enough to stay on a chest out to ~14 m — this is why the carbine hipfires so well.
Hipfire, standing1.5°Your point-blank default. The 0.21 m ring at 8 m in section 05 is this stance drawn out.
Hipfire, crouched1.0°The tightest hipfire you have (1.5° if you crouch-walk). Crouch when holding a doorway from the hip.
Aimed (ADS), feet planted0.10°Standing or crouched makes no difference here — fifteen times tighter than hipfire. This is why section 01's order matters.
Aimed (ADS), strafing0.30°Three times the planted cone, and you move at half speed (0.5×). Strafe between bursts to stay hard to hit; plant your feet during each burst. The moment you stop moving, the cone drops back to 0.10° — and you can fire 4 shots before bloom grows it back to 0.30°.

The cone grows by +0.05° per shot aimed (and +0.08° per shot from the hip), starting from shot 1. It shrinks back at 20°/s when you stop firing — that's why short bursts stay accurate and long sprays don't. From the hip, the same window is 6 shots: standing still at 1.5°, six shots of bloom (+0.08° each) bring you to 1.98°, just under the 2.0° moving floor.

06

Attachments: what to buy, what to skip

Every slot, judged
for a new player

The TRAC-5 S costs 1,000 certs (699 DBC) to unlock, and it arrives bare — iron sights, no grip, no extras. That's fine: the gun is already good. The free TRAC-5 fires faster (750 RPM vs 698) and kills about 7% quicker up close (480 ms vs 516 ms chest TTK) — the 1,000 certs buy you the underbarrel rail and the full attachment list, not raw speed. Everything below is an optional sidegrade, not an upgrade. Buy in the order shown and stop whenever the gun feels right. One warning first: there is no Extended Magazine for this gun — it simply doesn't exist for the TRAC-5 S, so if a loadout video tells you to buy one, it's for a different weapon.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's up-and-right climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  2. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double. And resist the urge to hold it down: you have 40 rounds, not 100, and a mag dump burns most of your magazine on a single kill.

  3. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.6 s; an empty gun takes longer — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after two kills or ~25 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  4. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  5. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and tight hipfire all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

  6. Use the S — the underbarrel is the point

    The grenade launcher is why this gun costs 1,000 certs. Lob one into a doorway or onto a MAX crash before you push, smoke a revive or a crossing, and let the shotgun save you when someone rounds a corner inside 5 m. Engineers: your ammo pack refills the launcher, so it never runs dry. Light Assaults: jump-jet to a rooftop and lob down onto the point. A TRAC-5 S fired like a plain TRAC-5 is 1,000 certs wasted.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

1 · The first shot kicks hardest

The first round of every burst jumps with a 1.85× multiplier — 0.63° against the usual 0.34°. Yank the trigger and your opening shot climbs off the head before the burst even starts.

Play around it: start each burst a touch low and let the climb walk into the target, or fit the Angled Forward Grip — it cuts that first-shot kick by 60%.

2 · Damage falls off early

Full damage ends at 10 m and minimum damage (112) arrives at just 60 m. Past 39 m you're on 9 chest shots — 13 against a shielded Heavy — with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. Just past 39 m your chest time to kill is 0.69 s — and it only gets worse from there.

3 · Small magazine, slow reload, no fix

40 rounds with a 2.6 s reload (rounds left) — and no Extended Magazine exists for this gun, so the 40-round magazine is all you get. Spray one kill and you're reloading while his friend rounds the corner.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 2) and early reloads (habit 3). Two kills per magazine is a good pace; three is a great one.

4 · Aiming makes you slow

You move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5×), the sight takes 0.15 s to raise, and the gun takes 0.55 s to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your sidearm or tool out.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

5 · The underbarrel costs you the grip

Every underbarrel option sits in the rail slot: fitting the grenade launcher, smoke or shotgun means giving up the Forward Grip's recoil control or the Laser Sight's hipfire — the two attachments that help every single bullet you fire.

Play around it: treat the launcher as a loadout choice, not a permanent fixture. Grip for duels, launcher for squad pushes — and switch back when the push is over.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up and to the right — that's your gun's fingerprint, and the first shot jumps hardest (1.85×). Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — even though your aimed cone caps at 3.0°, bloom still moves the later shots.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 40 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 2.6 s reload, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
