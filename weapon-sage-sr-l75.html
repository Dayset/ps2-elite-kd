SR-L75 — the fast trigger

The fast NSO semi-auto — 218 RPM, a 12-round magazine, and the quickest follow-up of any sniper rifle here. It trades damage per shot for speed, and wins the fights the others lose by missing.

Read this first — this gun is NOT a starter. The SR-L75 costs 1,000 certs (or 699 Daybreak Cash). Your NSO starter sniper rifle is the SR-100 — the SR-L75 is a purchase you make later, because you want the fastest follow-up and the biggest magazine, not a gun you start with.

The one-line verdict: the fastest trigger in the class. 275 ms between shots, 12 rounds, and a two-headshot kill at any range. Each bullet hits softer than the SR-100's — three chest shots only to 105 m, four beyond — but you get the next one sooner than anyone else.

01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — clicking as fast as your finger allows while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still working. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.8° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes 0.3 s with a 7x scope (0.2 s with a 4x). Trigger working through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and on this gun, with only 12 rounds and 275 ms between shots, four wasted rounds are a quarter of your magazine gone for nothing.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine. You have a cloak, not an overshield, to hide behind — and the cloak won't save a shot you already wasted. A clean first shot is worth more than a rushed spray.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.
02

Know your damage

How many bullets to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 400 damage up close, fading to 300 at long range — SR-L75 damage. Headshots count ×2.0, leg shots count less (×0.9). Sniper rifles use a separate Sniper Rifles resistance type: body and leg shots are reduced against Nanoweave wearers (about 20% — implied by the wiki's effectiveness tables), so the chest count rises in the far bands. Headshots ignore it.

Perfect-accuracy times: 2 headshots = 275 ms at any range — the fastest two-headshot kill here; 3 chest shots = 550 ms to 105 m, 4 chest shots = 825 ms beyond it. 1 headshot + 1 chest shot = 2 shots = 275 ms to 15 m (800 + 400 = 1,200); at 150 m it takes 3 shots = 550 ms (600 + 600 + 300 = 1,500). Against a shielded Heavy Assault (1,450 health): 2 headshots up close, 3 at range, or 4 chest shots up close and 5 at range. In real fights you will land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun: two head hits end it in 275 ms, and no bolt-action can fire its second shot in 275 ms.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 15 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 150 m. The headshot count is what matters — see the table above.

Rule of thumbInside 15 m you are at maximum power — 3 chest shots, 2 headshots. Past 105 m every kill takes one extra chest bullet (4 instead of 3), but the head stays at 2 all the way out. That is the gun's signature: the fastest pair in the class, paid for with the softest bullet — learn the neck, because the chest alone stops being enough past 105 m.

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and slows you down — at arm's length that gets you killed. But be honest with yourself: this gun's hipfire is loose (5.75° standing, 6.25° moving), so keep it truly close, strafe side to side (A/D) while firing, and let your 400-damage bullets do in three hits what a starter needs five for.

8–15 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident pairs

Aim: neck / upper chest. Two aimed shots, 275 ms apart.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 400 damage, a 0.00° starting cone, and the fastest follow-up here. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural straight-up climb walks your second shot into the head, and 2 head hits end the fight in 275 ms. Your home range as an Infiltrator: close enough to reach your squad, far enough that the enemy's hipfire can't touch you.

15–105 mControlled

ADS, short pairs, pull straight down

Aim: chest. One aimed shot at a time, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 337 but you still only need 3 chest shots — and still only 2 to the head. Fire a shot, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (angle −5°/+5°, no sideways bias), so pull your mouse gently straight down — no sideways correction needed. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

105 m +Long lane

Tap-fire — heads only, the chest has faded

Aim: head. One aimed shot at a time at exposed targets.

Past 105 m the chest takes 4 shots (825 ms) — but the head still only takes 2 (275 ms). Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets, and aim for the head: the chest kill has faded, the head kill hasn't. Your bullets are the slowest of the NSO semis (500 m/s), so hold over more than you think.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (how you work the trigger). The SR-L75 is a semi-automatic sniper rifle, so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger and the bolt — not a setting on the gun. And with a 6x scope or higher, a third thing matters: scope sway — the crosshair drifts, more the higher you zoom. Hold Breath (Shift by default) almost completely suppresses it; the Target Focus implant extends how long you can hold it.

One shot – pause – one shot: the paced rhythm

Every shot widens your cone of fire ("bloom"). Releasing the trigger for a heartbeat resets it. Same 4 shots, two very different outcomes:

The target pattern is one aimed shot, a pause, and one more aimed shot — that is the ideal. Past that, the bloom (0.3° per shot aimed) keeps growing with every shot from the very first one, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short strings also leave you rounds for the second enemy — remember, the magazine is only 12, and a 4-shot spam is a third of it gone.

7x scope — 7x

At 150 m through the 7x scope, the target is big enough to place a headshot — and you still see the ground around him. Fast to aim, nothing blocks your edges. Scope-in is 0.3 s; hold your breath (Shift) for the shot.

12x scope — 12x

Same target, same 150 m — but through the 12x scope he looks nearly twice as tall again, so placing shots on his head gets much easier. The price: your view narrows to the round window, the world outside the glass is gone, and the sway looks twice as big. Best past 100 m.

That's the whole 30-cert decision from section 06 in one picture: 7x for the fight you are in, 12x for the shot you are planning. Most veterans carry both and swap per base — 7x for open ground, 12x for the long lane.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats attachments

The gun is fine — your habits decide the fights. Six things to do on every engagement, in order.

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 2 kills at any range versus 3–4 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural straight-up climb carry the second shot into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free. At 275 ms for two heads, no starter out-kills you inside 105 m — and past 105 m the head is the only shot that still kills in two.

  2. One shot, reset, one shot

    Every shot widens your cone of fire by 0.30° aimed, from the very first shot — the worst ADS bloom of the NSO semis. Firing again before it resets means the second bullet leaves a 0.30° cone, past the moving floor. Click once, release, let it reset. With 12 rounds you can afford a miss — but not two.

  3. Use the magazine — four kills per mag

    Twelve rounds is four kills (three chest shots each) and a spare — the biggest magazine of any sniper rifle here. Don't reload after every kill like the 10-round guns do: three kills, then reload. Count roughly: after nine rounds, duck behind cover and reload early.

  4. Pre-aim the doorway, then peek — don't strafe-duel

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. And you move at 0.75× while aiming — faster than any other sniper — so peek, fire your pair, and break line of sight instead of standing in the open trading shots.

  5. Pick fights at your range, not theirs

    Your damage, accuracy and bullet speed all shine at 15–105 m, and your two-headshot kill holds at any range while theirs fades. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you. Never chase a fight into arm's length if you can help it: that's where the 750 RPM guns live.

  6. Cloak is your reload

    You're an Infiltrator, and the cloak is your escape — but a cloaked Infiltrator still has to reload. When the magazine runs dry, cloak first, then reload behind cover, then reappear somewhere else. The SR-L75's 2.1 s reload is an eternity in the open; behind a cloak it is free. Never reload in the open and never fight the second enemy with an empty gun.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them, play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the SR-L75 pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · The softest bullet here

400 damage up close, 300 at range — the lowest damage of any sniper rifle on the page. Past 105 m the chest takes 4 shots (825 ms), and against Nanoweave the chest takes 4 shots at every range. The head is the only shot that never fades.

Play around it: aim at the neck (habit 1). The 275 ms pair is the fastest here — but only the headshot pair stays fast at every range.

2 · The worst ADS bloom of the NSO semis

0.30° of bloom per shot aimed — double the SR-100's, triple the SR-150's. The first shot already pushes the cone past the 0.25° moving floor, so there are no free follow-up shots on this gun.

Play around it: one shot, reset, one shot (habit 2). The fast refire is a trap if you use it — the bloom punishes speed harder than the refire rewards it.

3 · The slowest bullet of the NSO semis

500 m/s — the lowest velocity of the three NSO semi-autos. The bullet drops 0.60 m by 200 m and 1.35 m by 300 m, and moving targets need real lead.

Play around it: hold over more than you think (section 03), and take the shot at stationary or slow targets. Your 275 ms pair punishes a miss less than a bolt-action's — but the drop punishes a lazy aim.

4 · It never one-shots

Two headshots, always — 400 × 2 is 800, two hundred short of a kill. Every bolt-action here kills in one headshot to 246 m. Your edge is the 275 ms second shot, not the first.

Play around it: aim at the neck and fire the pair (habit 1). The second shot is the fastest of any sniper rifle here — but only if the first head lands.

5 · Hipfire is its worst fight

5.75° standing and 6.25° moving from the hip — wide for a sniper rifle. A hipfire duel under 10 m against a fast-firing rifle is a fight you're built to lose.

Play around it: keep your distance (habit 5), ADS even at 10 m, and if someone does close on you, backpedal while hipfiring rather than standing still — or cloak and reposition.

6 · It costs 1,000 certs — and the SR-100 is free

A thousand certs buys a lot of Infiltrator tool ranks, suit slots and abilities. What you're buying over the free SR-100 is a faster refire (275 ms instead of 300 ms), a bigger magazine (12 instead of 10), a faster ADS strafe (0.75× instead of 0.5×) — and a softer bullet with worse bloom.

Play around it: buy it for the speed — the fastest pair and the biggest magazine in the class — and only after your cloak and suit are in decent shape. If you mostly fight past 105 m, the free SR-100 is the better buy.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area ~15 minutes a session

Five drills, all in the VR Training room — no certs, no enemies, no pressure. About 15 minutes a session; do one set, then go fight.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint: a −5°/+5° angle, barely any sideways drift at all. Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own. Two headshots is the whole gun — make the neck your default.

  • One-Shot Ladder 4 minutes · bloom discipline

    At 30–50 m, kill dummies with single aimed shots, releasing the trigger fully between shots. If a shot goes wide, slow down — the 0.30° bloom means the second shot is never free on this gun. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round. Count your magazine while you do it — 12 rounds is four kills at best, so every wild round is a quarter of a kill thrown away.

  • Peek Drill 3 minutes · the section 01 rhythm

    Stand behind a pillar 15–25 m from a dummy. Step out, stop, ADS, fire one pair, step back behind cover. Repeat until stop → aim → fire is one motion instead of three decisions. This is the rhythm that keeps a 0.75×-speed Infiltrator alive: you never stand in the open trading shots.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 9 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Remember: a short reload is 2.1 s and an empty one is 2.7 s, so the last 3 rounds are your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.
