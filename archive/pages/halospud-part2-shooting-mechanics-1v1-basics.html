Shooting Mechanics Cone of fire Each weapon in Planetside has an RNG cone ahead of it in which bullets are fired. Making this cone bigger decreases your accuracy because the RNG that decides where in that cone your bullet is going to go will have a larger off-target area, increasing the chance of a miss.

Your CoF is larger when you hipfire and when you move. It’s much smaller when you aim down sights, stand still or crouch. Basically there’s a trade-off between mobility and accuracy, so in return for becoming a much easier target to hit, you get a better CoF.

In almost every scenario it is much better to move when fighting anyway. It makes people miss more bullets and that’s worth way more than a tiny CoF improvement. Stationary targets are very easy to kill.

Your CoF also grows as you fire rounds. If you hold down the trigger of an automatic weapon you’ll notice that your reticule gets wider and the bullets spread out over an increasingly large area the longer you hold it down.

However, it resets really fast when you release mouse1. This means burstfire is definitely the best way to use your gun in most situations.

This video uses Planetside development tools to show the effective cone of fire on screen. It also shows just how quickly it shrinks when bursting.

Recoil There are two kinds of recoil to manage in Planetside, vertical and horizontal. Vertical recoil is the way that the gun kicks upwards when you fire and horizontal is the way it rattles from side to side a bit when you fire.

Vertical recoil is the easiest to counter. This can be done by simply pulling the mouse down the right amount to negate the recoil so that your cursor stays on target.

Inverted Y-Axis: A smaller percentage of players use an inverted Y axis so they have to push the mouse up to negate recoil. There are literally no benefits or downsides either way, inverted Y axis is entirely a matter of preference.

Managing vertical recoil does have a few little quirks that you need to be aware of though. Firstly, it’s not always exactly vertical. Each weapon has a recoil angle range.

You can see the recoil angles for each weapon in the PlanetStats spreadsheet under the Recoil Angle column. 0 is perfect vertical recoil, a positive value gives a right bias and a negative value gives a left bias.

Unnecessary Detail: The recoil angle is randomly selected from within the angle range for each round fired so it often feels like extra horizontal recoil. For newer weapons and more recently balanced ones, recoil angle is used to create horizontal recoil (e.g. the Promise is -5 +5 so averages at zero but can kick a little to either side.)

Recoil angle can take a little getting used to. It has a more marked impact for precision aiming at range, where being a pixel off target is more significant. At closer ranges you don’t notice it so much. The best way to get used to it is simply to use a weapon that has it for a long time and it will happen naturally.

More important is the first shot recoil multiplier (also visible in the Recoil First Shot Mod column of the PlanetStats spreadsheet.) Basically the vertical recoil for your first shot in a burst is multiplied by this value. So the first shot in a burst kicks a lot more than the ones that follow it. Managing this is one of the key basic skills of aiming in Planetside and it is possible with time and practice to negate it completely and keep the crosshairs steady.

Horizontal recoil cannot be countered by mouse movement, you can only correct for it afterwards that way. It can, however, be mitigated to an extent by using shorter bursts.

Bursting This video is a great example of good bursting and you can hear it more than you can see it. The main benefit of this is that it keeps your cone of fire tight, so that your rounds are going exactly where you’re aiming.

Shorter bursts also decrease the likelihood of hitting the maximum horizontal recoil tolerance so your cursor doesn’t kick to the sides as much as it might. This is especially helpful when using some of the more CQC orientated weapons at longer ranges. Decreasing the number of rounds per burst basically improves how controllable the weapon is at range and therefore how accurate you are.

The tiny gaps in between bursts can also give you a little window where it’s much easier to make aim corrections for target movement and recoil to get your crosshairs back on track.

The downside of bursting is the first shot recoil multiplier. It does make vertical recoil slightly more of a thing, but vertical recoil is mangeable. At first you might need to think about it and make a conscious effort to keep your crosshairs precise while bursting, but over time it becomes increasingly natural and automatic. It’s just a case of using a nice low sensitivity and building muscle memory.

Hipfire and Aiming Down Sights (ADS) So we’ve covered how key it is to use burstfire and keep your cone of fire as tight as it can be and that might make you think that you want to ADS as much as possible but this isn’t necessarily the case.

Hipfire is absolutely valid in very close engagements and with some SMGs and carbines it’s absolutely valid to use it out to slightly longer ranges too. It removes the need to aim in, which removes a very slight delay but more importantly there’s a movement speed penalty for aiming so the regular strafe speed is always higher.

Basically when you hipfire you can shoot a fraction earlier and you are more mobile. As Planetside has no real movement acceleration perfectly tracking targets as they change direction is extremely rare and that makes good movement very powerful. It will force misses from your opponent so the worse cone of fire that you get for hipfiring is often worth it.

  • Always ADS at range
  • Always ADS when the enemy is using a head glitch / partial cover position
  • It’s a strong option to ADS when you are low on health and need to secure a quick kill with headshots in order to survive
  • ADS when the enemy is not moving (get those easy headshots)
  • Always hipfire in extreme close quarters

Outside of that you need to use your judgement but try to hipfire more when using carbines and SMGs to take full advantage of that movement speed and make sure that you keep moving and changing direction.

TLDR

  • Always use burstfire
  • Shorter bursts keep your cone of fire very tight
  • Shorter bursts mitigate horizontal recoil
  • Shorter bursts increase vertical recoil due to the first shot multiplier, but learning to manage this is a big part of Planetside gunplay
  • Keep the gap between your bursts as short as possible to maximise DPS
  • Keep moving when shooting, stationary targets are easy to kill
  • Hipfire is great in CQC. It allows higher movement speed which forces more misses from your opponent

1v1 Basics An infantry engagement begins when either you or your opponent rounds a corner so that you have line of sight on each other. That’s pretty simple but how well you handle that peeking process is as big a part of winning the engagement as aiming is.

Crosshairs Placement The basic principle of crosshairs placement is to give yourself as little work to do in aiming as possible. You want to keep your sights just on the edge of the corner where your opponent’s head is going to appear before it arrives there so that when they peek all you have to do is fire and track your target. You’re basically pre-aiming the corner to give yourself as little work to do as possible.

This cuts down the delay in you having to react, aim and fire and also reduces the amount of aiming you have to do, making it less likely that you’ll miss.

To do it, first you need to know where enemies will be coming from (i.e. which corner or doorway are they going to peek you from.) You can get a strong idea of this by looking at the map, seeing where the friendly dots are, where the enemy ones are and this will help you predict where it’s most likely that enemies will be approaching you from.

Once you’ve decided which corner or doorway they’ll be coming from, aim just past it and at head level. When an enemy peeks now, they will be placing their head in your crosshairs for you.

It’s a simple and fairly instinctive principle but getting more disciplined about doing it will help you a lot. In the first guide we covered the importance of having a low mouse sensitivity to improve the precision of your aim. When you’re getting to grips with low sensitivity at first, good crosshairs placement is the thing that will help you the most because you don’t want to have to swing the mouse around all the time to acquire targets. That will come with time.

Minimising Line of Sight Crosshairs placement is not just about sitting and waiting for enemies though. You can do it while moving too, by holding your aim just on the edge of the corner so that as you round it enemies are right in your sights.

The key to getting this right (and in truth a fundamental principle of not dying all the time in Planetside) is to minimise lines of sight. If you peek too far around the corner and there are multiple guys aiming at you, you’re at a big disadvantage.

It’s good practice, especially when you’re just starting out on the path to higher quality infantry play, to avoid 1v2 engagements as much as possible. Be disciplined about your peeking so that you don’t go too far. If you place your crosshairs well, round the corner so you can only see one enemy and get him under your cursor, you’ve got a really good chance to win that engagement and move on to another one, chaining those kills.

It also means that you’re very close to cover, so if you do feel you’re losing or disadvantaged in that 1v1 you can quickly duck back into cover breaking line of sight.

Because it’s harder to dodge when peeking this way (your movement options are limited because you can’t peek out too far) aiming for the head is very important. It’s also better to use the highest DPS CQC orientated weapons that you can when taking these kinds of engagements as you’ll have a better chance of securing the kill.

To ADS or Not to ADS When peeking a corner you should not be aiming down sights. Aim down sights as you get around the corner. Aiming down sights applies a movement speed penalty as you strafe so doing that will mean you come out of the corner more slowly. You’ll lose any ping advantage that way and give your opponent a fraction more time to react.

Peek in hipfire but ADS immediately afterwards unless using a really good hipfire weapon or your opponent is immediately there in front of you.

If you are going to ADS, do not begin firing before you’ve aimed down sights. If you start firing before aiming, you will retain the hipfire cone of fire penalties even when aimed.

Very often there are stalemates around corners and doorways where both sides are preaiming the doorway and holding position. Pushing into that is difficult and even in a best case scenario you’ll take damage with each peek and be worn down. It’s usually better to try and flank rather than take these kinds of engagements.

Before trying a tough fight like that where your position isn’t a strong one, always ask yourself whether there might be a better option, such as a less well defended entrance.

ADAD In engagements where you have a bit more space to play with and don’t have concerns about ending up in a 1 vs many situation, movement becomes much more important.

The whole point of aiming down sights in FPS games is that you get improved killing power at the cost of being much more vulnerable. Slow or stationary targets are really easy to headshot.

Just a little movement by alternating pressing the A and D keys will cause your opponent to miss more rounds in 99% of engagements. How you time your A and D switches has quite a big impact on how effective this is. If the gap is too long, your opponent will get their tracking aim back on you more easily, but too short and your overall movement amount is so small that they’ll hit you by accident often enough just aiming at a rough area.

There’s a rhythm to good ADAD that will come with time but most players leave too long a time between switching directions so if in doubt try alternating the keys a little quicker and see how it works out for you.

One thing that makes ADAD much more effective is 0.75 ADS speed weapons. When you aim down sights, most weapons apply a movement speed penalty of 0.5, but a handful set it to 0.75 and this makes ADAD more effective.

These weapons include SMGs, the NS15 and NS11 weapons, the Zenith, Bandit and Jaguar Carbines and some Assault Rifles. You should take full advantage of ADAD and peek advantage with these weapons as it’s one of their major strengths.

Peek First Advantage Aim isn’t the only thing that decides who wins a 1v1, not even close. You can’t perform reliably well without factoring the mechanics of FPS games into your play and the peek advantage is one of the biggest ones. The peek first advantage is at its core a measurement of time.

Your ping + server delay + your opponents ping = peek advantage.

The aggressor in a 1v1 (the guy who peeks first) has that amount of time until the packet telling his opponent that he’s peeking arrives and that gives him a significant advantage. Certainly time to get a round or two off.

First things first, slow peeking by aiming down sights and strafing a corner pretty much negates this advantage because you’re moving so slowly that you don’t cover enough distance in that time to actually take an advantage.

The way the player models are and your viewpoint is placed means that the enemies around a corner can sometimes see your shoulder, feet or weapon before you can see them. Combine that with slow-peeking and you can even be handing a small advantage to the other guy. (This is explained in depth in Serenity’s R6S video. Different game but the same concepts apply.)

To take the peek first advantage you want to move quickly. To start with just strafe out without ADSing and ADS as soon as you see your opponent. Make sure you have your hipfire crosshairs ready in the right spot though so that when you ADS you don’t have much work to do with the mouse.

You can even do this when playing defensively by peeking the corner and ducking back into cover repeatedly until you see someone out there. In this way you can hold an angle defensively while still giving yourself a good chance at taking the peek advantage.

There are other ways of using this that we’ll cover in more detail in later guides.

Partial Cover / Head Glitching In this video Therum explains the mechanics of head glitching and in this video MrGuanabana demonstrates how to take advantage of this in practice to make yourself harder to hit.

Your viewpoint is positioned at the head / neck of your player model and that’s where the bullets come from, not actually from your weapon, though the tracer does. This basically means that you can use perspective to give yourself a pretty good view of an enemy player while they can barely see you at all.

There’s a really simple rule about how this works: The player closest to the apex of the partial cover has the advantage. If you’re right behind the box and the other guy is further away then you can see much more of him than he can of you.

It’s not necessarily about height advantage, though it often works out that way because of the way that bases are laid out. The most commonly used head glitch spots are actually at the top of stairs, where you’re a little lower than your opponent but due to proximity to that line at the top of the stairs that blocks line of sight, you have a better view of your opponent than he does of you.

These spots are strong and MrGuanabana’s video demonstrates that you can be quite creative about finding them. The downside is that if your opponents hits you, it’s almost certainly going to be a headshot. If you take any damage in these spots you should back away immediately because the other guy has his cursor on your head and staying still means a very quick death.

One issue you may encounter when head glitching is that the hitbox for an object in the game sometimes extends beyond the visible edge of the object. You’ll see this most frequently when using barriers and railings as cover that aiming just above the railing results in your bullets hitting the railing. This applies much less with stairs and crates and is less common with terrain too.

TLDR

  • Preaim at corners where you expect the enemy’s head to appear, try to be disciplined about doing this
  • If there are multiple enemies, don’t peek too far, take them one at a time
  • Alternate pressing the A and D keys to strafe and be harder to hit. Getting the timing just right makes this work much better.
  • Being the aggressor can give you a small ping advantage over the other guy
  • Do not aim down sights before peeking, it slows you down reducing your peek advantage
  • Use head glitch partial cover positions to make yourself much harder to hit

Conclusion The idea of short bursts with minimal gaps between them, crosshairs placement, minimising line of sight to avoid getting into 1 vs many situations and using good movement to maximise the peek first advantage is fairly straightforward but in this Sightilicious clip you can see just how powerful it is when you perfect those basic skills and do them in a fairly disciplined way.

Using those skills he’s able to turn a situation in which he’s heavily outnumbered into a series of 1v1s where he has the peek advantage, allowing him to hold his ground and keep notching up the kills long after he would otherwise have been able to, even with his aim.
