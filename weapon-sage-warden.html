Sage Guide · New Conglomerate · Battle Rifle

Warden — four shots, two heads

The New Conglomerate's original battle rifle: 250 damage a shot, a 0.00° aimed cone, and a two-headshot kill inside 15 m. Slow, heavy, and honest about it.

Read this first — this gun is NOT a starter. The Warden costs 325 certs (or 250 Daybreak Cash). It is usable by the Infiltrator, Engineer, Heavy Assault and Combat Medic — every class except the Light Assault. No battle rifle is a starter: every faction's new player starts with a carbine, an assault rifle or an LMG. The Warden is a purchase you make later, because you want the reach.

The one-line verdict: a 250-damage semi-automatic that kills in two headshots inside 15 m and four chest shots — if you tap, re-aim, and never spam. The 0.00° aimed cone is the whole gun: every shot starts exactly where the dot points.

01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — clicking as fast as your finger allows while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still working. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

Four beats, left to right — the same mistake the cards above describe, drawn out.

Same fight, done right. Each beat has a job, and the order is the whole lesson.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.2° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes 0.15 s. Trigger working through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and on this gun, with only 20 rounds and 180 ms between shots, four wasted rounds are a fifth of your magazine gone for nothing.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine. A clean first shot is worth more than a rushed spray, and no class ability covers for a wasted one.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.00°, your first shot lands where the dot points — and the 180 ms refire gives you time to re-aim before the next one.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.
02

Know your damage

How many bullets to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 250 damage up close, fading to 225 at long range — Warden damage. Headshots count ×2, leg shots count less (×0.9). Against a shielded Heavy Assault (1,450 health with the shield up) the chest count rises — last column. A range like "4 → 5" means the count changes inside the band, as the damage slides.

Perfect-accuracy times: 2 headshots = 180 ms up close, 3 headshots = 360 ms at range; 4 chest shots = 540 ms up close, 5 chest shots = 720 ms at range. Against a shielded Heavy Assault (1,450 health with the shield up) it is 3 headshots up close and 4 at range, or 6 chest shots up close and 7 at range. In real fights you will land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun: two clean headshots end it in 180 ms, and no starter rifle does that.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 15 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 75 m. The dashed blue line is with High Velocity Ammunition: 250 → 225 (13–95 m) — see section 06 for what it costs.

Rule of thumbInside 15 m you are at maximum power — four chest shots, two headshots. Step past 15 m and every kill takes one extra bullet — five chest, three heads — and it stays that way all the way out. The falloff is a cliff at 15 m, not a slope: close the distance, or aim for the head.

04

Tap-fire discipline

How you stand and how you work the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (how you work the trigger). The Warden is a semi-automatic battle rifle, so the whole gun is built around one idea: every shot is aimed, and the cone only grows when you stop aiming. On a semi-automatic there is no fire selector to save you — your finger is the fire selector.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats attachments

The gun is fine — your habits decide the fights. Six things to do on every engagement, in order.

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 2 kill inside 15 m versus 4 to the chest, and 3 past it versus 5. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the first-shot kick climb into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free. At 180 ms for two heads, no starter out-kills you inside 15 m.

  2. One shot, reset, one shot

    Every shot widens your cone of fire by 0.1° aimed, from the very first shot. Firing again before it resets means the second bullet leaves a 0.1° cone. Click once, release, let it reset. With only 20 rounds, you can't afford to decorate the wall.

  3. Count your twenty rounds — reload early

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.0 s; an empty gun takes 2.6 s. That extra 0.6 s is more than half a second standing defenceless. After one kill, duck behind cover and reload. Treat the last round as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  4. Pre-aim the doorway, then peek — don't strafe-duel

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. And you move at 0.5× while aiming, same as everyone else — so peek, fire your one shot, and break line of sight instead of standing in the open.

  5. Pick fights at your range, not theirs

    Your damage, accuracy and bullet speed all shine at 10–50 m, and your two-headshot kill holds to 15 m while theirs fades. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you. Never chase a fight into arm's length if you can help it: that's where the 750 RPM guns live.

  6. Reload after every kill

    Twenty rounds is five kills at perfect accuracy and two at real accuracy. When the magazine runs dry mid-fight you are defenceless for 2.0 s — longer than any duel lasts. Reload after every kill, reload behind cover, and never start a fight on a half-empty magazine.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them, play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Warden pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · Only 20 rounds — five kills at best

Twenty rounds is five kills if every round lands, and two or three in a real fight. A sprayed pair of shots can leave you dry mid-fight, and the Extended Magazine only adds ten.

Play around it: tap-fire discipline (habit 2) and early reloads (habit 3). Your 2.0 s short reload is fast — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

2 · A miss costs you 180 ms — and the kill

At 333 RPM there are 180 ms between your shots. Every miss costs you a full 180 ms of kill time, and two misses in a duel hand it to the enemy. Your damage per second is strong on paper, but only if the bullets land.

Play around it: aim first, then fire (section 01), and fire one aimed shot at a time. This gun forgives a bad position; it does not forgive bad aim.

3 · The two-headshot kill dies at 15 m

Two headshots kill only while your damage is still 250 — inside 15 m. Step past it and the head takes three, the chest five, and your 180 ms kill becomes 360 ms. The falloff is a cliff, not a slope.

Play around it: fight inside 15 m when you can, and past it aim for the head anyway — three heads in 360 ms still beats most guns' chest TTK.

4 · Hipfire is its worst fight

3.0° standing and 3.5° moving from the hip — workable point-blank, but the bloom is 0.2° per shot and the refire is slow. A hipfire duel under 10 m against a fast-firing rifle is a fight you're built to lose.

Play around it: keep your distance (habit 5), ADS even at 10 m, and if someone does close on you, backpedal while hipfiring rather than standing still.

5 · Slow to bring out, slow to reload dry

It takes 850 ms to equip, and an empty reload is 2.6 s against a 2.0 s short reload. Swapping to your pistol mid-fight, or running the magazine dry, leaves you defenceless for the better part of a second.

Play around it: reload early and only in cover (habit 3), and decide which tool is in your hands before the fight starts, not during it.

6 · It costs 325 certs — and the magazine never stops being 20 rounds

Three hundred and twenty-five certs buys a lot of class ability ranks, suit slots and tools. What you're buying is the reach — 250 damage at 60 m where your starter rifle has already fallen off — and only that.

Play around it: buy it for the reach — the 540 ms four-shot kill at 15 m and the two-headshot kill inside it — and only after your class tools are in decent shape. If you mostly fight past 100 m, a sniper rifle is the better buy.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area ~15 minutes a session

Five drills, all in the VR Training room — no certs, no enemies, no pressure. About 15 minutes a session; do one set, then go fight.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up and drift right — that's your gun's fingerprint: a 25° angle with a rightward bias. Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own. Two headshots is the whole gun inside 15 m — make the neck your default.

  • One-Shot Drill 4 minutes · the tap rhythm

    At 30–60 m, kill dummies with two headshots each inside 15 m and three past it, reloading after every fifth kill. If a dummy takes an extra head, you were past the falloff or you missed — slow down. Goal: five dummies, twenty rounds, no reload mid-kill. Count your magazine while you do it — 20 rounds is five kills, so every wild round is a fifth of a kill thrown away.

  • Peek Drill 3 minutes · the section 01 rhythm

    Stand behind a pillar 15–25 m from a dummy. Step out, stop, ADS, fire one shot, step back behind cover. Repeat until stop → aim → fire is one motion instead of three decisions. This is the rhythm that keeps a 0.5×-speed rifleman alive: you never stand in the open trading shots.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 4 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Remember: a short reload is 2.0 s and an empty one is 2.6 s, so the last round is your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.
