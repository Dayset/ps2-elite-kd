Betelgeuse 54-A — the auraxium LMG that never reloads

The reward for completing the Master Light Machine Guns directive: an Orion that trades its magazine for a heat gauge. No reloads, no ammo packs, no running dry — just a gauge you have to respect.

The one-line verdict: the fastest-killing LMG in the starter class, tied with the CARV and Orion — with infinite ammo and a 3.0-second heat reload. Its price is the gauge: 45 shots, then you manage the heat or it manages you.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 143 damage up close, fading to 112 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.24 s, 7 chest shots ≈ 0.48 s up close. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun. Maximum damage per second is 1,788 — tied with the CARV and Orion for the highest of the starter class. The breakpoints are exact: 7 chest shots to 10.3 m, 8 to 41.9 m, 9 past that.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 10 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 65 m. The teal line shows Soft Point Ammunition, which stretches full power to 15 m.

Rule of thumbInside 10 m you are at maximum power. Past 42 m every kill takes 9 chest shots — close the distance or burst carefully instead of spraying. And remember: those 7–9 shots come out of a 45-shot heat gauge, not a magazine — section 03 explains the gauge.

How to fight hereAim down sights at the upper chest and neck. Fire in bursts of 5–8 — your recoil climbs into the head for free damage.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The Betelgeuse has the tightest standing hipfire of any starter-class LMG (2.5° cone — the 0.35 m ring at 8 m in section 04). Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let the overshield soak the return fire.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage, fast handling, and a 0.48 s chest kill. Start your crosshair at the neck — the recoil climbs straight up, so it walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull straight down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 112 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil is a straight vertical climb (angle 0°), so pull your mouse gently down — no sideways correction needed, just fight the climb. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 112 damage you need 9 chest shots — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

03

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The Betelgeuse has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun. And then there is the third thing no other starter LMG has: the heat gauge.

Standing still, your cone holds its starting size for the first 6 aimed shots (7 from the hip) before per-shot bloom starts widening it — the first burst is free accuracy, which is one more reason the ideal burst is 5 rounds. Aimed bloom caps at a 3.0° cone — and at 0.05° per shot you'd need 58 shots to reach it, more than the 45-shot gauge holds, so you cannot max out the aimed cone in one gauge. Horizontal recoil is 0.22° per shot with a 0.80° tolerance, the recoil angle is 0°/0°, and recovery runs at 13°/s — the sideways jitter is small and directionless, which is why the climb reads as straight up and fades fast between bursts.

The heat gauge — your magazine that refills itself

Here is what makes the Betelgeuse different from every starter LMG, including the Orion it is built from. There is no magazine and no reserve ammo. Every shot pours heat into a gauge; stop shooting and the gauge drains on its own. You never reload because you are empty — you manage heat because the gun will stop you if you don't.

Heat numberValueWhat it means
Shots before overheat45One full gauge of fire — 3.6 seconds held down. Census data: 999 heat capacity at ~22 heat per shot, so 44 shots leave you one short of the line and the 45th crosses it.
Cooling delay0.572 sAfter your last shot, the gauge sits for just over half a second before it starts draining.
Cooling rate232 / sAbout 10 shots' worth of heat per second. A 7-shot kill (155 heat) is fully cooled in ~1.2 seconds; a 20-shot burst (~444 heat) in ~2.5 seconds.
Overheat penalty3.0 sFire the shot that fills the gauge and the gun locks for 3.0 seconds — then it still has to cool all the way to zero before it fires again. Total silence: roughly 7–8 seconds.
Manual reload (R key)3.0 sPressing R clears the gauge in 3.0 seconds, at any heat level. This is your "reload" — the only one the gun has.
Reserve ammoNoneNo ammo pool at all. You never run dry, never visit a terminal, never pick up ammo. The overheat is the price of that freedom.

Reload on your terms

After a fight, if the gauge is high, press R — 3.0 seconds and it is clear. But if you fired fewer than about 25 shots, just wait: passive cooling (0.572 s delay + heat ÷ 232) clears that faster than a 3.0-second reload. High gauge → press R. Low gauge → keep moving, it cools itself.

One gauge, several kills

45 shots is 6 kills at perfect accuracy up close (7 shots each) — realistically 3–4 with misses. That is the gun's whole economy: never dump a gauge on one doorway, and never let it hit the top mid-fight. Treat the last 10 shots of heat as your emergency reserve, not your working gauge.

The one heat ruleAn overheat costs you ~7–8 seconds of silence. A manual reload costs 3.0 seconds. Waiting out a half-full gauge costs ~2 seconds. The overheat is the only expensive option — so it is the only one you must never choose. Burst, pause, press R early.

05

Attachments: what to buy, what to skip

Every slot, judged
for a new player

Old guides will tell you directive weapons take no attachments. That was true before the March 2022 Arsenal Update — it isn't anymore. The Betelgeuse takes attachments in every slot: optics, barrel, rail and ammo. One honest limit: the barrel choice is short. There is no compensator, no suppressor and no heavy barrel for this gun, and the heat gauge can't be extended. Everything below is an optional sidegrade, not an upgrade. Buy in the order shown and stop whenever the gun feels right.

Ammo — two options, both honest

Soft Point Ammunition

First buy100 certs

Full 143 damage now reaches to 15 m instead of 10 m (the teal line on the chart above). Cost: bullets fly 10% slower (540 → 486 m/s).

Good: nearly always — most duels happen at 10–20 m, exactly where this extends your 7-shot / 4-headshot kill.

Bad: long-range duels against runners, where the slower bullet needs more lead. A small price.

High Velocity Ammunition

Situational100 certs

Bullets fly 10% faster (540 → 594 m/s) and minimum damage holds out to 85 m instead of 65 m — but full damage ends at 8 m instead of 10 m.

Good: long lanes and tower fights, where leading moving targets at 40–60 m is the hard part.

Bad: close fights — you give up 2 m of full damage for speed you don't need indoors.

07

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Shield first, then shoot

    Your overshield (F key) is extra health that only helps if it's on before the bullets arrive. Hear footsteps or expect a corner? Shield up, then peek. A shielded Heavy needs 11 chest shots to drop up close instead of 7 — that gap wins duels.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per gauge roughly double.

  4. Never overheat — reload on your terms

    Pressing R clears the heat gauge in 3.0 s, at any heat level — but an overheat locks you out for ~7–8 seconds. Fired fewer than ~25 shots? Just wait — passive cooling clears that faster than a reload. Fired more? Duck behind cover and press R early. Treat the last 10 shots of heat as your emergency reserve, not your working gauge.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving Heavy is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and shield all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

08

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Betelgeuse pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · Heat punishes long holds

A held trigger burns the whole gauge in 3.6 seconds — and the overheat that follows costs you ~7–8 seconds of silence.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). The gauge is a budget: spend it in 5-round bursts and it lasts the whole fight.

2 · Only 45 shots per gauge — small for an LMG

The Orion carries 50 in a magazine, the CARV 100. Your 45 vanish fast when suppressing a doorway, and there's no extended-magazine option on a heat weapon at all.

Play around it: burst, don't suppress. Your 3.0 s heat reload is fast — reloading often is the design, not a failure — and you never have to find ammo afterwards.

3 · The harshest first-shot kick of the starter class

Your first shot kicks 2.25× as hard as the rest, on top of 0.40° vertical recoil per shot. In a long burst your aim walks off the target's head fast — you'll see this clearly in the VR drill below.

Play around it: pull your mouse straight down while firing (the climb is vertical, angle 0°). The Angled Forward Grip cuts that first-shot kick by 60% if it keeps bothering you.

4 · Slow to draw

It takes 1.05 s to bring the gun up — a quarter-second slower than the Orion — and the gun takes that long to swap to. Slow if a fight surprises you with your rocket launcher out.

Play around it: keep the Betelgeuse in your hands when a fight is near, and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

5 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 65 m you're at 112 damage: 9 chest shots, 13 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a Heavy — the front line is your address.

6 · No stealth or max-recoil build exists

There is no suppressor and no compensator for this gun — you can't silence it, and you can't buy your way out of the vertical climb the way a CARV owner can.

Play around it: win the fair fight instead: positioning, the overshield, and a heat gauge that never runs dry. The gun's strength was never the attachments.

09

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full gauge without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint. Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5–8, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your shield and movement do the defending.

  • Heat Discipline 3 minutes · the gauge

    Fire exactly 40 rounds at dummies, stop, press R — feel the 3.0-second clear. Then fire until the gun overheats, once, so you know what the lockout feels like and never choose it in a real fight. Then practise stopping at ~35 shots and letting the gauge cool while you reposition — that pause is free.
