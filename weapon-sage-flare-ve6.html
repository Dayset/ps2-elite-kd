01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

Three moments, one bad habit. Read left to right:

1 · Sprinting, trigger held

You start firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone starts at 7.0° — the widest it can ever be, and every hip shot widens it further (+0.12° per shot).

2 · ADS pressed mid-hold

Then you press ADS mid-hold — and nothing resets. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring.

3 · The spray

Two timers stack the deck further: the gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — that's the whole mystery.

The fix — three beats, in order

Same courtyard, same enemy. Three beats, in order — no shortcuts:

1 · Stop — ~0.3 s

Feet. Let go of sprint — and the trigger, if you're already holding it. Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint. Give it ~0.3 s to settle.

2 · Aim — ~0.15 s

Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, your overshield (F) covers it.

3 · Fire — from 0.03°

Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.03°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

The exception — inside ~8 m, skip all three beats

At arm's length, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving. The hip cone (3.75° standing) still covers the chest at that range, and stopping to aim costs you the fight. No ADS needed, stopping optional: strafe, hold the trigger, and let the overshield soak the return fire.

Stop → aim → fireIf you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04. The one exception: inside ~8 metres, hipfire and keep moving (section 03). When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The Flare's hipfire is loose for an LMG (3.75° standing), but at this range the ring still covers the chest. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let the overshield soak the return fire.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 167 damage, mild recoil, a 75-round magazine. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 3 head hits end the fight in a fifth of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull straight down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 143 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (0° angle, no sideways drift), so pull your mouse gently down — no sideways correction needed. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 125 damage you need 8 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The Flare VE6 has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.06° per shot aimed, 0.12° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short bursts also leave you rounds for the second enemy — with 75 in the magazine you can afford two kills per mag, but only if you don't spray the first one away.

Good: nearly always — most duels happen at 10–20 m, exactly where this extends your 6-shot / 3-headshot kill.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Shield first, then shoot

    Your overshield (F key) is extra health that only helps if it's on before the bullets arrive. Hear footsteps or expect a corner? Shield up, then peek. A shielded Heavy needs 9 chest shots to drop up close instead of 6 — that gap wins duels.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 kills up close versus 6 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 4.24 s; an empty gun takes 5.225 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after two kills or ~40 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving Heavy is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and shield all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Flare VE6 pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It loses the pure DPS race up close

577 RPM is slow for an LMG. The VS Orion and TR CARV both fire 750 RPM — if you and an enemy Heavy trade chest shots face-to-face, their bullets add up faster than yours.

Play around it: don't trade fairly. Shield first, aim for the neck, and use corners — your per-bullet damage and accuracy win fights that start on your terms.

2 · The reload is long

4.24 s with rounds left, 5.225 s on empty — the Orion reloads in 3.0 s and the GD-22S in 2.3 s. Caught reloading in the open, you are defenceless for an age, and no attachment speeds it up.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4) — and the Extended Magazine, which turns 75 rounds into 150 so the reload almost never happens mid-fight.

3 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 75 m you're at 125 damage: 8 chest shots, 12 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a Heavy — the front line is your address.

4 · The first shot kicks hardest

Your first shot climbs 1.85× as far as the rest, and every shot climbs straight up (0.4°). In a long burst your aim walks off the top of the target's head — you'll see this clearly in the VR drill below.

Play around it: pull your mouse straight down while firing — no sideways correction needed, the recoil has no sideways drift. The Angled Forward Grip cuts that first-shot kick by 60%.

5 · Aiming makes you slow

Like all LMGs, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5x), and the gun takes 0.9 s to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your rocket launcher out.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

6 · Hipfire is loose — don't hipfire-duel an Orion

3.75° standing, 4.25° on the move. The Orion (2.5°) and the XMG-100 (2.5°) both hipfire tighter than you, so a VS Heavy with the starter gun will out-hipfire you under 10 m.

Play around it: make him fight you aimed, where your 0.03° cone wins — or close to point-blank, where even a loose cone covers the chest. The Laser Sight (section 06) closes the gap to 0.35 m at 8 m.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint (no sideways drift on the Flare, but the first shot jumps hardest at 1.85×). Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5–8, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your shield and movement do the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 40 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 4.24 s reload, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
