01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.12° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — use it to finish sidestepping into your firing line.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.03°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and slows you to 0.75× speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The Bandit has one of the tightest hipfire cones on any NC carbine (1.25° standing — see the 0.17 m ring in section 05). Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track. As Light Assault, your jump jets make this range yours: drop onto a target, don't walk up to him.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 167 damage to 10 m, a 7-shot kill out to ~36 m, and a 0.75× ADS strafe speed no other NC carbine matches — you keep moving while scoped where other carbines crawl. Start your crosshair at the neck — the climb walks your shots into the head, and 3 head hits end the fight in about a fifth of a second. Strafe while scoped; a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding from 157 toward 130 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (0° angle, no sideways bias), so pull your mouse gently down — one direction only. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 112 damage you need 9 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover — or, as Light Assault, over it. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The Bandit has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

Stances — the cone you start with, before the first shot

StanceStarting coneWhat it means for you
Sprinting7.0°Maxed out — the widest the cone can be. This is the section 01 disaster: never start firing from a sprint.
Hipfire, moving1.75°Walking or strafing from the hip. Tight enough to hit reliably inside ~15 m — far better than most carbines manage.
Hipfire, standing1.25°Your point-blank default. The 0.17 m ring at 8 m in section 05 is this stance drawn out.
Hipfire, crouched1.0°The tightest hipfire you have (1.25° if you crouch-walk). Crouch when holding a doorway from the hip.
Aimed (ADS), feet planted0.03°Standing or crouched makes no difference here — about forty times tighter than hipfire. This is why section 01's order matters.
Aimed (ADS), strafing0.35°The moving floor while scoped — nearly twelve times the planted cone — but you still move at 0.75× speed, faster than any other NC carbine while aiming. Strafe between bursts to stay hard to hit; plant your feet during each burst.
Aimed (ADS), crouch-walking0.20°The middle ground: nearly as tight as planted, and you keep creeping forward. Use it to peek a lane.

The strafe window. Standing still and aimed, your cone starts at 0.03° and grows 0.06° with every shot — so after 5 shots it sits at 0.33°, still under the 0.35° a strafing shot starts at. That 5-shot window is the ADAD window: it is not a freeze, the cone grows from the very first shot. From the hip the window is 4 shots (1.25° to 1.75° at 0.12° per shot).

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.06° per shot aimed, 0.12° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short bursts also leave you rounds for the second enemy — and with a 30-round magazine, you have room for about five kills per mag if you land your shots. Remember the trap, though: a small magazine tempts you to reload at the worst moment — reload early, behind cover.

First buy100 certs

Good: nearly always — most duels happen at 10–15 m, exactly where this extends your 6-shot kill.

Bad: long-range duels against runners, where the slower bullet needs more lead. A small price.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Stop, aim, then fire

    The section 01 rhythm is a combat skill, not a classroom exercise. Every fight you start sprinting and hipfiring is a fight you start with a 7.0° cone. Feet, sight, trigger — in that order, every time, until it's automatic.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 kills up close versus 6 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's straight-up climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double. And respect the 30-round magazine: a full mag dump is 2.76 s of noise, and then you're reloading.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.68 s; an empty gun takes 3.08 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after a kill or two, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Strafe while scoped — it's your edge

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: at 0.75× ADS speed you stay mobile where other carbines crawl, and a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back. As Light Assault, add the jump jets: arrive from above, where nobody pre-aims.

  6. Pick fights inside 36 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and hipfire all shine at close-mid range: a 7-shot kill holds to ~36 m, and your aimed cone stays tight far longer than most carbines'. Sprint between cover — or fly over it — and arrive at your range before opening fire. Past 55 m it's 9 shots and 0.76 s. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Bandit pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · The magazine is small

30 rounds is five kills at best, and the reload is slow: 2.68 s with rounds left, 3.08 s on empty. A second enemy rounding the corner while you reload is the Bandit's classic death.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4) — and the Extended Magazine adds 5 rounds, so you reload less often in the first place.

2 · The first shot kicks hardest

The first round of every burst jumps with a 1.75× multiplier. Yank the trigger and your opening shot climbs off the head before the burst even starts.

Play around it: start each burst a touch low and let the climb walk into the target, or fit the Angled Forward Grip — it cuts that first-shot kick by 60%.

3 · Damage fades fast past 36 m

Eight chest shots at 40 m, nine past ~56 m, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. Soft Point Ammunition buys you five more metres of full damage.

4 · Slow bullets

440 m/s is on the slow side for a carbine, so moving targets at 40 m and beyond need real lead — and Soft Point (396 m/s) or a suppressor (374 m/s) make it worse.

Play around it: fit High Velocity Ammunition (484 m/s) for open ground, or get closer before you open fire.

5 · A duellist, not a lane-holder

No compensator, no heavy barrel, no 100-round magazine: sustained suppressive fire isn't this gun's job, and it will lose a staring match with a weapon built for one.

Play around it: play it as the mobile duelling carbine it is — strafe, burst, relocate — and let the LMGs hold the lanes.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint, and the first shot jumps hardest (1.75×). Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — even though your aimed cone caps at 3.0°, bloom still moves the later shots.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — and with a 1.25° hipfire cone, your rounds stay on the dummy while you move. As Light Assault, try it while drifting on the jump jets.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 2.68 s reload, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
