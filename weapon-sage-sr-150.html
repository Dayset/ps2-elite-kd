The heavier SR-100 — slower, harder-hitting, and steadier at range. Same two-headshot kill, a longer falloff, and the tightest aimed cone of the NSO semis.

Read this first — this gun is NOT a starter. The SR-150 costs 1,000 certs (or 699 Daybreak Cash). Your NSO starter sniper rifle is the SR-100 — the SR-150 is a purchase you make later, because you want the longer falloff and the harder hit, not a gun you start with.

The one-line verdict: the SR-100's bigger brother. 465 damage, a 451 ms refire, and an aimed cone that stays inside the moving floor for two shots. You trade follow-up speed for range — the two-headshot kill holds to 200 m instead of 150 m.

01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — clicking as fast as your finger allows while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still working. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

Four beats, left to right — the same mistake the cards above describe, drawn out.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+1.0° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes 0.3 s with a 7x scope (0.2 s with a 4x). Trigger working through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and on this gun, with only 10 rounds and 451 ms between shots, four wasted rounds are a quarter of your magazine gone for nothing.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine. You have a cloak, not an overshield, to hide behind — and the cloak won't save a shot you already wasted. A clean first shot is worth more than a rushed spray.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.
02

Know your damage

How many bullets to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 465 damage up close, fading to 334 at long range — SR-150 damage. Headshots count ×2.0, leg shots count less (×0.9). Sniper rifles use a separate Sniper Rifles resistance type: body and leg shots are reduced against Nanoweave wearers (about 20% — implied by the wiki's effectiveness tables), so the chest count rises in the far bands. Headshots ignore it.

Perfect-accuracy times: 2 headshots = 451 ms at any range; 3 chest shots = 902 ms at any range; 1 headshot + 1 chest shot = 2 shots = 451 ms at any range (930 + 465 = 1,395 up close, 668 + 334 = 1,002 at 200 m). Against a shielded Heavy Assault (1,450 health): 2 headshots up close, 3 at range, or 4 chest shots up close and 5 at range. In real fights you will land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun: two head hits end it at any distance, and the second one is the steadiest of any semi-auto here.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 50 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 200 m. The headshot count is what matters — see the table above.

Rule of thumbInside 50 m you are at maximum power — 3 chest shots, 2 headshots. Past 50 m every kill still takes the same number of shots: the chest stays at 3 and the head stays at 2 all the way to 200 m, because 334 damage is still a third of 1,000. That is the gun's signature: the longest falloff of any NSO semi-auto, and a two-headshot kill that never fades.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and slows you down — at arm's length that gets you killed. But be honest with yourself: this gun's hipfire is loose (6.0° standing, 6.5° moving), so keep it truly close, strafe side to side (A/D) while firing, and let your 465-damage bullets do in three hits what a starter needs five for.

8–50 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident pairs

Aim: neck / upper chest. Two aimed shots, 451 ms apart.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 465 damage, a 0.00° starting cone, and fast bullets (600 m/s). Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural straight-up climb walks your second shot into the head, and 2 head hits end the fight in 451 ms. Your home range as an Infiltrator: close enough to reach your squad, far enough that the enemy's hipfire can't touch you.

50–200 mControlled

ADS, short pairs, pull straight down

Aim: chest. Two aimed shots, pause between pairs.

Damage is sliding toward 334 but you still only need 3 chest shots — and still only 2 to the head. Fire a pair, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (angle −1°/+1°, the tightest here), so pull your mouse gently straight down — no sideways correction needed. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

200 m +Long lane

Tap-fire — and unlike the starters, keep shooting

Aim: chest. One aimed shot at a time at exposed targets.

At 334 damage you still need only 3 chest hits — your kill time doesn't get any worse past 200 m, while other rifles' kill times keep getting worse with range. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Your bullets are fast (600 m/s) and your two-headshot kill is still 451 ms. This is the one NSO semi-auto that genuinely owns long range.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (how you work the trigger). The SR-150 is a semi-automatic sniper rifle, so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger and the bolt — not a setting on the gun. And with a 6x scope or higher, a third thing matters: scope sway — the crosshair drifts, more the higher you zoom. Hold Breath (Shift by default) almost completely suppresses it; the Target Focus implant extends how long you can hold it.

One shot – pause – one shot: the paced rhythm

Every shot widens your cone of fire ("bloom"). Releasing the trigger for a heartbeat resets it. Same 4 shots, two very different outcomes:

The target pattern is one aimed shot, a pause, and one more aimed shot — that is the ideal. Past that, the bloom (0.1° per shot aimed) keeps growing with every shot from the very first one, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short strings also leave you rounds for the second enemy — remember, the magazine is only 10, and a 4-shot spam is a third of it gone.

7x scope — 7x

At 150 m through the 7x scope, the target is big enough to place a headshot — and you still see the ground around him. Fast to aim, nothing blocks your edges. Scope-in is 0.3 s; hold your breath (Shift) for the shot.

12x scope — 12x

Same target, same 150 m — but through the 12x scope he looks nearly twice as tall again, so placing shots on his head gets much easier. The price: your view narrows to the round window, the world outside the glass is gone, and the sway looks twice as big. Best past 100 m.

That's the whole 30-cert decision from section 06 in one picture: 7x for the fight you are in, 12x for the shot you are planning. Most veterans carry both and swap per base — 7x for open ground, 12x for the long lane.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats attachments

The gun is fine — your habits decide the fights. Six things to do on every engagement, in order.

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 2 kills at any range versus 3 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural straight-up climb carry the second shot into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free. At 451 ms for two heads, the pair is the steadiest of any semi-auto here.

  2. Fire in pairs, reset, fire again

    Two quick shots stay inside the moving cone (0.00° → 0.10° → 0.20°) — the only semi-auto here where the pair is genuinely free. The third shot crosses the floor. Click twice, release, let it reset. With only 10 rounds, you can't afford to decorate the wall.

  3. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.25 s; an empty gun takes 2.75 s. That extra 0.5 s is half a second standing defenceless. Count roughly: after three kills or ~9 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last round as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  4. Pre-aim the doorway, then peek — don't strafe-duel

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. And you move at 0.5× while aiming, same as everyone else — so peek, fire your pair, and break line of sight instead of standing in the open trading shots.

  5. Pick fights at your range, not theirs

    Your damage, accuracy and bullet speed all shine at 8–200 m, and your kill time doesn't fade past 200 m while theirs does. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you. Never chase a fight into arm's length if you can help it: that's where the 750 RPM guns live.

  6. Cloak is your reload

    You're an Infiltrator, and the cloak is your escape — but a cloaked Infiltrator still has to reload. When the magazine runs dry, cloak first, then reload behind cover, then reappear somewhere else. The SR-150's 2.25 s reload is an eternity in the open; behind a cloak it is free. Never reload in the open and never fight the second enemy with an empty gun.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them, play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the SR-150 pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · The slowest semi-auto follow-up here

At 133 RPM there are 451 ms between your shots — the slowest refire of any semi-automatic sniper rifle here. Every miss costs you nearly half a second of kill time, and two misses in a duel hand it to the enemy.

Play around it: aim first, then fire (section 01), and fire in pairs. This gun forgives a bad position; it does not forgive bad aim.

2 · It never one-shots

Two headshots, always — 465 × 2 is 930, seventy short of a kill. Every bolt-action here kills in one headshot to 246 m. Your edge is the steady 451 ms second shot, not the first.

Play around it: aim at the neck and fire the pair (habit 1). The second shot is the steadiest of any semi-auto here — but only if the first head lands.

3 · Only 10 rounds — three kills at best

Ten rounds is three kills (three chest shots each) and a spare. A sprayed burst can leave you dry mid-fight, and the Extended Magazine only adds a few.

Play around it: shot discipline (habit 2) and early reloads (habit 3). Your 2.25 s short reload is fast — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

4 · The strongest recoil of the NSO semis

1.2° of vertical climb per shot — the hardest of the three NSO semi-autos — with the tightest recoil angle (−1°/+1°). In a fast pair your second shot is already climbing off the target's head.

Play around it: pull your mouse straight down while firing — the recoil angle is nearly 0°, so there's no sideways correction to learn. And don't plan a third shot from the same aim point: re-aim every pair.

5 · Hipfire is its worst fight

6.0° standing and 6.5° moving from the hip — wide for a sniper rifle. A hipfire duel under 10 m against a fast-firing rifle is a fight you're built to lose.

Play around it: keep your distance (habit 5), ADS even at 10 m, and if someone does close on you, backpedal while hipfiring rather than standing still — or cloak and reposition.

6 · It costs 1,000 certs — and the SR-100 is free

A thousand certs buys a lot of Infiltrator tool ranks, suit slots and abilities. What you're buying over the free SR-100 is 15 more damage per bullet, a longer falloff (200 m instead of 150 m), 50 m/s more velocity, and a steadier pair — and a slower refire.

Play around it: buy it for the range and the steadier pair — the two-headshot kill that holds to 200 m — and only after your cloak and suit are in decent shape. If you mostly fight inside 100 m, the free SR-100 is the better buy.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area ~15 minutes a session

Five drills, all in the VR Training room — no certs, no enemies, no pressure. About 15 minutes a session; do one set, then go fight.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint: a −1°/+1° angle, the tightest here, no sideways drift at all. Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own. Two headshots is the whole gun — make the neck your default.

  • Pair Ladder 4 minutes · the two-shot kill

    At 30–50 m, kill dummies with pairs of headshots, releasing the trigger fully between pairs. If a pair goes wide, slow down. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — and notice how the pair stays tight where other guns spread. Count your magazine while you do it — 10 rounds is three kills at best, so every wild round is a third of a kill thrown away.

  • Peek Drill 3 minutes · the section 01 rhythm

    Stand behind a pillar 15–25 m from a dummy. Step out, stop, ADS, fire one pair, step back behind cover. Repeat until stop → aim → fire is one motion instead of three decisions. This is the rhythm that keeps a 0.5×-speed Infiltrator alive: you never stand in the open trading shots.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 9 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Remember: a short reload is 2.25 s and an empty one is 2.75 s, so the last round is your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.
