01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new sniper makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullet sails past his head. He turns, and you are standing in the open with a bolt to work. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+1.0° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullet keeps flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun. And on a bolt-action, a miss isn't a wasted bullet — it's a 1.7 s rechamber while the enemy knows exactly where you are.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs a moment to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes 0.3 s. Firing through all of it means your one shot — the only one that matters — is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes 0.3 s on this gun — a touch longer than the reflex-sight timing in the strip below, because a magnified scope is slower to raise. with a 6× or higher scope, your crosshair also sways — it drifts on its own, and the higher the magnification, the more the drift shows. Hold Shift (hold breath) to steady it, and let go while the bolt cycles so your breath is back for the next shot.

3 · Trigger. Now fire — once. Starting from the aimed cone of 0°, your shot lands where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

Two headshots land 1.8 s apart (one rechamber), two body shots the same. A headshot kills an Infiltrator (900 HP) at any range — even at minimum damage, 450 × 2.1 = 945 damage is still a kill; the switches in the mid band happen at 262 m (legs), 335 m (chest), 366.0 m (head) and 356 m (shielded Heavy) — two body shots drop a shielded Heavy only to 42 m. Magazine: 4 rounds, with 40 in reserve.

A note on Nanoweave. Sniper rifles use a separate Sniper Rifles resistance type: Nanoweave trims body- and leg-shot damage from sniper rifles, and since the 2026-11-08 Nanoweave rebalance it is the suit slot that matters against snipers. A Nanoweave wearer can need one more body shot than this table says, near the edge of your two-shot range — so if a body shot leaves a man standing at 200 m, assume Nanoweave and aim for the head next. It never touches headshots: your one-shot headshot range is the same against everyone.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 10 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 400 m. The red dashed line is the one-shot headshot threshold (476 damage × 2.1 = 1,000): cross it at 366.0 m and a headshot leaves the enemy alive. The gold line is the two-body-shot threshold (500 damage).

Rule of thumbInside 366.0 m you are a one-shot killer — every fight is decided by the first trigger pull. Past 366.0 m you need two headshots, which costs a 1.8 s cycle: close the distance, or be certain of the second shot before you take the first.

How to fight here—

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. Your hipfire cone is wide (5.5° standing), so this is a desperation range for a sniper: two body shots up close still kill, but a carbine or shotgun owner will usually beat you here. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing, and if you survive the first exchange, reach for your sidearm — it is faster than any rifle at this range.

8–366.0 mOne-shot zone

ADS, head, one shot

Aim: head. One shot, then move.

This is the whole gun: inside 366.0 m a single headshot kills. Scope in (0.3 s), hold your breath, wait for the sway to settle, and take the shot — then break line of sight and reposition, because your shot just put you on the minimap out to 100 m. Your bullet takes 0.24 s to reach 200 m and drops 0.21 m on the way — about a quarter of a standing target — so hold slightly high at the far end of this band.

366.0–400 mTwo-headshot zone

ADS, head, twice — or don't shoot

Aim: head. Two headshots, 1.8 s apart.

Past 366.0 m a headshot leaves the enemy alive with a sliver of health, and your second shot is a full rechamber away. Only take the first shot if you are certain of the second — a stationary target, a man reviving a friend, a target who hasn't seen you. Otherwise close the distance first. Drop at 300 m is 0.47 m and the bullet takes 0.35 s to arrive: lead a walking target by a step, a sprinting one by much more.

400 m +Minimum damage

Same kill, more drop — or don't engage

Aim: head, held high. Two headshots or three chest shots.

At minimum damage the kill is the same as the band before — two headshots, three chest shots — but the drop keeps growing and the flight time keeps stretching. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets only. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too — and a missed long shot tells every enemy in 100 m exactly where you are.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The AF-8 RailJack has no fire-mode switch — bolt-action only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

Scope sway. With a 6× or higher scope, your crosshair drifts on its own — the same amount on every rifle and every scope, but higher magnification makes it look bigger. Hold Shift (hold breath) to almost completely suppress it. Your breath is limited, so release Shift while the bolt cycles and it will be back for the next shot. The Target Focus implant stretches your hold-breath time if you want more of it.

Scope-in time: raising the sight takes 0.3 s on this gun — the 6× and higher scopes are slower to raise than a reflex sight. Add the brief blackout as the magnified overlay appears, and you are looking at roughly a third of a second from decision to shot: pre-aim the doorway or the ridge line before the enemy appears, don't start scoping after you see him.

Fire — rechamber — re-acquire: the bolt-action rhythm

One aimed shot, then the bolt. There is no second shot until the rechamber finishes — 1.7 s of working the bolt, then re-acquire and fire again. Two body shots take 1.80 s plus your aim time; a headshot takes one.

The discipline is the gun. A bolt-action does not forgive a rushed second shot — it simply isn't ready. Aim once, fire once, work the bolt, and let the sway settle before the next shot. The practice window in section 04 trains exactly this: one shot, one rechamber, one re-acquire.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Aim for the head, not the chest

    Headshots deal ×2.1 damage: one kills out to 366.0 m, versus two to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the top of the head and let the sway carry the shot, or hold your breath and put the dot on the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  2. Stop, aim, then fire

    The section 01 lesson, every single time: stop sprinting, scope in (0.3 s), then fire. A shot taken from a sprint is a shot wasted — and on this rifle a wasted shot costs a 1.7 s rechamber while the enemy knows where you are.

  3. Hold your breath — then let it go

    Hold Shift to steady the sway before the shot, and release it while the bolt cycles: your breath is back by the time the next round is chambered. Holding your breath through the whole rechamber just wastes it.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 4.1 s; an empty gun takes 5.1 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after one or two kills, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 4 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Reposition after every shot

    Every shot you fire puts you on the enemy minimap out to 100 m. One kill from a ridge is a triumph; two from the same ridge is a death sentence — the second shot tells every enemy in 100 m exactly where to look. Shoot, move, shoot from somewhere new.

  6. Pick fights inside 366.0 metres

    Your damage, your one-shot range and your whole job all live inside 366.0 m. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the AF-8 RailJack pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · The 0.2 s firing delay

Pull the trigger and the shot leaves 0.2 s later — the longest delay of any infantry weapon. A target walking at 200 m has moved before your bullet even starts flying, and a target who steps behind cover during the delay is safe.

Play around it: Play around it: lead moving targets by more than you think, and only shoot at targets who are standing still or walking predictably. The delay is also a gift: it does your quick-scope timing for you.

2 · The slowest cycle of any bolt here

1.7 s to work the bolt, 1.8 s between shots — and the delay on top. Miss the head and the enemy has nearly two seconds to reach cover or find you.

Play around it: Play around it: only take shots you are certain of. A shot not taken costs nothing; a shot missed costs the kill.

3 · Four rounds, then a 4.1 s reload

Four rounds is the smallest magazine in this guide — and the longest reload. Empty it on a squad and you are defenceless for 4.1 s — 5.1 s on empty.

Play around it: Play around it: reload early, behind cover, after every kill. Never let the magazine hit zero in the open.

4 · Scope sway at 6× and up

Your scope sways, and the sway is magnified with the scope. A rushed shot at 300 m drifts off the head on its own.

Play around it: Play around it: hold Shift before the shot, and release it while the bolt cycles so your breath is back for the next one.

5 · The minimap gives you away

Every shot puts you on the minimap out to 100 m. A sniper who stays in one place is a sniper who gets flanked.

Play around it: Play around it: shoot, move, shoot from somewhere new. The Suppressor (100 certs) removes the dot entirely if you flank often.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • Sway Watch 3 minutes · breath control

    Scope a dummy at 100 m and watch the crosshair drift. Hold Shift (hold breath) and fire when the drift carries the dot onto the head. Release Shift while the bolt cycles — your breath is back for the next shot. Goal: five headshots in a row without a miss.

  • Head-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to head height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Rechamber Rhythm 4 minutes · shot discipline

    One shot per dummy, then rechamber and re-acquire a different dummy — never shoot the same dummy twice in a row. If a shot misses, the next dummy is your penance: take it slow, hold your breath, and make it count.

  • Relocate Drill 3 minutes · repositioning

    Shoot one dummy, then move to a new position before shooting the next — never fire twice from the same spot. This is your survival skill: in real fights, the second shot from the same ridge is the one that gets you killed.

  • Reload Discipline 2 minutes · habit

    Fire 4 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 4.1 s reload, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
