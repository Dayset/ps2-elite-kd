SR-7 — the mid-range bolt-action, mastered

A 1,000-cert bolt-action for the middle distances: the same damage as the starter M77-B, but 600 m/s bullets, an 8-round magazine, and a 6x scope. This page teaches you to hold a lane at 100–250 metres and make every shot count.

The one-line verdict: the M77-B's damage with a faster bullet and a bigger magazine: one headshot kills out to 246.4 m, two body shots to 214 m, and 600 m/s means less lead and less drop at range. You pay for it with the slowest rechamber of the 650-damage tier — 1.57 s between shots.

01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. And a sniper rifle's hipfire is the worst in its class: a 5.0° standing cone, so even standing still your rounds spread across a 0.7 m circle at 8 m.

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun. On a bolt-action it costs double: you burned your one shot, and now you must rechamber for 1.565 s before you can try again.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes 0.30 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and a bolt-action has no second bullet in the chamber to make up for it.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint. A sniper rifle is a planted weapon: your aimed cone is 0.0° only when your feet are still.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, the enemy can't see you yet if you picked your spot. With a 6x scope or higher, hold your breath (Shift) once the sight is up: it almost completely stops the scope sway.

3 · Trigger. Now fire — once. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.0°, your first shot lands where the dot points. Then work the bolt, re-acquire the target, and only then fire again. One aimed shot, rechamber, re-acquire: that is the whole rhythm of this rifle.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the trainer in section 04.

Know your damage

How many shots
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 650 damage up close, fading to 400 at 350 m. Headshots count ×2.1, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising: a headshot kills out to 246.4 m; two body shots kill out to 214 m; and against a shielded Heavy (1,450 health) you need 2 headshots at every range.

Nanoweave armour: since the Arsenal Update, sniper rifles use their own resistance type — Nanoweave cuts body and leg damage by 20% and does nothing to headshots. Aim: the head if scoped, the chest if hipfiring. Keep moving.

At arm's length your scope is a liability: the sight takes 0.30 s to come up and the target is already on you. Hipfire a body shot if you must, but the honest answer at this range is your sidearm — a bolt-action's 1.57 s between shots is an eternity in a room fight.

Aim: the head. One shot, one kill.

Your whole job lives here: feet planted, 6x scope up, breath held, crosshair on the head. One hit ends the fight. Your 600 m/s bullet drops only 0.42 m at 200 m and crosses it in 0.33 s — less lead, less holdover than the M77-B. Don't rush — a miss costs you 1.57 s of rechamber.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (one shot, then the bolt). The SR-7 has no fire-mode switch — bolt-action only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your hands, not a setting on the gun. 6x–12x scopes sway — the crosshair drifts on its own, and higher magnification magnifies the sway. Hold your breath (Shift, default) to almost completely stop it; the Target Focus implant makes the breath last longer.

Shoot — rechamber — re-acquire: the bolt-action rhythm

One shot, then the bolt. The same shot, two very different outcomes:

The target pattern is one aimed shot, then a full rechamber — 1.565 s on this rifle before the next round is ready. The rechamber is not dead time: with the Straight-Pull Bolt you stay scoped and re-acquire while the bolt works; without it you leave ADS, work the bolt, and re-scope. Either way, the shot after the rechamber is a new shot — re-aim it from zero, don't fire from where the recoil left you.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Pick your spot before the fight

    A sniper rifle is a planted weapon. Find a ridge, a rooftop, or a rock with cover at your back and an escape route behind it — then stop moving. The player who picks the range usually wins the duel; a sniper picks it before the enemy even spawns.

  2. Aim at the head, harvest the one-shot

    Headshots deal ×2.1 damage: one kills inside 246.4 m, versus two body shots. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the top of the head and let the sway drift through it. One clean headshot is the whole rifle; everything else is a backup plan.

  3. One shot, rechamber, re-acquire

    Every shot costs you 1.565 s before the next one is ready. Use that time: with the Straight-Pull Bolt you stay scoped and watch the target while the bolt works; without it you leave ADS, work the bolt, and re-scope. Never fire the instant the bolt closes — re-aim first, or the second shot is a gift to the enemy.

  4. Hold your breath — and know when not to

    With a 6x scope or higher, hold Shift once the sight is up: it almost completely stops the scope sway. But the breath runs out, and the Target Focus implant is what extends it. At close range with a 1x–4x scope there is no sway at all — don't waste the breath you don't need.

  5. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 3.1 s; an empty rifle takes 4.7 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after two kills, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last round as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  6. Move after every kill — or every miss

    A sniper rifle's tracer is thick and long, and your minimap dot reaches 100 m. Every shot you fire is a flare that says 'here I am'. After a kill — or a miss — relocate: ten metres sideways is enough to make the counter-sniper's return shot miss. The rifle that stays in one place is the rifle that dies in one place.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the SR-7 pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It loses every close fight

A bolt-action fires once per 1.565 s. Inside 20 m a carbine kills you in 0.5 s while you are still working the bolt. Your hipfire cone is 5.0° — wider than a man's chest at 8 m.

Play around it: Play around it: don't take close fights. Pick a spot with 50 m of open ground in front of it, and keep your sidearm ready for the enemy who closes the gap anyway.

2 · A miss costs you the fight

One missed headshot means 1.565 s of rechamber before you can try again — and the enemy now knows exactly where you are. Two misses and you have spent 2.8 s achieving nothing.

Play around it: Play around it: don't take the shot until it is a shot. Wait for the target to stop, hold your breath, and fire once — a patient miss is still a miss, but a rushed one is a death sentence.

3 · Scope sway moves your crosshair

With a 6x scope or higher, the crosshair drifts on its own — and higher magnification magnifies the sway. At 12x the sway can walk your shot off a head at 200 m.

Play around it: Play around it: hold your breath (Shift) for the shot, and buy the Target Focus implant when you can — it makes the breath last longer. Or drop to a lower magnification: the sway shrinks with the zoom.

4 · Reloads are slow

3.1 s with rounds left, 4.7 s on empty — an age when a second enemy rounds the corner, and you only have 8 rounds to begin with.

Play around it: Play around it: reload early and only in cover (habit 5), and count your rounds — two kills, then reload, every time.

5 · You are loud on the map

Every shot puts you on enemy minimaps from 100 m away, and your tracer is thick and long. A good counter-sniper reads your first shot and answers it.

Play around it: Play around it: move after every kill (habit 6), and take the Suppressor when the fight is worth the damage cost — it erases the minimap dot entirely.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The One-Shot Test 3 minutes · aim

    At 100 m, kill dummies with single headshots — one round, one kill. If you miss, work the bolt and try again, but count the misses: five clean one-shots in a row is the goal. This is the whole rifle in one drill.

  • Breath Control 3 minutes · sway

    At 150 m with your 6x scope, hold your breath (Shift) and kill dummies only while the breath lasts. When the sway returns, stop firing and wait for it to recover. Learn exactly how long your breath lasts — and how long it takes to come back.

  • Rechamber Rhythm 4 minutes · timing

    Kill dummies at 100 m with the trainer rhythm from section 04: shot, rechamber, re-acquire, shot. If you fire the instant the bolt closes, you failed the drill — the second shot must start from a re-aimed crosshair, not from where the recoil left you.

  • The Relocation Drill 3 minutes · movement

    Kill one dummy, then move ten metres sideways to a new position before the next shot. Repeat five times. This is habit 6 made automatic: never fire twice from the same spot.

  • Reload Discipline 2 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly three rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a slow reload, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
