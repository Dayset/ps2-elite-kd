01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did The spray

Sprinting, trigger held, sight raised mid-hold — three things in the wrong order. Read it left to right.

What happened: you started firing from a sprint, from the hip — cone already at 7.0°, fully bloomed — and every hipfire shot widened it further (+0.12° per shot). Pressing ADS mid-hold only changed the picture; the bullets kept flying out of the wide cone you had already built. Two timers stack the deck further: the gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

The fix Three beats, in order

Stop. Aim. Fire. One beat at a time — never two at once.

The rhythm: 1 · Feet — stop, or drop into a strafe. 2 · Sight — press ADS and let it come up; a heartbeat behind cover costs nothing. 3 · Trigger — now fire, starting from the aimed cone of 0.03°. Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

✓ The exception: under ~11 m, skip the sight entirely

Inside about 11 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving. At arm's length your hipfire ring is only about half a metre wide (section 05), so the shots land, and staying mobile keeps you alive. Scoping in costs time and halves your speed — here, that gets you killed. No ADS needed, and stopping is optional: strafe, hipfire, keep moving.

The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.12° per shot).

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 167 damage up close, fading to 143 at long range — and that 143 is the highest minimum damage of any 167-damage assault rifle in the game. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you just two numbers worth memorising:

RangeDamageHeadChestLegsChest vs shielded Heavy
Close · 0–10 m1673679
Mid–far · 11–75 m+167→14347811

Only two tiers — there is no third band where the Corvus falls off a cliff. Damage per second: 1531 up close, 1311 at long range. Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 3 headshots ≈ 0.22 s, 6 chest shots ≈ 0.55 s up close, 7 chest shots ≈ 0.65 s at any longer range. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun, and why the Corvus's whole identity is "the 167-damage rifle that never gets weak".

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 10 m, then a gentle slide to 143 at 75 m — and it never goes lower. The teal line shows High Velocity Ammunition, which stretches the slide out to 95 m.

Rule of thumbInside 10 m you are at maximum power. Past 10 m every kill takes exactly one extra bullet — 7 instead of 6 — and then it stays that way forever. No range in the game makes this gun weak; that's the whole reason to spend 650 certs on it.

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The Corvus has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun. (Some rifles offer a semi-auto mode; the Corvus doesn't — and you don't need it.)

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 kills up close versus 6 to the chest, 4 versus 7 at range. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  2. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  3. Never reload on empty — the 2.02-second rule

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.02 s; an empty gun takes 2.47 s — nearly half a second longer, an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after a kill or ~20 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine. Your 30-round mag means you reload more often than a 40-round T1 — make that count.

  4. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back. Remember your ADS cone while strafing is 0.30°, not 0.03° — strafe between bursts, plant your feet during them.

  5. Pick fights at 15–50 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and velocity all shine at mid-to-long range — and your 143 minimum damage means the far end of that band is still yours. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

  6. The magazine is a budget — and the rifle is your second job

    Thirty rounds is two kills if every shot lands, one kill plus a few misses in real life. Reload after every fight, behind cover, before you push on. And remember what the rifle is for: as a Medic you win fights so you can revive the squad — heal and revive between engagements, and never stand in the open trading shots while a teammate bleeds out next to you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Corvus pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It loses the pure DPS race up close

550 RPM is slow for an assault rifle: 1,531 damage per second at best. If you and an enemy trade chest shots face-to-face with a faster-firing rifle, their bullets add up faster than yours.

Play around it: don't trade fairly. Aim for the neck, and use corners — your per-bullet damage and accuracy win fights that start on your terms, and at range the race flips in your favour.

2 · A 30-round magazine

Thirty rounds is a small magazine, and at 7 shots a kill, two enemies plus a few misses empties it. Caught reloading in the open, you are defenceless for 2.02 s (2.47 s on empty).

Play around it: reload early behind cover (habit 3) and make each burst count — five rounds that hit beat fifteen that don't. There is no Extended Magazine for this gun, so the habit is the upgrade.

3 · Average hipfire — don't brawl with it

2.0° standing from the hip is workable at arm's length and poor beyond it — no better than a free starter rifle's. The Corvus is a mid-range gun that tolerates close quarters; it is not a close-quarters gun.

Play around it: hipfire only inside ~11 m, and keep your distance the rest of the time.

4 · The recoil climbs straight up — and the first shot kicks 1.75×

Vertical recoil is 0.3° per shot with a 1.75× first-shot multiplier (0.525° on shot one), and the angle is 0° — straight up, no sideways drift at all. In a long burst your aim walks straight off the top of the target. (Recoil recovers at 18°/s once you stop firing, so the pause between bursts does double duty.)

Play around it: pull your mouse straight down while firing — no sideways correction needed. The Forward Grip trims the small sideways wobble (0.15°) by 25%.

5 · Aiming makes you slow — and the gun costs 650 certs

Like all rifles, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5×), and the gun takes 650 ms to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your Medi-Tool out. And unlike a starter rifle, the Corvus is a purchase: 650 certs is a lot for a beginner to spend on one gun.

Play around it: hipfire inside ~11 m instead of scoping, swap weapons behind cover, never in the open — and be honest before you buy: if you mostly fight indoors, your free starter rifle is the better gun. Buy the Corvus for the 15–50 m game, not for everything.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint (0° angle, no sideways drift). Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 40–60 m — the Corvus's home — kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round, at the range where most rifles start missing.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending. Remember: this only works inside ~11 m.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 2.02-second reload (2.47 s on empty), in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.

Sources: Corvus VA55 — PlanetSide 2 Wiki. Weapon render via the Census API; spread rings and sight views are scale diagrams drawn from the gun's published cone-of-fire and magnification values. PlanetSide 2 is a Daybreak Game Company title; this is a fan-made tutorial, not affiliated with Daybreak.
