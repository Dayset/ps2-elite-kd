01 First, stop running

You arrive on a fight, sprinting, because that's how you got there. You see him first, you aim down sights while still moving, you hold the trigger — and your pellets go everywhere except into him. The kill feed says he killed you. This section is why.

What just happened

When you sprint, your aim cone is at its widest — and it stays wide until you stop moving and wait. Firing from a sprint doesn't just make your shots inaccurate: your whole pellet pattern is centred on a point that can be anywhere inside that wide cone. Six pellets, 4° of spread, all of them faithfully centred on the wrong place.

Aiming down sights while still moving doesn't fix it. The sight doesn't shrink the pellet spread — it is 4° hip or ADS — and it doesn't shrink the cone you fired from, either. All it does is steady the pattern's centre, and the centre was never the problem: where it was pointing was.

The rule

Stop. Aim. Then shoot. Release the sprint key, let your feet stop, give the cone a beat to close, then pull the trigger. On this gun that beat is short — but it is not optional, and it is not zero.

One difference from a rifle: there is no bloom on a shotgun. The cone never grows as you fire, because the cone is the pellet spread — constant on every pull, the same hip and ADS. What grows as you fire is the sideways walk: 0.95° of horizontal recoil per pull, dragging your aim point off the chest. Short bursts, re-aim, short bursts. That is the whole discipline, and section 04 is where you practice it.

The exception: inside 5 metres, don't aim down sights at all — hipfire and keep moving. The pattern is 0.35 m wide at 5 m; it fits a torso without a sight. ADS only slows you down at that range.

02 What the pellets do

Every pull throws 6 pellets, each worth 112 damage up close and 50 at range. That is 672 damage a pull at point blank and 300 at 20 m — so the kill is 2 pulls, then 3, then 4, and the whole game is knowing which side of each line you are standing on.

Damage is per pellet. A "pull" is one trigger pull — the whole pattern. Shots to kill counts pulls, never single pellets. Headshots multiply each pellet by 1.5×, but on a shotgun that rarely changes the count: aim centre of mass and let the pattern do the work. The table assumes every pellet lands; section 03 is about how many actually do.

Ammo: Flechette and Slug change the math

Flechette Ammunition (100 certs) triples the damage ranges — 5–20 m becomes 15–60 m — and drops you to 5 pellets a pull. Up close it costs you the 1-pull headshot (5 × 112 × 1.5 = 840, not enough), but the chest kill is still 2 pulls. The payoff is range: the 2-pull chest kill stretches to ~24 m, 3 pulls to ~48 m. At 1–5 m its TTK is the same as standard pellets (Dorf EP01) — so you lose nothing up close and gain everything past 20 m.

Slug Ammunition (100 certs) replaces the pattern with one slug: 450 damage up close falling to 300 at range. That is 3 pulls to kill on the chest (4 at range) and 2 to the head. It turns the gun into a slow semi-auto rifle that punishes every miss — take it only if you genuinely ADS at 10–30 m, and accept that a missed slug is a wasted pull.

Reload — shells load one at a time

Short reload 2.6 s, on empty 3.8 s — the difference is the chamber time after the last shell. Shells load one at a time, and you can interrupt the reload and fire with whatever is already loaded. So reload in short bursts between fights, and never let it run dry: the empty reload is the one that gets you killed.

03 Where it works

A shotgun's range is not set by its damage. It is set by the size of the pattern against the size of a torso.

0–5 m — hipfire, don't aim

2 pulls to kill (1 to the head). The pattern is 0.35 m wide at 5 m — inside a torso. Hipfire, keep strafing, fire 2-pull bursts. ADS here only slows you down: the spread is the same either way, and the sight doesn't make the pellets any tighter.

5–20 m — ADS, 2–3 pull bursts

2 pulls to ~12 m, 3 to ~19 m. This is the gun's real range. ADS to steady the pattern's centre on the chest, fire a short burst, re-aim, fire again. With a Smart-Choke the pattern is 0.34 m wide at 8 m — half the hipfire size — which is why the choke earns its 100 certs here.

Beyond 20 m — don't take the fight

4 pulls, if every pellet lands — and at 20 m the pattern is 1.40 m wide, so most pellets miss. A carbine kills you in the time it takes you to land four full patterns. Close the gap, or switch weapons. Flechette stretches the 2-pull kill to ~24 m; past that, you are decorating a wall.

That is the whole range game, and it is why the damage chart in section 02 is only half the story: the chart assumes every pellet lands. The pattern decides how many do, and the pattern grows with distance — 4° of spread is 0.35 m wide at 5 m and 1.40 m at 20 m. Section 05 draws it to scale so you can see it.

04 Stances, bursts and the trigger

Hold the trigger and watch what happens to the pattern. Bloom is 0, so the cone never grows — what grows is the sideways walk: each pull drags the aim point by the gun's 0.95° of horizontal recoil, capped at 5° of tolerance.

There is no bloom on this gun — no ADAD rhythm to manage, no cone that grows as you fire. The cone is the pellet spread: constant on every pull, the same hip and ADS. What changes with stance is where the pattern is centred, and what changes as you fire is where the next pull's centre lands. The table is the whole stance story:

StancePellet spreadWhat changes
Standing still, hipfire4.0°Pattern centre: 0.15° CoF — the pattern is the cone; your aim point sits in the middle of it.
Standing still, ADS4.0°Same spread — the sight steadies the pattern's centre, it doesn't shrink it. Smart-Choke cuts it to 2.4°.
Moving / strafing, hipfire4.0°Same spread; the pattern centre wanders with your movement — the cone is still 4.0°, but it's centred where your feet put it.
Moving, ADS4.0°Same spread; ADS move speed 0.75× — you strafe slower for a steadier centre.
SprintingmaxedNever start firing from a sprint — the pattern centre can be anywhere inside the sprint cone. Stop first.

The spread never changes. ADS doesn't shrink it, moving doesn't grow it, crouching doesn't touch it. Only the Smart-Choke changes it (section 06) — and only by trading hipfire width for ADS tightness.

The mag dump — the mistake

Hold the trigger and all 6 shells leave in 1.16 s. The first two pulls are on target; the rest walk sideways off the chest at 0.95° a pull. Dorf's finding (EP03): spraying with MPL + Flechette at 20 m gives a 46% chance to NOT kill before the reload. The magazine is six shells — a mag dump is one kill attempt and an empty gun.

Bursts of 2–3 — the default

Hold for 2–3 pulls, release, re-aim, fire again. Same gun, same enemy: Dorf's tested combo — 3-pull bursts, ADS, Smart-Choke + Flechette + Extended Magazine — gives a 99% one-mag kill. The bursts are the whole difference.

Single pulls — for the second enemy

One pull, re-aim. For a far target, a second enemy, or the last shell in the mag. A single pull up close is 672 damage — most of a health bar — so a lone pull followed by a re-aim is a legitimate way to fight.

Try it: the 2–3 pull burst

A target 8 m away, firing from the hip. Hold FIRE and watch the pattern walk sideways — that is the 0.95° horizontal recoil dragging your aim off the chest. Release, re-aim, fire again. Aim for bursts of 2–3 pulls; 4 is tolerable; 5+ means you are holding the trigger through the whole magazine.

08 Six habits that win fights

  1. Fire in bursts of 2–3 pulls, then re-aim. The walk is the enemy, not the cone. A 4-pull burst is tolerable; 5+ is the whole magazine on one enemy.
  2. Reload after every fight, and reload in cover. Six shells is one kill and a miss. The reload is 2.6 s — longer than most fights.
  3. Hipfire inside 5 m, ADS past it. The spread is the same either way — the sight is for the pattern's centre, and past 5 m the centre needs to be on the chest, not near it.
  4. The tube reloads one shell at a time — interrupt it. If a fight starts mid-reload, fire what you have. Three shells in the tube beats six in the reload animation.
  5. Don't chase past 20 m. Close the gap or swap weapons. Spraying at 25 m is how you die with a full magazine.
  6. Count your pulls out loud in VR until bursts are automatic. Two pulls, re-aim, two pulls. Do it until you don't have to think about it.

09 Where it loses

Know the losing matchups before you walk into them. A shotgun's weaknesses are all range and reload — which means they are all decisions, not stats.

  1. Reload time. 2.6 s short, 3.8 s on empty. Anyone who hears you reload owns the next two seconds.
  2. Range. Past 20 m you need 4 pulls with every pellet landing, and the pattern is 1.40 m wide. A carbine kills you first.
  3. Magazine. 6 shells, 1.16 s of holding the trigger. Two enemies is the whole magazine, and then some.
  4. Horizontal recoil. 0.95° per pull, tolerance 5° — the worst sideways walk of any shotgun class. That is why the laser doesn't fix it (section 06).
  5. ADS move speed. 0.75× while aiming. You are a slower target the moment you ADS — which is why you hipfire inside 5 m.

10 VR drills

Ten minutes in the VR training room, before you take this gun into a real fight. Do them in order — each one builds on the last.

  1. Wall Test. 8 m, hipfire, one pull at a time: count the pellets inside the torso outline. Expect 5–6 of 6. If fewer, your aim point is off, not your spread.
  2. Burst Ladder. 8 m: hold 2 pulls, re-aim, hold 3, re-aim, hold 4. Grade each burst in the simulator (section 04). Stop when 2–3 pulls is automatic.
  3. Circle Strafe. 8 m, strafe around a target while firing 2-pull bursts. Keep the pattern on the chest while moving — the ADS move penalty is 0.75×, so stay hipfire.
  4. Reload Discipline. Empty the mag, reload, and interrupt the reload after 3 shells to fire. Repeat until interrupting is reflex, not a decision.
  5. Range Walk. Start at 5 m, walk back one metre per kill: 2 pulls to ~12 m, 3 to ~19 m. Learn where the 2-pull kill dies by feel, not by the chart.

Pocket summary: Bursts of 2–3 pulls. Hipfire inside 5 m, ADS past it. Reload after every fight. Don't chase past 20 m.
