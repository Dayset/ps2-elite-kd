01

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 143 damage up close, fading to 112 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

RangeDamageHeadChestLegsChest vs shielded Heavy
Close · 0–10 m14347811
Mid · 10–50 m143→12548912
Far · 50 m+125→112591013

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.24 s, 7 chest shots ≈ 0.48 s up close. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun. The exact edges: 7 chest shots hold to 10.3 m, 8 shots to 50.6 m. Maximum damage per second: 1,788. Magazine: 40 rounds, 240 in reserve.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 10 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 80 m. The gold line shows Soft Point Ammunition, which stretches full power to 15 m.

Rule of thumbInside 10 m you are at maximum power. Past 50 m every kill takes two extra bullets — close the distance or burst carefully instead of spraying.

0–10 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The Unity's hipfire is honest for an assault rifle (2.0° standing, 2.5° moving), so keep it truly close and let the 40-round magazine do the work. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track.

10–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage, mild recoil, fast handling. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull straight down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 125 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (0° angle — no sideways drift in the climb itself), so pull your mouse gently straight down — but expect a little sideways wander from the horizontal recoil between bursts. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 112 damage you need 9 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

03

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The Unity has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

Stances — the cone you start with, before the first shot

StanceStarting coneWhat it means for you
Sprinting7.0°Maxed out — the widest the cone can be. Never start firing from a sprint.
Hipfire, moving2.5°Walking or strafing from the hip. Fine inside ~10 m, leaks rounds past the target beyond it.
Hipfire, standing2.0°Your point-blank default. The 0.35 m ring at 10 m in section 04 is this stance drawn out.
Hipfire, crouched1.5°The tightest hipfire you have (2.0° if you crouch-walk). Crouch when holding a doorway from the hip.
Aimed (ADS), feet planted0.10°Standing or crouched makes no difference here — twenty times tighter than hipfire. This is why you plant your feet before you fire.
Aimed (ADS), strafing0.30°Still tight, but three times wider than planted (0.20° if you crouch-walk) — and you move at half speed (0.5×). Strafe between bursts to stay hard to hit; plant your feet during each burst.

Firing modes — the cone you grow, shot by shot

ModeHow your finger does itUse it at
The mag dumpHold the trigger and don't let goPoint blank only, or suppressing a doorway. Bloom maxes out fast — past ~8 rounds you're spraying, not aiming, even with 40 rounds in the magazine.

Why 5? The first 4 aimed shots (5 from the hip) fire before bloom pushes the cone up to the moving floor — that's the free window in every burst, and the reason 5 rounds is the ideal burst length. The cone still grows by 0.05° with every aimed shot, so the fifth shot is where the cost starts — but it still lands close, and 5 rounds is the burst that ends most close fights in the fewest clean hits.

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.05° per shot aimed, 0.10° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short bursts also leave you rounds for the second enemy — remember, the magazine is 40, and with the Heavy Magazine it's 45.

RTA Reflex Sight — 1x

No zoom. The target at 35 m is small but the view is wide open — you see the crate, the tower, and the next enemy coming. Fast to aim, nothing blocks your edges. Best inside ~40 m.

MH2 Reflex Sight — 2x

Same target, same 35 m — but he looks twice as tall, so placing shots on his chest and head gets much easier. The price: your view narrows to the round window, the crate and tower are gone, and close targets feel fast and huge. Best at 25–50 m.

That's the whole 30-cert decision from section 05 in one picture: 1x for awareness, 2x for precision. Most veterans carry both and swap per base — indoors 1x, open ground 2x.

05

Attachments: what to buy, what to skip

Every slot, judged
for a new player

The gun itself can't be bought with certs — it's the reward for completing the Master Assault Rifle directive (auraxium medals on five assault rifles). Every attachment on it costs certs too. Your most important buys are cheap, though: both reflex sights are 30 certs each. Buy in the order shown and stop whenever the gun feels right.

Two things already come built in: the forward-grip effect — your sideways recoil is already 25% tighter than the T1 Cycler's (0.169° per shot instead of 0.225°) — and the Cure Launcher underbarrel. Both stack with what you buy: a second Forward Grip tightens the sideways recoil again.

Underbarrel — yours already, and it's a healer

Cure Launcher

Already yours0 certs · default

Fires a healing grenade that heals affected allies over time and cleanses most status effects on impact. It comes fitted by default. Cost: the gun takes 0.25 s longer to draw (1.0 s fitted, vs 0.75 s without it).

Good100 certs

Cuts side-to-side recoil by 25%, keeping your bursts on a single target — and it stacks with the grip already built into the gun (0.169° per shot becomes about 0.127°). Tiny cost: weapon swap is 0.15 s slower.

Ammo — three options, all honest

Soft Point Ammunition

First buy100 certs

Full 143 damage now reaches to 15 m instead of 10 m (the gold line on the chart above). Cost: bullets fly 10% slower (551 → 496 m/s).

Good: nearly always — most duels happen at 10–20 m, exactly where this extends your 7-shot / 4-headshot kill.

Bad: long-range duels against runners, where the slower bullet needs more lead. A small price.

High Velocity Ammunition

Situational100 certs

Bullets fly 10% faster (551 → 606 m/s) and minimum damage reaches to 100 m instead of 80 m. Cost: full damage ends at 8 m instead of 10 m.

Good: long lanes and leading moving targets at 40 m+, where the faster bullet and later falloff both help.

Bad: close fights — you give up 2 m of full damage for speed you don't need at arm's length.

Heavy Magazine

Good100 certs

Adds 5 rounds to the magazine (40 → 45) — the Unity is the only TR assault rifle that can take one. Cost: hipfire spread grows 20%.

Good: holding a point or suppressing a lane, where 40 rounds never feels like enough — and it papers over the 2.76 s reload by making reloads rarer.

Bad: hipfire duels — the wider hip cone costs you exactly where the extra rounds were meant to help.

07

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Use cover like an overshield

    A Medic or Engineer has no overshield — never stand in the open to trade shots. Peek from a doorway, fire your burst, duck back. (Heavy Assault with ASP: your shield (F key) is the cover you carry — put it up before the corner, not during the duel.) A shielded Heavy needs 11 chest shots to drop up close instead of 7 — that gap wins duels, so don't hand it to the enemy for free.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.76 s; an empty gun takes 3.55 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after one kill or ~30 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving Medic is dramatically harder to headshot back, even at half speed while scoped.

  6. Pick fights at 10–40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and bullet velocity all shine at mid-range — longer than a close-quarters rifle's comfort zone, shorter than a marksman's. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

08

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Unity pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · Damage falls off fast

143 damage is only yours to 10 m. Past 50 m you need 9 chest shots to kill — two more than up close — while your bullets also take longer to arrive (551 m/s).

Play around it: close the distance behind cover instead of trading at range (habit 6), and take the Soft Point Ammo — full damage to 15 m buys back your 7-shot kill across most duels.

2 · No suppressor — you are always on the minimap

Unlike the T1 Cycler, the Unity cannot mount a suppressor at all. Every shot marks your position for every enemy in the area.

Play around it: never camp one window. Fire your burst, move to the next piece of cover, fire again. The minimap dot only matters if you're still standing where it says you are.

3 · The reload is slow for an assault rifle

2.76 s with rounds left, 3.55 s on empty. Caught reloading in the open, you are defenceless for a long time.

Play around it: reload early and only in cover (habit 4), and consider the Heavy Magazine — 45 rounds means reloads come less often in the first place.

4 · The recoil walks sideways

Vertical recoil is 0.27° per shot with a 2.5× first-shot kick, and it climbs straight up (0° angle) — but horizontal recoil is 0.169° per shot with a 0.375° tolerance, so bursts wander left and right unpredictably between the vertical steps. In a long burst your aim climbs off the head and drifts off the chest.

Play around it: pull your mouse straight down while firing, and expect a little sideways wander — don't chase it, just re-centre between bursts. The Forward Grip stacks with the built-in grip and trims the sideways walk further; short bursts (habit 3) keep it small.

5 · Aiming makes you slow, and the draw is slow

You move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5×), and the gun takes 0.75 s to draw — slower than the T1 Cycler's 0.65 s, and the fitted Cure Launcher adds another 0.25 s on top of that. A fight that surprises you with your tool out starts badly.

Play around it: hipfire inside 10 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open. As a Medic, keep the Unity out when you expect contact — the tool can wait.

09

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up with a little sideways wander — that's your gun's fingerprint. Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 30 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Your 2.76 s reload is the one that punishes you most on this gun — train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic. In real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
