First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.05° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, a quarter-second of patience is cheaper than a magazine of misses.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.30°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 125 damage up close, fading to 84 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.21 s, 8 chest shots ≈ 0.50 s up close. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 6 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 42 m. The gold line shows Soft Point Ammunition, which stretches full power to 11 m. Past 18 m the kill costs 10 chest shots or more.

Rule of thumbInside 6 m you are at maximum power. Past 17 m every kill takes one or two extra bullets — close the distance or burst carefully instead of spraying.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and drops your speed to 0.75× — at arm's length that gets you killed. SMGs have the tightest hipfire of any weapon class. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track.

8–20 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 125 damage to 6 m, still 111 at 18 m, and a 50-round magazine that forgives a miss. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in 0.21 s. Strafe while scoped; at 0.75× you move faster aimed than a scoped Heavy, and a moving target still beats a still one.

20–42 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down and to the left

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding from ~111 toward 84 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil drifts up and to the right (20° angle), so pull your mouse gently down and to the left. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

42 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 84 damage you need 12 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The Blitz GD-10 has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

Hold – release – hold – release: the burst rhythm

Every shot widens your cone of fire ("bloom"). Releasing the trigger for a heartbeat resets it. Same 18 rounds, two very different outcomes:

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.05° per shot aimed, 0.05° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short bursts also leave you rounds for the second and third enemy — and with a 50-round magazine, you can afford to stay in the fight longer.

TekLyte Reflex — 1x

No zoom. The target at 25 m is small but the view is wide open — you see the crate, the tower, and the next enemy coming. Fast to aim, nothing blocks your edges. Best inside ~25 m.

GD RefleXR — 2x

Same target, same 25 m — but he looks twice as tall, so placing shots on his chest and head gets much easier. The price: your view narrows to the round window, the crate and tower are gone, and close targets feel fast and huge. Best at 15–40 m.

That's the whole 30-cert decision from section 06 in one picture: 1x for awareness, 2x for precision. Most veterans carry both and swap per base — indoors 1x, open ground 2x.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 8 to the chest, in 0.21 s instead of 0.50 s. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  2. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  3. Use the magazine — pre-fire the corner

    Fifty rounds is a resource, not a decoration. You can afford a short pre-fire burst into a doorway as you round it, and you can keep firing through a second enemy without reloading. Spend the ammo; that's what it's for. Just don't spend it all in one hold — bursts still win.

  4. Reload early — the penalty is real, the habit is not

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.5 s; an empty gun takes 3.5 s — a full second more, so a dry magazine really can doom you. Fifty rounds means you reload less often than anyone else in the class — but when you do reload, do it behind cover, because it takes a while.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: at 0.75× aimed speed you're faster than almost anyone scoped in against you, and a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Hipfire is a weapon, not a fallback

    Your standing hip cone is 1.0° — the tightest hipfire of any weapon class in the game — and the 0.14 m ring at 8 m fits inside his chest. Inside ~8 m, don't ADS at all: keep strafing and let the hipfire do the work. Past ~10 m the rings start leaking past him, and that's the exact moment to switch to the sight.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Blitz GD-10 pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · 125 damage — 8 chest shots up close

Eight chest shots to kill in 0.50 s — the second-slowest close-range time to kill of the five SMGs here (only the Hailstorm is slower). Every bullet matters less than on the Cyclone's 167.

Play around it: Don't trade chest shots. Headshots close the gap entirely — 4 to kill up close in 0.21 s — and your 845 RPM forgives a miss (71 ms) better than any slower gun forgives one.

2 · The slowest reload of the five

Reloading with rounds left takes 2.5 s; an empty gun takes 3.5 s — the longest reloads of any SMG on this page. A reload at the wrong moment is a death sentence.

Play around it: Reload early and only in cover (habit 4). Fifty rounds means you reload less often — make each one count by choosing the moment.

3 · Recoil drifts up and to the right

The recoil climbs at a 20° angle — up and to the right — so a held burst walks off the target's left shoulder if you don't correct it.

Play around it: Pull down and to the left as you fire, and let the Forward Grip trim the sideways part by 25%. Short bursts never give the drift time to build.

4 · No Extended Magazine to buy

Fifty rounds is already the extended magazine — there is no +10-round option on this gun, so the rail slot can't buy you more ammo.

Play around it: Spend the 100 certs on the Laser Sight instead: it tightens the hipfire this gun is built for, and you already have the ammo.

5 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 42 m you're at 84 damage: 12 chest shots, 18 against a shielded Heavy. Soft Point Ammunition stretches your full damage to 11 m, but nothing lifts the 84-damage floor.

Play around it: Tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're an SMG — the front line, the doorway, the stairwell is your address.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up and to the right — that's your gun's fingerprint (20° angle). Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 20–30 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 40 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
