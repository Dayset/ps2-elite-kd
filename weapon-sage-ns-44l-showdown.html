First lesson: stop, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you start firing while you're still running. Your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened. And on this gun the lesson is sharper than on any other, because there is no sight to save you.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be.

Then you kept firing while moving — and the cone never shrank. This gun has no aim-down-sights mode at all, so there is no sight picture to rescue you: every shot is a hipfire shot, and the only thing that tightens the cone is stopping. Firing on the move from a sprint cone means your bullets fly out of the widest cone the gun can ever have.

Your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint. Shots fired through all of it are pure confetti — and at 500 ms between shots, every wasted round is half a second of a fight you are losing.

The Showdown makes this worse in one specific way: there is no ADS to press, no sight picture to snap into, no rescue at all. Every shot is a hipfire shot, and a sprint cone is 7.0° — the widest the cone can ever be. A panicked spray from a sprint is three misses, and three-quarters of your cylinder is gone.

✓ The fix — two beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint. Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint. Your standing hipfire cone is 1.5°, and with zero bloom it never grows from there.

2 · Trigger. Now fire — one deliberate tap at a time. There is no sight to raise, so the whole skill is the pause: stop, then shoot. Starting from the standing cone, your shots land where the dot points.

Stop → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

Showdown note: your hip cone is 1.5° standing and moving alike, and your bloom is 0 — the cone never grows, however long you hold the trigger. The fix is pure timing: stop, then tap. The gun does the rest.

The one exception — there isn't oneOn every other pistol, the exception is "inside 8 m, hipfire and keep moving." On the Showdown, hipfire is the gun. The rule is simpler and stricter: stop moving, then shoot. A strafe costs you nothing in cone size (1.5° either way), but a sprint costs you everything.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 450 damage up close — and unlike every other pistol on this page, it barely fades: 275 at 125 m, the longest falloff of any sidearm. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising — and a range table that looks nothing like a pistol's.

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 2 headshots ≈ 0.50 s, 3 chest shots ≈ 1.00 s — at 20 m, at 60 m, at 100 m. Even at 125 m the kill is still 4 chest shots in 1.50 s. The kill time is slow — the slowest on this page, because the trigger only cycles every 500 ms — but it does not get slower with range. That is the whole point of the gun.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Rule of thumbYou are a three-tap gun out to 100 m: 3 chest shots, 2 headshots, 4 against a shielded Heavy. The only number that changes with range is how much you have to lead the target — at 550 m/s, barely anything.

How you stand and

The Showdown is semi-automatic only — one click, one round, no fire-mode switch — and it has no aim-down-sights mode at all. Every shot is a hipfire shot. Read it this way: the 0.21 m ring fits inside the chest at 8 m — most rounds hit a target this size, which is why hipfire works point-blank on this gun. Tap – pause – tap: the cadence

A semi-automatic fires once per click, and no faster than its refire — one shot every 500 ms on this gun. Clicking faster does nothing; clicking slower hands the enemy time. Same 6 shots, two very different outcomes:

The target pattern is one even tap every 500 ms. Not faster — the gun cannot fire faster, and clicks inside the refire window do nothing. Not slower — every extra 100 ms of hesitation is a tenth of a second added to your kill time, and at 450 damage a round, you cannot afford to give it away. The trainer in section 04 grades exactly this: even taps at the refire cadence.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's upward climb carry the next shot into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  2. Tap at the cadence, not faster

    This gun fires once per click and no faster than its 500 ms refire. Clicks inside the refire window do nothing — they are wasted motion that shakes your aim. Even taps at the cadence are the whole skill; the simulator in section 04 grades exactly this.

  3. Count your four

    Four rounds is one kill and one spare round — and nothing else. Know how many you've fired. After three taps, you have one round left: make it count, or reload behind cover before the next target.

  4. Reload early — the penalty is real, the habit is not

    Reloading takes 3.00 s — the longest reload of any pistol on this page. A dry cylinder at the wrong moment is a death sentence. Reload early and only in cover, because three seconds is a long time to stand in a doorway.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: the cone is 1.5° whether you stand or move, so the strafe is free — and a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Trust the cone, not the ring

    At 40 m your spread ring is 1.05 m wide — wider than the man. That does not mean you miss: the ring is where rounds can land, and the target fills the middle of it. Aim at the centre of the chest, tap once, and let the cone do its work. Panic-aiming at the edges is how you miss.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the NS-44L Showdown pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · 450 damage — but only 4 rounds, and 500 ms apart

Three taps kill anyone out to 100 m. But the cylinder holds four, and the trigger only cycles every half second: the kill takes a full second, the slowest on this page.

Play around it: Don't trade taps. Headshots close the gap entirely — 2 to kill in 0.50 s — and never start a fight you haven't already half-won. This gun finishes fights; it does not start them.

2 · The longest reload on this page

Reloading takes 3.00 s. A reload at the wrong moment is a death sentence.

Play around it: Reload early and only in cover (habit 4). Four rounds means you reload constantly — make each one count by choosing the moment.

3 · No ADS — the long shot is a hipfire shot

At 100 m you are aiming a 1.5° hipfire cone at a target 0.5 m wide. There is no magnification, no precise sight picture, nothing to steady the shot except your own timing.

Play around it: Slow down. One tap, breathe, one tap. Your bloom is 0, so the cone never punishes patience — the only thing that punishes you is rushing.

4 · Recoil kicks up and sideways, hard

Vertical 3.0° from the hip at a 15° angle, with slow recovery (14/s). The second tap of a fast double lands high and wide if you don't pull down.

Play around it: Pull down and slightly left after every shot. At 500 ms between taps you have time to re-aim properly — use all of it.

5 · A sprint is a death sentence

Sprinting maxes the cone at 7.0°, and there is no ADS to recover into. A sprinting tap is a wasted round, and you only have four.

Play around it: Stop before you shoot. Every time. The strafe is free; the sprint is not.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and fire four single taps without touching the mouse. Watch the holes climb up and to the side — that's your gun's fingerprint (3.0° a shot at 15°). Repeat, pulling down-left after every tap, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Cadence Ladder 4 minutes · tap control

    At 40–60 m, kill dummies with even taps at the 500 ms cadence — use the simulator in section 04 first, then do it live. If a tap goes wide, slow down. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • The Patient Shot 3 minutes · long range

    At 80–100 m, kill dummies with one tap every two seconds — far slower than the gun allows. Learn what the cone feels like when you give every tap its full respect. Then speed up to the real 500 ms cadence.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly three rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Remember: it takes 3.00 s, and you only have four rounds.
