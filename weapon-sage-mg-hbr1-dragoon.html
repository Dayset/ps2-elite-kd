MG-HBR1 Dragoon — three shots, any range

The Terran Republic's heavy battle rifle: 334 damage a shot, flat at every range, and the only automatic battle rifle in the class. Slow, loud, and honest about it.

Read this first — this gun is NOT a starter. The MG-HBR1 Dragoon costs 1,000 certs (or 699 Daybreak Cash). It is usable by the Infiltrator, Engineer, Heavy Assault and Combat Medic — every class except the Light Assault. No battle rifle is a starter: every faction's new player starts with a carbine, an assault rifle or an LMG. The Dragoon is a purchase you make later, because you want the flat damage.

The one-line verdict: a 334-damage automatic that kills in two headshots at any range and three chest shots — if you fire in short bursts and never hold the trigger. The flat damage is the whole gun: the kill never fades, so range stops being a damage question.

01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — holding the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still working. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

Four beats, left to right — the same mistake the cards above describe, drawn out.

The fix — three beats, in order

Same fight, done right. Each beat has a job, and the order is the whole lesson.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.2° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes 0.15 s. Trigger working through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and on this gun, with only 10 rounds and 300 ms between shots, two wasted rounds are a fifth of your magazine gone for nothing.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine. A clean first shot is worth more than a rushed spray, and no class ability covers for a wasted one.

3 · Trigger. Now fire — in a short burst, then release. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.00°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many bullets to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 334 damage up close — MG-HBR1 Dragoon damage. Headshots count ×2, leg shots count less (×0.9). Against a shielded Heavy Assault (1,450 health with the shield up) the chest count rises — last column. A range like "4 → 5" means the count changes inside the band, as the damage slides.

In real fights you will land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun: two clean headshots end it in 300 ms at any range, and no starter rifle does that.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

The gold line is flat — 334 damage at every range. That is the whole point of this gun: the kill never fades, so range stops being a damage question and becomes purely an aim question.

Rule of thumbThere is no falloff. 334 damage at 10 m is 334 damage at 200 m — three chest shots, two headshots, everywhere. Range stops being a damage question and becomes purely an aim question: the only thing a long shot costs you is the holdover.

04

Tap-fire discipline

How you stand and how you work the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (how you work the trigger). The MG-HBR1 Dragoon is a automatic battle rifle, so the whole gun is built around one idea: every shot is aimed, and the cone only grows when you stop aiming. On an automatic the trigger does the pacing for you — your job is to let go of it.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats attachments

The gun is fine — your habits decide the fights. Six things to do on every engagement, in order.

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 2 kill at any range versus 3 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the first-shot kick climb into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free. At 300 ms for two heads, no starter out-kills you at any range.

  2. Two shots, release, two shots

    Every shot widens your cone of fire by 0.1° aimed, from the very first shot. Holding the trigger means the third bullet already leaves a 0.2° cone. Hold for a heartbeat, release, re-aim. With only 10 rounds, you can't afford to decorate the wall.

  3. Count your ten rounds — reload early

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.0 s; an empty gun takes 3.1 s. That extra 1.1 s is an eternity standing defenceless. After one kill, duck behind cover and reload. Treat the last round as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  4. Pre-aim the doorway, then peek — don't strafe-duel

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. And you move at 0.5× while aiming, same as everyone else — so peek, fire your burst, and break line of sight instead of standing in the open.

  5. Pick fights at your range, not theirs

    Your damage, accuracy and bullet speed all shine at 10–100 m, and your two-headshot kill holds at any range while theirs fades. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you. Never chase a fight into arm's length if you can help it: that's where the 750 RPM guns live.

  6. Reload after every kill

    Ten rounds is three kills at perfect accuracy and one at real accuracy. When the magazine runs dry mid-fight you are defenceless for 2.0 s — longer than any duel lasts. Reload after every kill, reload behind cover, and never start a fight on a half-empty magazine.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them, play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the MG-HBR1 Dragoon pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · Only 10 rounds — three kills at best

Ten rounds is three kills if every round lands, and one in a real fight. A 6-shot hold can leave you dry mid-fight, and the Heavy Magazine only adds three.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 2) and early reloads (habit 3). Your 2.0 s short reload is fast — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

2 · A miss costs you 300 ms — and the kill

At 200 RPM there are 300 ms between your shots — the slowest follow-up of any automatic here. Every miss costs you a full 300 ms of kill time, and two misses in a duel hand it to the enemy. Your damage per second is strong on paper, but only if the bullets land.

Play around it: aim first, then fire (section 01), and fire in short bursts. This gun forgives a bad position; it does not forgive bad aim.

3 · The slowest bullet in the class

550 m/s is the slowest muzzle velocity of any battle rifle here, and the bullet drops about 0.7 m by 200 m. At 150 m you are holding a full head above the target.

Play around it: learn the holdover (section 03), and take the long shots at stationary targets. The flat damage is worth the arc.

4 · Hipfire is its worst fight

4.0° standing and 4.5° moving from the hip — the widest of any battle rifle here, and the bloom is 0.24° per shot from the hip. A hipfire duel under 10 m against a fast-firing rifle is a fight you're built to lose.

Play around it: keep your distance (habit 5), ADS even at 10 m, and if someone does close on you, backpedal while hipfiring rather than standing still.

5 · Slow to bring out, slow to reload dry

It takes 1,000 ms to equip — the slowest of any battle rifle here — and an empty reload is 3.1 s against a 2.0 s short reload. Swapping to your pistol mid-fight, or running the magazine dry, leaves you defenceless for over a second.

Play around it: reload early and only in cover (habit 3), and decide which tool is in your hands before the fight starts, not during it.

6 · It costs 1,000 certs — and the magazine never stops being 10 rounds

A thousand certs buys a lot of class ability ranks, suit slots and tools. What you're buying is the flat damage — 334 at any range, two headshots anywhere — and only that.

Play around it: buy it for the flat damage — the 600 ms three-shot kill at 200 m — and only after your class tools are in decent shape. If you mostly fight inside 50 m, a 250-damage rifle is the better buy.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area ~15 minutes a session

Five drills, all in the VR Training room — no certs, no enemies, no pressure. About 15 minutes a session; do one set, then go fight.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint: a 0° angle, no sideways drift at all. Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own. Two headshots is the whole gun at any range — make the neck your default.

  • Two-Shot Drill 4 minutes · the burst rhythm

    At 30–60 m, kill dummies with two headshots each, bursting two at a time with a real pause between bursts. If a dummy takes three heads, you held too long — slow down. Goal: three dummies, ten rounds, no reload mid-kill. Count your magazine while you do it — 10 rounds is three kills, so every wild round is a third of a kill thrown away.

  • Peek Drill 3 minutes · the section 01 rhythm

    Stand behind a pillar 15–25 m from a dummy. Step out, stop, ADS, fire a short burst, step back behind cover. Repeat until stop → aim → fire is one motion instead of three decisions. This is the rhythm that keeps a 0.5×-speed rifleman alive: you never stand in the open trading shots.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 3 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Remember: a short reload is 2.0 s and an empty one is 3.1 s, so the last round is your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.
