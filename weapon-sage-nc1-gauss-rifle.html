First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

Three moments, one bad habit. Read left to right.

Read it this way: the disaster is set before the sight ever comes up. Sprinting puts your cone at 7.0°, hipfiring widens it further (+0.12° per shot), and pressing ADS does not shrink a cone that is already bloomed — the scope only gives you a precise picture. Your bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running.

The fix — three beats, in order

Same courtyard, same enemy. Three beats, in order — never two at once.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

Under ~8 m? Skip the whole dance.

Inside about 8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving. No sight to raise, no settling to wait for, and stopping is optional: the hipfire ring at 8 m is only 0.28 m wide standing still, so it covers his chest. The three-beat fix is a mid-range habit; up close, your movement is your defence.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.12° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, your Nano-Regen Device (F) is already healing you through it.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.03°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The NC1 has good hip accuracy for an assault rifle. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let your Nano-Regen Device top you up between duels.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: near-full 167 damage, mild recoil, fast handling. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 3 head hits end the fight in 0.20 s. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 143 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil drifts up and to the right (23–25°), so pull your mouse gently down and to the left. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 125 damage you need 8 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover — and as a medic, your squad needs you up close anyway. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The NC1 has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Regen on, then peek

    Your Nano-Regen Device (F key) heals you and nearby allies — pop it before a duel, not during one. You're your squad's medic: staying alive is the job, and a medic who wins her own duel gets to revive the teammate behind her too.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 kills up close versus 6 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 1.95 s; an empty gun takes 2.6 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after one kill or ~20 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving medic is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Fight near your squad, inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy, heal beam and revive tool all shine at close-mid range. Don't chase a kill away from the people you keep alive — the revive you miss costs more than the kill you gain. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the NC1 pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It loses the pure DPS race up close

600 RPM is unhurried. The TR T1 Cycler fires 750 RPM — if you and a TR medic trade chest shots face-to-face, their bullets add up faster than yours: 480 ms to your 500 ms.

Play around it: don't trade fairly. Regen first, aim for the neck, and use corners — your per-bullet damage and accuracy win fights that start on your terms, and one headshot erases their whole 20 ms edge.

2 · Only 30 rounds — small for a starter rifle

The T1 Cycler carries 40. Your 30 vanish fast when suppressing a doorway, and the Extended Magazine only takes you to 35.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Your 1.95 s short reload is the second-fastest of the four starters — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

3 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 90 m you're at 125 damage: 8 chest shots, 12 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a medic — the front line, next to your squad, is your address.

4 · The recoil pulls up and to the right — and the first shot kicks

Its recoil angle is 23–25° rightward, and the first shot of a burst kicks 2.15× the normal vertical recoil. In a long burst your aim walks off the target's right shoulder — you'll see this clearly in the VR drill below.

Play around it: pull your mouse down-left while firing, in the opposite direction of the climb. The Forward Grip already trims the sideways part by 25%, and the Angled Forward Grip tames that first-shot kick if you burst properly.

5 · Aiming makes you slow — and you're a priority target

Like all assault rifles, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5x), and the gun takes 0.65 s to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your Medical Applicator out. And every enemy knows a medic keeps the squad alive, so you get shot first.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), swap weapons behind cover, never in the open — and never stand still in the open, reviving or shooting.

T1 Cycler · TR

The hose. 750 RPM and a 40-round magazine — it wins a straight hold-the-trigger trade up close, 0.48 s to your 0.50 s.

How you beat it: don't trade mag dumps. Its bullets walk sideways more than yours (0.5° tolerance vs your 0.4°), so peek, burst, and break line of sight. And punish the reload — at 2.76 s it's defenceless for an age. Push the moment you hear it click dry.

Pulsar VS1 · VS

The duellist. Same damage model as the T1, same 30-round magazine as you — but the gentlest recoil of the four (0.22 vertical) and the fastest short reload here (1.75 s).

How you beat it: don't let it re-engage for free — its reload is faster than yours, so the window is small. Your edge is per-bullet damage: you need one fewer shot in every band, and your first-shot cone (0.03° vs 0.10°) wins aimed fights from about 15 m out.

AR-100 · NSO starter

The robot's gun. What every Nanite Systems Operative is handed on day one: 143 damage at 667 RPM with the tightest horizontal tolerance here (0.2°) and near-zero recoil angle — but the slowest time to kill of the four in every band and the slowest bullets (550 m/s).

How you beat it: it needs one more bullet than you in every band (7/8/9 vs your 6/7/8), so you kill 40 ms faster up close and 20 ms faster at range. Its slow bullets need more lead on runners — strafe while you shoot and its accuracy advantage stops mattering.

NC1 Gauss Rifle · you

The hammer. Slowest fire rate here, but every bullet hits hardest, your first shot is the straightest, and your bullets are the fastest of the four.

Your discipline: a miss costs you 100 ms — more than a T1 miss costs them (80 ms). That's the whole gun in one fact: aim first, then fire, in the bursts from sections 03 and 04, and the percentages above stop mattering.

Rule of thumbOn paper the T1 kills 4–9% faster than you, shot for shot. In a real duel, after reaction and ping, it's 3–7% — and one headshot from you (0.20 s for three, up close) beats every number on this page. Fight on your terms: regen up, neck-height aim, inside 40 m.

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up-right at 23–25° — and notice how far the first shot jumps (2.15× kick). Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement and Nano-Regen do the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. With a 30-round magazine, the habit matters more than on any other starter rifle.
