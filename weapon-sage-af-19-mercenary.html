01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.12° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and at 600 RPM, that's five wasted rounds before you've even started aiming.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, your movement is your cover: as a Light Assault your jump jets break the sightline, as an Engineer the corner you picked does.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.03°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The AF-19's hipfire is the tightest of any starter carbine (section 05 shows why) — and the Laser Sight it ships with is already making it tighter. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track. As a Light Assault, your jump jets are the escape hatch: fire, hop over the doorway, reposition.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 167 damage, mild sideways walk, and the fastest time to kill of any starter carbine that needs this few bullets. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 3 head hits end the fight in a fifth of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull straight down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 139 at 50 m and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (0° angle — no sideways bias), so pull your mouse gently straight down. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 125 damage you need 8 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover — jump jets and rooftops as a Light Assault, cover-hopping as an Engineer. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The AF-19 Mercenary has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

Every slot, judged
for a new player

Good news: two of your most useful attachments — the 1x reflex sight and the Laser Sight — are already on the gun, free. Everything below is an optional sidegrade, not an upgrade. Buy in the order shown and stop whenever the gun feels right.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Use your class to choose the fight

    Your gun is good; your class is what makes the fight unfair. As a Light Assault, your jump jets put you on rooftops and above doorways — shoot down at heads from angles nobody checks. As an Engineer, your ammo pack keeps you firing and your MANA turret holds the sightline you don't want to take with a carbine. Either way, arrive at the fight on your terms: the player who chooses the range usually wins the duel.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 kills up close versus 6 to the chest, in 0.20 s instead of 0.50 s. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 1.8 s; an empty gun takes 2.75 s — and with only 30 rounds, you'll be reloading a lot. Count roughly: after one kill or ~20 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and fire rate all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones — or your jump jets as a Light Assault — and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the AF-19 Mercenary pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · No overshield to save you

You're a Light Assault or an Engineer, not a Heavy: 1,000 health and nothing extra. A shielded Heavy takes 9 chest shots up close; you take 6. One mistake in the open is the whole duel.

Play around it: your defense is movement, cover and height — jump jets, doorways, rooftops — not health. Never take a fair face-to-face trade with a shielded Heavy; shoot him from above or from behind, or don't shoot at all.

2 · Only 30 rounds — the second-smallest magazine here

The TRAC-5 carries 40. Your 30 vanish fast when suppressing a doorway, and at real accuracy that's barely one kill plus change per magazine.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). The Extended Magazine buys you 5 more rounds — and your 1.8 s reload is tied for the fastest of the four starters, so reloading often is the design, not a failure.

3 · The slowest fire rate of the four

600 RPM against 750 for the TRAC-5. A miss costs you 100 ms — more than a TRAC-5 miss costs them (80 ms) — and in a straight hold-the-trigger trade up close, their bullets add up faster than yours.

Play around it: don't trade fairly. Aim first, burst in 5s, and use your per-bullet damage: you need one fewer hit than any rival in every band.

4 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 70 m you're at 125 damage: 8 chest shots, 12 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance — jump jets and rooftops as a Light Assault, cover-hopping as an Engineer.

5 · The recoil climbs straight up — and the first shot jumps hardest

Its recoil angle is 0°, straight up, with a vertical magnitude of 0.4 — the strongest upward kick of the four starters — and the first shot kicks 1.75× as hard as the rest. In a long burst your aim walks off the target's head.

Play around it: pull your mouse straight down while firing, in the opposite direction of the climb. The Angled Forward Grip trims the first-shot kick by 60% if it still bothers you — and remember your sideways walk is the smallest here (0.45°), so the climb is the only direction you need to fight.

The ± matters more than the headline. That 200 ms is an average, not a promise: reaction time easily swings 100–250 ms with surprise, fatigue and who saw who first, and ping varies by server and connection. Up close, the TRAC-5's whole advantage is 20 ms — smaller than the normal wobble in your own reaction, and the CB-100's is just 4 ms. React a fraction sooner, pre-aim the doorway (habit 5), or land one headshot, and it's gone. Against the Solstice there is nothing to respect up close at all: you're 16 ms faster. At range the TRAC-5's edge grows to 60 ms — more than half of one of your 100 ms shot intervals — so range is where you respect it: break line of sight and close in instead of trading.

TRAC-5 · TR

The hose. 750 RPM and a 40-round magazine — it wins a straight hold-the-trigger trade up close, 0.48 s to your 0.50 s, and it keeps firing long after you've reloaded.

How you beat it: don't trade mag dumps. Its bullets walk sideways further than yours (0.55° vs 0.45°), and its first shot kicks harder than yours (2.35× vs 1.75×). And punish the reload — at 2.6 s it's the slowest of the four. Push the moment you hear it click dry. Your 1.8 s reload means you're back in the fight first.

Solstice VE3 · VS

The duellist. The fastest reload of the four (1.65 s), the fastest bullets (515 m/s), and the mildest vertical recoil (0.25) — but the biggest first-shot kick (2.8×) and a 30-round magazine like yours.

How you beat it: you kill faster up close (0.50 s to 0.52 s) and your bullets hit harder per shot. Don't let it dictate range: inside 25 m your tighter first-shot cone (0.03° vs 0.10°) wins aimed fights, and its 2.8× first-shot kick throws its opening burst off more than yours does. Past 40 m its faster bullets and softer recoil start to matter — close in or break off.

CB-100 · NSO starter

The robot's gun. What every Nanite Systems Operative is handed on day one: 143 damage at 714 RPM with the longest full-power reach of the four (15 m) — but the smallest magazine (25 rounds) and a 1.8 s reload like yours.

How you beat it: it needs one more bullet than you in every band (7/8/9 vs your 6/7/8), so its time-to-kill edge is tiny — 4 ms up close, and only past ~53 m does its 9-shot tier even start. It also kicks harder per shot (2.65× first-shot recoil) and its horizontal recoil is the joint-worst (0.25). Punish the magazine: at 25 rounds it runs dry before you do — count the burst, then push while it reloads.

AF-19 Mercenary · you

The hammer. Slowest fire rate here, but every bullet hits hardest, your first shot is the straightest (0.03°), your sideways walk is the smallest (0.45°), and your first-shot kick is the gentlest (1.75×).

Your discipline: a miss costs you 100 ms — more than a TRAC-5 miss costs them (80 ms). That's the whole gun in one fact: aim first, then fire, in the bursts from sections 03 and 04, and the percentages above stop mattering.

Rule of thumbOn paper the TRAC-5 kills 4–9% faster than you, shot for shot, while the Solstice is slower than you up close and the CB-100 is within 4%. In a real duel, after reaction and ping, it's 3–7% over the TRAC-5 — and one headshot from you (0.20 s for three, up close) beats every number on this page. Fight on your terms: height or cover first, neck-height aim, inside 40 m.

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint (0° recoil angle, no sideways bias, but the strongest upward kick of the four starters at 0.4). Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — and on this gun it works further out than on any other starter, because your hipfire starts at 1.5°. As a Light Assault, add a jump-jet hop over the dummy mid-circle: same drill, now from above.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 30-round magazine and a 1.8 s reload, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
