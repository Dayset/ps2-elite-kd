Equinox VE2 Burst — three shots, on target

The Vanu Sovereignty's burst-fire assault rifle — 650 certs, and the most accurate-feeling rifle a new VS Medic can buy. One trigger pull fires exactly three shots, and the first shot of each burst kicks less than the rest. Learn the rhythm and it out-shoots full-auto rifles at the ranges that matter.

The one-line verdict: a calm, precise rifle that trades a trigger finger for accuracy. It will not hose a room down — it wins by putting three tight shots exactly where you aimed, then doing it again. Fight between 10 and 50 metres and it will carry you.

One name warning: in the game today this rifle is called the Pulsar Burst — it was renamed in the March 2022 Arsenal Update (the old name was misleading — it isn't a variant of the Equinox VE2 at all). Same gun, same stats. If you search the store or your loadout, look for Pulsar Burst. This guide keeps the original name in its title so you can find it.

01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

Three moments, one bad habit. Read left to right.

That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun. You started firing from a sprint, from the hip — your cone at 7.0°, fully bloomed — and every hipfire shot widened it further (+0.1° per shot). Pressing ADS mid-hold changed the picture, not the bullets.

The fix — three beats, in order

Slow is smooth, smooth is fast. One beat at a time.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one: 1 · Feet — let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). 2 · Sight — press ADS and let it come up (~0.15 s). 3 · Trigger — now fire, starting from the aimed cone of 0.06° instead of a maxed-out 7.0°. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.1° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, take it behind cover; you're a Medic, not a Heavy, and cover is your shield.

3 · Trigger. Now fire — one pull, three shots. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.06°, your first burst lands where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~10 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 143 damage up close, fading to 112 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.26 s, 7 chest shots ≈ 0.52 s up close. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun. Best-case chest times: 0.52 s close, 0.60 s mid, 0.69 s far. 0–10 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. Inside ~10 m your moving hipfire ring is only about 0.52 m wide, still roughly chest-sized, so bursts land. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let your Medic self-heal top you up after the fight.

10–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, one burst at a time

Aim: neck / upper chest. One 3-round burst, pause, next burst.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage, a tight 0.06° starting cone, and the first shot of every burst kicks less than the rest. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks shots two and three into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in about a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, two-burst volleys, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Volleys of 5–6 rounds (two quick bursts), pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 125 and your cone of fire grows with every shot. Fire a volley, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil drifts up and to the right (17–19°), so pull your mouse gently down and to the left. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Single bursts, or semi-auto taps, at exposed targets only.

At 112 damage you need 9 chest shots — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you pull the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (every shot grows that cone). This gun has a fire-mode switch (B) — but the burst is the whole point of it, so learn the burst first and treat semi-auto as a specialist tool.

Hipfire, standing2.5°Your point-blank default. The 0.35 m ring at 8 m in section 05 is this stance drawn out.

One more rhythm worth knowing: standing still, your first aimed shot is the free window — after 1 shot your cone has grown to 0.11°, just under the 0.125° moving minimum, and the second shot already pushes past it (0.16°). So there is barely a still window at all: strafe between bursts from the start. The cone still grows during that first shot; the window is about the switch to moving, not about bloom pausing. (This is the Ideal ADAD rhythm: 1 free still shot, then move.)

Why the burst kicks less: most automatic rifles kick harder on the first shot of a burst (a 1.5×–3× multiplier). This gun is the opposite — its first shot kicks only 0.75×, a quarter less than the shots after it. Every burst starts gentle and then climbs. That is the gun's whole personality: the first shot is the free one, so make it count.

The target pattern is volleys of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Volleys of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). On this gun a volley of 6 is two full bursts, the natural unit — one burst plus the first two shots of the next gets you to 5. Past that, the bloom (0.05° per shot aimed, 0.1° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short volleys also leave you rounds for the second enemy — remember, the magazine is only 30.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. One pull, three shots — then decide

    The gun fires in bursts whether you like it or not. Your skill is the pause between bursts: fire, let the cone reset for a heartbeat, fire again. Mashing the trigger as fast as you can feels busy and looks impressive, but the cone never resets and your later bursts go wide. Calm re-pulls kill faster than frantic ones.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Stop, aim, then fire

    Section 01's rhythm, every single fight: feet first, sight second, trigger third. A burst fired from a maxed-out sprint cone is three wasted bullets — and with only 30 in the magazine, you can't afford to waste them.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 1.75 s; an empty gun takes 2.2 s. Count roughly: after two kills or ~15 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe between bursts: on this gun the strafing cone (0.125°) is barely wider than the planted one (0.06°), so moving costs you almost nothing.

  6. Fight near your squad, at 10–50 m

    Your damage, accuracy and burst rhythm all shine at close-mid range — and as a Medic, your healing wins fights too. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Equinox VE2 Burst pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · Your fire rate is your finger

There is no full-auto mode. Perfect re-pulls reach 698 RPM, but a real finger is slower — and while you're re-pulling, a full-auto rifle just keeps firing. In a straight hold-the-trigger trade up close, the other gun's bullets add up faster than yours.

Play around it: don't trade mag dumps. Peek, burst, and break line of sight — your tight groups and gentle first shot win fights that start on your terms, not theirs.

2 · The magazine is small

30 rounds is about four kills at best, and there is no Extended Magazine for this gun. Two kills and a few misses and you're reloading — and a 1.75 s reload in the open is a dead Medic.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 1) and early reloads (habit 4). Never start a second duel on a half-empty magazine if you can help it.

3 · Semi-auto mode is a trap

Pressing B switches to one shot per click — and silently takes away the burst's first-shot recoil reduction, so every single shot kicks harder than the first shot of a burst did.

Play around it: stay in burst mode unless you're plinking at a stationary target past 50 m. If your bursts suddenly feel jumpier than usual, check you didn't bump B.

4 · Hipfire is only middling

At 2.5° standing and 3.0° moving, your hipfire cone is wide. Inside ~10 m you're fine; past that, hipfire leaks rounds.

Play around it: don't hipfire-duel at 15 m — that's where the other rifles live. Aim down sights from 10 m out, where your 0.06° cone and low bloom take over.

5 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 80 m you're at 112 damage: 9 chest shots, 13 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. High Velocity Ammo stretches your falloff to 100 m — a cheap fix, and the only ammo this gun takes.

6 · The recoil pulls up and to the right

Its recoil angle is 17–19° rightward. In a long volley your aim walks off the target's right shoulder — you'll see this clearly in the VR drill below.

Play around it: pull your mouse down-left while firing, in the opposite direction of the climb. The Forward Grip already trims the sideways part by 25%, and the first shot of each burst kicks less to begin with.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine in single bursts without touching the mouse. Watch each 3-shot group climb up-right (17–19°) — that's your gun's fingerprint. Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the groups stay fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with single 3-round bursts, pausing fully between bursts. Then try two-burst volleys. If a volley goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down in bursts without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
