01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.10° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and at 577 RPM, that's about five rounds gone.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — take it behind cover, or above the fight on your jump jets, and the enemy still has to find you first.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.03°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and slows you down — at arm's length that gets you killed. The NC2's hipfire is tight for a carbine — a 1.5° cone standing still — so trust it: strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track. This is your home turf — the gun was built for it.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its 1,000 certs: full 167 damage to 10 m (15 m with Soft Point), a hard-hitting 577 RPM, and a tight 0.03° aimed cone. Start your crosshair at the neck — the straight-up climb walks your shots into the head, and 3 head hits end the fight in a fifth of a second.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 125 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (0° angle), so pull your mouse gently down. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 125 damage you need 8 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The NC2 Gauss Compact has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

Attachments: what to buy, what to skip

Every slot, judged
for a new player

One thing to know first: the NC2 Gauss Compact costs 1,000 certs to unlock, and it arrives bare — iron sights, no rail, no barrel, standard ammo. Nothing below is free. Buy in the order shown and stop whenever the gun feels right. If you already own the free starter AF-19 Mercenary — same 167 damage at a faster 600 RPM — the NC2's 1,000 certs buy you its underbarrel options and a gentler 1.4× first-shot kick, not more damage. 08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Height first, then shoot

    Your class is your positioning tool, and it only helps if you use it before the bullets arrive. Light Assaults: your jump jets put you on roofs and ledges nobody checks — land above the fight, then open fire from an angle nobody is watching. Engineers: your version is the ground itself — set your ammo pack down, fight from behind cover, and let the enemy come to your crosshair.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 kills up close versus 6 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's straight-up climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire — 0.06° per aimed shot — and each one costs you 104 ms. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.365 s; an empty gun takes 2.94 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after one or two kills or ~20 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe between bursts: a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Pick fights inside 40 metres — and with an exit

    Your damage, accuracy and mobility all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. Light Assaults: your jump jets are the exit — if the fight turns, go up and over, not back through the doorway you came in.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the NC2 Gauss Compact pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It falls off a cliff at range

Past 70 m you're at 125 damage: 8 chest shots, 12 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Your 500 m/s bullets need real lead on runners out there.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance — or let a longer-ranged squadmate take the shot.

2 · Only 30 rounds — and the fix is only +5

At 577 RPM the magazine lasts about 3 seconds of holding the trigger, and the Extended Magazine only adds 5 rounds (30 → 35) — while making the gun slower to equip every time you pull it out.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Your 2.365 s short reload is quick for the class — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

3 · The first shot jumps — straight up

First-shot recoil is ×1.4 on top of 0.45 vertical recoil — the first round jumps noticeably, and the climb is straight up (0° angle), so every shot stacks in the same line. In a long burst your aim walks straight off the top of the target's head — you'll see this clearly in the VR drill below.

Play around it: pull your mouse straight down while firing. The Forward Grip already trims the sideways part by 25%, and short bursts mean the first-shot kick only bites once per burst.

4 · Bloom grows every shot you hold

Your cone grows 0.06° with every aimed shot (0.10° from the hip) — a shot every 104 ms. Ten rounds held down adds 0.6° to your cone, twenty times your 0.03° starting spread.

Play around it: five-round bursts, always. The release between bursts is what makes this gun accurate — without it, you're just making noise at 577 RPM.

5 · Aiming still slows you down

0.5× ADS move speed is half your normal pace — and the gun takes 0.55 s to swap to, slow if a fight surprises you with your repair tool out.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint, and the first shot's ×1.4 kick makes the jump at the start. Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5–8, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
