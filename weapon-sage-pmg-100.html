01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

3 minutes · learn this before anything else

Most new players die in a way they cannot explain: they ran in, aimed at an enemy, held the trigger — and hit nothing. It is not your aim. It is the cone. This gun fires inside a cone of spread that gets huge while you move and tiny when you stop and aim. Start a fight from the big cone and you miss shots you aimed perfectly. Start from the tiny one and the same fight is a kill.

The order matters

Every fight starts the same way: stop, aim, then fire — in that order, every time. Release the sprint key and let your feet settle (about 0.3 s), press right mouse to aim down sights (about 0.15 s), then press the trigger. The order is the whole lesson: aiming first shrinks the cone to 0.30°, and from there the gun holds it. Skip a beat and the bullets go everywhere.

The second mistake: holding the trigger

The other way to lose a fight you were winning is to hold the trigger down. Every shot adds 0.05° of bloom to the cone, and the cone only shrinks once you release the trigger and let it settle. A held trigger turns a 0.30° cone into a 0.80° cone in ten shots — and at that width the bullets miss the chest entirely. Fire in bursts of 5, release for a beat, fire again. Forty rounds in the magazine is not a reason to hold the trigger.

The fix — three beats, in order

Same fight, done right. Each beat has a job, and the order is the whole lesson.

1 · StopLet go of sprint (and the trigger). About 0.3 s for the gun to settle.

→

2 · AimPress ADS and let the sight come up (~0.15 s).

→

3 · FireNow fire — from the aimed cone. First shots land where the dot points.

The exception — under ~8 m, skip the whole danceHipfire works, no ADS needed, and stopping is optional. Keep moving, strafe, and let the hipfire ring do the work.

Why this works on the PMG-100

This gun's aimed cone is 0.30° in every stance — standing, crouched, strafing, it never changes. The problem is never the gun; it is the 7.0° cone you start in when you sprint. The gap between those two numbers is the whole lesson: stop first, and the gun does the rest. And once the sight is up, you can keep moving — strafing while aimed costs nothing on this gun, so the stop is only for the sprint, not for the fight.

Stances and firing modes

How you stand decides how far your bullets fly

The cone — the spread your bullets land inside — depends on what your body is doing. Your first shots are always the most accurate. Every shot after the first makes the cone bigger, by a fixed amount, until you stop firing and let it settle.

StanceStarting coneWhat it means
Aimed (ADS), standing still0.30°Your best accuracy — and it never gets better than this. Plant your feet and every bullet goes where the crosshair is.
Aimed (ADS), crouched0.30°Same cone as standing, but you are a smaller target and the rifle is steadier — use it behind cover.
Aimed (ADS), strafing0.30°The same cone as standing still — strafing while aimed costs nothing at all, and you move at three-quarter speed (0.75×). Strafe through the whole burst.
Hipfire, standing still1.0°Fine up close (under ~8 m). Past that, bullets start missing the chest.
Hipfire, crouched1.0°Same cone as standing hipfire — crouching does not tighten it on this gun.
Hipfire, moving1.5°The loosest cone you should ever fire from. Only inside ~5 m, only because the enemy is that close.

One more number worth knowing: your ADAD window is 0 aimed shots (10 from the hip). Your aimed cone is 0.30° in every stance — standing, crouched, strafing, it never changes — so strafing while aimed costs nothing at all: the cone is already at the moving floor. From the hip you get 10 free still shots: your first 10 hipfire shots are the window — after 10 shots your cone has grown to 1.50°, exactly the 1.50° moving minimum, so starting to strafe then costs nothing. The cone still grows during those 10 shots; the window is about the switch to moving, not about bloom pausing. (This is the Ideal ADAD rhythm: 0 free still shots aimed, then move.)

One more number: the first-shot kick is 2× — your opening shot jumps 0.4° (0.2° × 2) before the burst settles. Aim the first shot at the chest, where the jump still lands, and let the burst settle onto the head.

How to kill efficiently

Six habits, in order of value

Killing fast is not about flicking. It is about a short list of habits, done the same way every fight.

  1. Stop, aim, then fire

    Section 01, every fight, no exceptions past 10 m. The 0.30° cone is your biggest advantage — don't throw it away by firing from a sprint.

  2. Let the fire rate do the work

    896 RPM means nine chest shots take 536 ms — you do not need to land every bullet perfectly, you need to keep the cone on the chest and let the bullets arrive. Don't over-aim each shot; aim the burst.

  3. Fire in bursts of 5

    Five rounds, release for a beat, five more. The cone resets between bursts, so every burst starts at 0.30°. Holding the trigger through the magazine is how you lose fights you were winning — even with 40 rounds.

  4. Reload before you need to

    Your reload is 1.9 s with rounds left, 2.3 s on empty. Forty rounds is four kills' worth — reload after every kill, behind cover, while the fight is still yours. Never start a fight on 9 rounds.

  5. Aim for the head, accept the chest

    5 headshots kill in 0.27 s; 9 chest shots take 0.54 s. That is twice as fast. Aim at the head, and the chest hits you land on the way still count — a mix of 1 headshot + 7 chest shots kills in 8 shots, faster than 9 chest alone.

  6. Lead your target at range

    Your bullets are the fastest in this set (450 m/s — a target at 50 m needs about 0.11 s of travel). At 25 m+ aim a body-width ahead of a running enemy, and a little more with Soft Point (405 m/s).

Weak spots of the gun

Know them, and they stop killing you

Every gun has a price. The PMG-100's price is the damage: 112 a bullet is the lightest in this set, and the 40-round magazine hides how fast it empties. Here is the full list, with the counter-habit for each.

1 · 112 damage is the lightest in this set

Nine shots to kill up close, where the Tengu needs five pulls and the PMG-200 needs eight. Every missed bullet costs you 67 ms and a tenth of the kill.

Counter: Aim for the head (5 headshots take 0.27 s), and let the 896 RPM cover the misses — the fire rate is the compensation for the damage.

2 · The magazine lies

Forty rounds feels endless, so you hold the trigger. The cone blooms 0.05° a shot, and by shot 12 you are spraying — the magazine is still half full and the fight is already lost.

Counter: Fire in bursts of 5 even with 40 rounds. Eight bursts is the whole magazine; count them.

3 · Damage falls off early

Minimum damage is 84, and it arrives at 40 m — the shortest falloff in this set. Past 33 m you need 12 shots to kill.

Counter: Fit Soft Point (full damage to 15 m) and close the distance behind cover. Don't trade at 40 m against a gun that kills in 8 shots — reposition and take the fight at 15 m, where you are at full power.

4 · No HVA, no compensator

The fastest bullets here (450 m/s) cannot get faster — there is no High Velocity Ammo — and the 0.2° vertical recoil cannot be cut by a barrel, because there is no compensator.

Counter: Fit the Forward Grip for the sideways walk (0.75° → 0.56°), and accept that 450 m/s is already the fastest bullet in this set.

5 · It costs 1,000 certs and looks free

The flavour text calls it standard-issue, and new NSO players assume it is the starter. It is not — the PMG-100 is a 1,000-cert purchase, and the free NSO starter LMG is the XMG-100.

Counter: Try the playstyle in VR Training first — the gun is free to test there — before you spend the certs.

Drills: 45 minutes in VR Training

Do these once, then play

VR Training (the warpgate terminal, free, no one shoots back) is where you build the habits. Forty-five minutes here saves you a week of dying to the same mistakes.

  • The 0.3-second stop10 min · VR Training
    Sprint toward a target, stop, aim, fire 5. Repeat until the stop-aim-fire sequence is one motion. Goal: every first burst lands 4 or more of 5 shots.
  • Five and release10 min · VR Training
    At 20 m, fire bursts of 5 at a target, releasing between bursts. Count hits per burst. Goal: 5 of 5 hits on the first three bursts, every time.
  • The 8-metre line5 min · VR Training
    Walk toward a target firing from the hip. Find the distance where your hits start missing the chest — that is your hipfire line, about 8 m. Memorise it.
  • Forty rounds, eight bursts10 min · VR Training
    At 20 m, empty a magazine in bursts of 5, counting each burst. Eight bursts, no held trigger. Goal: every burst lands 4 or more of 5 shots.
  • Count to nineLive play
    In your next five fights, count the shots of each kill out loud. Nine chest shots is the number — learn to feel when the ninth lands.
