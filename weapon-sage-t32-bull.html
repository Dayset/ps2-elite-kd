T32 Bull — the accurate TR LMG

A 1,000-cert light machine gun for the Terran Republic Heavy Assault. It trades raw damage for the tightest aim in the TR arsenal — best hipfire of any TR LMG, a steady 0.1° aimed cone, and recoil that climbs straight up instead of wandering sideways.

The one-line verdict: the accurate one. It rewards good aim and short bursts, and punishes spray-and-pray. Fight between 10 and 40 metres, burst in fives, and it will out-shoot guns that look stronger on paper.

01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

Read it left to right: sprint, aim mid-hold, miss. The sight picture lies — the cone of fire underneath it does not reset.

The fix — three beats, in order

One beat at a time. Each beat is short — together they take about half a second, and your overshield (F) covers it.

Close-up exception — under ~8 m, skip all three beats Hipfire works here — no ADS needed, and stopping is optional. Just point at the chest and keep moving; the Bull has the best hipfire of any TR LMG, and at arm's length the wide hip cone still lands. Save the stop–aim–fire rhythm for everything past 8 m.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.1° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, your overshield (F) covers it.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.1°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 143 damage up close, fading to 125 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you two numbers worth memorising — this gun has only two damage tiers, not three:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.27 s, 7 chest shots ≈ 0.55 s (546 ms) up close, 8 chest shots ≈ 0.64 s (637 ms) past 10 m. Damage per second is 1,571 up close and 1,373 at range — steady rather than spectacular, which is why the head matters so much on this gun. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, four head hits end a duel in about a quarter of a second.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 10 m, then a gentle slide to minimum at 65 m — the smallest damage drop of any TR LMG. The gold line shows Soft Point Ammunition, which stretches full power to 15 m; High Velocity Ammunition does the opposite job, pushing minimum damage out to 85 m.

Rule of thumbInside 10 m you are at maximum power, and past it you lose only one bullet's worth of killing power — 7 shots become 8, and stay 8 all the way out. That's the Bull's quiet strength: it barely fades, so a mid-range duel plays almost like a close one.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving. 7 shots to kill.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The Bull has the best hipfire of any TR LMG, so trust it: strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let the overshield soak the return fire.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5 rounds. 7 shots inside 10 m, 8 just past it.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage, mild recoil, fast handling. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in about a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull straight down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them. 8 shots to kill.

Damage is sliding toward 125 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (0° angle, no sideways bias), so pull your mouse gently down. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only. 8 shots to kill.

At 125 damage you need 8 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The T32 Bull has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.05° per shot aimed, 0.1° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short bursts also leave you rounds for the second enemy — remember, the magazine is 60.

RTA Reflex — 1x

No zoom. The target at 35 m is small but the view is wide open — you see the crate, the tower, and the next enemy coming. Fast to aim, nothing blocks your edges. Best inside ~40 m.

MH2 Reflex — 2x

Same target, same 35 m — but he looks twice as tall, so placing shots on his chest and head gets much easier. The price: your view narrows to the round window, the crate and tower are gone, and close targets feel fast and huge. Best at 25–50 m.

That's the whole 30-cert decision from section 06 in one picture: 1x for awareness, 2x for precision. Most veterans carry both and swap per base — indoors 1x, open ground 2x.

for a new player

One honest warning first: unlike a starter gun, the Bull arrives bare — the 1x sight and the Forward Grip cost certs here, they don't come in the box. Everything below is an optional sidegrade, not an upgrade. Buy in the order shown and stop whenever the gun feels right.

Good30 certs

Doubles your view magnification. Costs almost nothing — the price of a kill or two.

3.4x & 4x scopes

Skip for now30 certs

DMO / HDS (3.4x) and TMS / ACS (4x) — sniper-style magnification on an automatic LMG.

Good: almost nothing a beginner does — recoil magnifies too, and tunnels your vision.

Bad: any fight inside 30 m, which is where you'll spend your first hundred hours.

Good — default pick100 certs

Good: the default for aimed, mid-range play — leave it on until you deliberately build for hipfire.

Situational100 certs

The first shot of each burst kicks 60% less — but side-to-side recoil rises 10% on everything.

Good: disciplined burst and tap-fire at 40 m+, where every burst starts fresh. The Bull's first-shot kick is a strong 2.0x, so this pays better here than on gentler guns.

Bad: beginners' longer bursts — the sideways tax adds up once you hold past 5 rounds.

Darklight Flashlight

Tool, not a buff100 certs

A torch whose beam reveals cloaked infiltrators. Toggle it with the attachment key (X by default).

Good: hunting a stalker infiltrator camping your capture point — sweep corners with it.

Bad: as your everyday rail — it gives zero accuracy help, and the beam advertises your position.

Ammo — three options, one kind one, one trap

Soft Point Ammunition

First buy100 certs

Full 143 damage now reaches to 15 m instead of 10 m (the gold line on the chart above). Cost: bullets fly 10% slower (640 → 576 m/s).

Good: nearly always — most duels happen at 10–20 m, exactly where this extends your 7-shot kill.

Bad: long-range duels against runners, where the slower bullet needs more lead. A small price.

High Velocity Ammunition

Situational100 certs

Bullets fly 10% faster (640 → 704 m/s) and minimum damage now holds out to 85 m instead of 65 m. Cost: full damage ends 2 m sooner (10 m → 8 m).

Good: open-field fights at 40 m+, where faster bullets mean less leading on moving targets.

Bad: close work — you give up 2 m of full damage for speed you don't need indoors.

KCAP Ammunition

Skip for now200 certs

Leg shots stop being punished — the leg multiplier flips from 0.9x to 1.2x, so leg hits deal 20% more than chest hits. The price is steep: headshots drop from 2x to 1.5x, turning your 4-shot head kill into 5.

Good: almost nothing while you're learning — it only pays if you reliably hit legs and almost never hit heads.

Bad: this gun. The whole point of the Bull is accurate aimed fire, and KCAP taxes exactly that. Twice the price of the other ammo types, for a trade a beginner loses on.

One thing you can't buy: an Extended Magazine. The Bull doesn't take one, so 60 rounds is your ceiling — burst discipline is your extended magazine.

Spend order for your first certsRTA Reflex 1x (30) → Forward Grip (100) → Soft Point Ammo (100) → then stop. 230 certs total. Put the next several hundred certs into your overshield and suit slots — they win more fights than any barrel.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Shield first, then shoot

    Your overshield (F key) is extra health that only helps if it's on before the bullets arrive. Hear footsteps or expect a corner? Shield up, then peek. A shielded Heavy needs 11 chest shots to drop up close instead of 7 — that gap wins duels.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.89 s; an empty gun takes 3.28 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after two kills or ~40 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 15 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving Heavy is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and shield all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the T32 Bull pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It loses the pure DPS race up close

143 damage at 659 RPM is 1,571 damage per second. The VS Orion and TR CARV both fire 750 RPM — about 1,788 per second — so if you and an enemy Heavy trade chest shots face-to-face, their bullets add up faster than yours.

Play around it: don't trade fairly. Shield first, aim for the neck, and use corners — your per-bullet accuracy and tight cone win fights that start on your terms.

2 · Only 60 rounds — and no extended magazine exists for it

The T9 CARV carries 100, and the Bull can't mount an Extended Magazine at all. Your 60 vanish fast when suppressing a doorway.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Your 2.89 s reload is quick for an LMG — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

3 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 65 m you're at 125 damage: 8 chest shots, 12 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a Heavy — the front line is your address.

4 · The first shot kicks twice as hard

Its first-shot recoil multiplier is 2.0x — the opening kick of every burst is double the rest. The good news: the recoil climbs straight up (0° angle, no sideways bias), so the fix is simple, and once you release the trigger your aim settles back at 13° per second.

Play around it: pull your mouse straight down while firing. The Forward Grip already trims the small sideways part (0.18°) by 25%, and the Angled Forward Grip exists for players who live on that first shot.

5 · Aiming makes you slow

Like all LMGs, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5x), and the gun takes 0.8 s to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your rocket launcher out.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint, no sideways wander. Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your shield and movement do the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 40 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
