01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

3 minutes · learn this before anything else

Most new players die in a way they cannot explain: they ran in, aimed at an enemy, held the trigger — and hit nothing. It is not your aim. It is the cone. This gun fires inside a cone of spread that gets huge while you move and tiny when you stop and aim. Start a fight from the big cone and you miss shots you aimed perfectly. Start from the tiny one and the same fight is a kill. With the Tengu there is a second spread to respect: the four pellets scatter inside their own pattern, and that pattern is three times wider from the hip than aimed.

The order matters

Every fight starts the same way: stop, aim, then fire — in that order, every time. Release the sprint key and let your feet settle (about 0.3 s), press right mouse to aim down sights (about 0.15 s), then press the trigger. The order is the whole lesson: aiming first shrinks the cone to 0.15°, and the smart choke squeezes the four pellets into a fist-sized group. Skip a beat and the pellets go everywhere.

The second mistake: holding the trigger

The other way to lose a fight you were winning is to hold the trigger down. Every pull adds 0.07° of bloom to the cone, and the cone only shrinks once you release the trigger and let it settle. A held trigger turns a 0.15° cone into a 0.71° cone in eight pulls — and at that width the pellets miss the chest entirely. Fire in bursts of 5, release for a beat, fire again. And remember: every pull that loses a pellet costs you a quarter of the damage, so the first pulls matter more than the last ones.

Same fight, done right. Each beat has a job, and the order is the whole lesson.

The exception — under ~8 m, skip the whole danceHipfire works, no ADS needed, and stopping is optional. Keep moving, strafe, and let the hipfire ring do the work.

Why this works on the NSX Tengu

This gun's aimed cone is 0.15° — tight — and the smart choke squeezes the pellet spread to 0.75° when you aim. The problem is never the gun; it is the 7.0° cone you start in when you sprint, and the 3.0° pellet spread you fire from the hip. The gap between those numbers is the whole lesson: stop first, aim, and the pellets land in a group 0.10 m wide at 8 m.

The 8-metre exception is weaker on this gun

Section 01's exception — hipfire under 8 m — barely applies to the Tengu. From the hip the pellets spread 0.42 m wide at 8 m, so even a perfect centre hold can lose a pellet or two, and one lost pellet is a quarter of the pull's damage gone. Aim down sights whenever you have the quarter-second to do it. Hipfire only when the enemy is close enough to fill the whole pattern — about 5 m — and keep strafing while you do it.

Damage: how many pulls to kill

The numbers you will use every fight

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Each trigger pull fires four pellets of 50 damage — 200 damage per pull up close, fading to 144 at long range. A pull only counts if the pellets land: one missed pellet is 25% of the damage gone. Headshots count double per pellet, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

Stances and firing modes

How you stand decides how far your bullets fly

The cone — the spread your bullets land inside — depends on what your body is doing. Your first shots are always the most accurate. Every shot after the first makes the cone bigger, by a fixed amount, until you stop firing and let it settle.

StanceStarting coneWhat it means
Aimed (ADS), standing still0.15°Your best accuracy. Plant your feet and every pellet goes where the crosshair is.
Aimed (ADS), crouched0.15°Same cone as standing, but you are a smaller target and the rifle is steadier — use it behind cover.
Aimed (ADS), strafing0.32°The moving floor while scoped — twice the planted cone — and you move at three-quarter speed (0.75×). Strafe between bursts to stay hard to hit; plant your feet during each burst.
Hipfire, standing still1.75°The cone is honest, but the pellets are not: from the hip they scatter 3.0°, about 0.42 m wide at 8 m. Aim down sights when you can.
Hipfire, crouched1.75°Same cone as standing hipfire. Crouching does not tighten the pellet spread.
Hipfire, moving2.0°The loosest cone you should ever fire from, and the pellets spread wider still. Only inside ~5 m, only because the enemy is that close.

One more number worth knowing: your ADAD window is 2 aimed pulls (3 from the hip). Standing still, your first 2 aimed pulls are the free window — after 2 pulls your cone has grown to 0.29°, a hair under the 0.32° moving minimum, so starting to strafe then costs almost nothing. The cone still grows during those 2 pulls; the window is about the switch to moving, not about bloom pausing. (This is the Ideal ADAD rhythm: 2 free still pulls, then move.)

One more number: the first-shot kick is 3.5× — your opening pull jumps 1.4° (0.4° × 3.5) before the burst settles. Aim the first pull at the chest, where the jump still lands, and let the burst settle onto the head.

08

How to kill efficiently

Six habits, in order of value

Killing fast is not about flicking. It is about a short list of habits, done the same way every fight.

  1. Aim down sights, even at arm's length

    The smart choke is the whole gun: aimed, the pellets land in a 0.10 m group; from the hip they scatter across 0.42 m. The quarter-second to raise the sight is worth two pellets.

  2. Count pulls, not pellets

    Five pulls kill up close, six to 26 m, seven past it — if every pellet lands. One missed pellet is a quarter of a pull's damage gone. When a kill takes an extra pull, that is usually why.

  3. Fire in bursts of 5

    Five pulls, release for a beat, five more. The cone resets between bursts, so every burst starts at 0.15°. Holding the trigger through the magazine is how you lose fights you were winning.

  4. Reload before you need to

    Your reload is 1.9 s with pulls left, 2.5 s on empty — and the magazine only holds 22 pulls, four kills' worth up close. Reload after every kill, behind cover. Never start a fight on 6 pulls.

  5. Move between bursts, not during them

    Strafe while your trigger is up and the cone is resetting — that is when you are hardest to hit. Plant your feet while the pellets are actually leaving the barrel. Two still pulls, then move: that is the ADAD rhythm.

  6. Lead your target at range

    Your pellets travel at 380 m/s — a target at 50 m needs about 0.13 s of travel. At 25 m+ aim a body-width ahead of a running enemy, and a little more with Soft Point (342 m/s).

09

Weak spots of the gun

Know them, and they stop killing you

Every gun has a price. The Tengu's price is the pellets: all of its damage assumes four hits per pull, and the gun punishes you the moment one goes wide. Here is the full list, with the counter-habit for each.

1 · Four pellets, or nothing

200 damage per pull assumes all four pellets hit. Lose one pellet and the pull does 150; lose two and it does 100 — worse than a 125-damage SMG. At range, or against a strafing enemy, pellets miss.

Counter: Aim down sights, burst in 5s, and fight inside 15 m where the pattern is tight. Never trust a hipfire pull past 8 m.

2 · A 22-pull magazine

Four kills' worth up close — fewer if pellets miss. The Punisher carries 25, the PMG-100 carries 40, and a second enemy after the first can catch you reloading.

Counter: Reload after every kill (1.9 s is quick), and count your pulls — four bursts of 5 is nearly the whole magazine. Extended Magazine (26 pulls) exists for exactly this problem.

3 · Slow between pulls (117 ms)

513 RPM is the slowest fire rate in this set. Every missed pull costs you 117 ms — more than any other SMG here.

Counter: Make the first pulls count: stop, aim, fire. The 468 ms kill time is the fastest here, but only on a clean first burst.

4 · Hipfire is a trap

The hip pellet spread is 3.0° — 0.42 m wide at 8 m — the widest hipfire pattern of any SMG in this set. The section 01 hipfire exception barely applies to this gun.

Counter: Aim down sights in every fight you have time to. The Hybrid Laser (100 certs) is the only attachment that fixes hipfire, and even then the pellets spread 0.28 m.

5 · Damage falls off hard past 26 m

Past 26 m you need 7 pulls to kill, and each pull takes 117 ms — 702 ms best case, slower than every other SMG here at that range.

Counter: Close the distance behind cover, or tap-fire at stationary targets only. Don't trade at 30 m against a gun that kills in 6 shots — reposition and take the fight at 10 m, where you are at full power.

Drills: 45 minutes in VR Training

Do these once, then play

VR Training (the warpgate terminal, free, no one shoots back) is where you build the habits. Forty-five minutes here saves you a week of dying to the same mistakes.

  • The 0.3-second stop10 min · VR Training
    Sprint toward a target, stop, aim, fire 5 pulls. Repeat until the stop-aim-fire sequence is one motion. Goal: every first burst lands 4 or more of 5 pulls on the chest.
  • Five and release10 min · VR Training
    At 15 m, fire bursts of 5 pulls at a target, releasing between bursts. Count pulls per burst, not pellets. Goal: 5 of 5 pulls on the chest on the first three bursts, every time.
  • The 8-metre pellet test5 min · VR Training
    Stand 8 m from a target and fire one pull from the hip, then one pull aimed. Compare the pellet groups. That difference is why you aim down sights on this gun — memorise it.
  • Headshots on the move10 min · VR Training
    Strafe left and right while aiming at a target's head at 15 m. Fire 2-pull bursts. Goal: 2 headshot pulls per burst.
  • ADS every fightLive play
    In your next five fights, aim down sights even when the enemy is 5 m away. Win or lose, note how many pulls each kill took. The sight costs a quarter-second and saves a pull.
