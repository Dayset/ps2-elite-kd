01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

Three moments, one mistake. Read left to right.

1 · Sprinting

You're sprinting, firing from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot widens it further (+0.12° per shot from the hip).

2 · ADS, mid-hold

Then you press ADS mid-hold — and nothing resets. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring.

3 · The result

That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun. Two timers stack the deck further — about 0.3 s for the gun to settle after a sprint, and ~0.15 s to raise the sight. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

The fix — three beats, in order

Same fight, done right. One beat at a time — never two at once.

1 · Stop

Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint. Give the gun its ~0.3 s to settle.

2 · Aim

Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up — about 0.15 s. That's fine: your overshield (F) covers it. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.03°, your first shots land where the dot points.

3 · Fire

Trigger. Now fire — in short bursts of about 5. You control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone. Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one.

The exception — under ~8 m, skip all of this

Inside about 8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving. At arm's length the hipfire ring is small enough to hit, scoping in costs you time and halves your speed, and stopping is optional: a strafing hipfire spray wins the room fight. The three-beat rule is a mid-range habit — close enough to reach for the sight, far enough for the spray to miss. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The EM6's hipfire is loose for an LMG (4.0° standing), so keep it close and let the 100-round magazine do the talking: strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let the overshield soak the return fire.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 167 damage, manageable recoil, a magazine that outlasts two enemies. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 3 head hits end the fight in a fifth of a second. Strafe between bursts; plant your feet during each one, because strafing while scoped opens your cone to 0.4°.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull straight down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 143 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (0° angle, no sideways drift), so pull your mouse gently straight down. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 125 damage you need 8 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The EM6 has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.06° per shot aimed, 0.12° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short bursts also leave you rounds for the second enemy — and with a 100-round magazine, you have rounds to spare for the third.

Every slot, judged
for a new player

Unlike the starter GD-22S, the EM6 arrives bare: iron sights, no grip, no ammo. Your first purchases are cheap and obvious — a sight and a grip — and everything after that is an optional sidegrade, not an upgrade. Buy in the order shown and stop whenever the gun feels right.

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Shield first, then shoot

    Your overshield (F key) is extra health that only helps if it's on before the bullets arrive. Hear footsteps or expect a corner? Shield up, then peek. A shielded Heavy needs 9 chest shots to drop up close instead of 6 — that gap wins duels.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 kills up close versus 6 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 4.7 s; an empty gun takes 5.7 s — an eternity mid-fight, and the slowest reload of any gun you'll meet. Count roughly: after two kills or ~60 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 20 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe between bursts: a moving Heavy is dramatically harder to headshot back, and planting your feet during each burst keeps your cone at 0.03°.

  6. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and shield all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the EM6 pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · The reload is the slowest you'll meet

4.7 seconds with rounds left, 5.7 on empty — the GD-22S reloads in 2.3 s, the CARV in 5.4 s but carries the same 100 rounds. A reload started at the wrong moment is a death sentence, and the Extended Magazine makes it worse by teaching you to forget to reload at all.

Play around it: reload early and only in cover (habit 4). With 100 rounds you can afford to reload after every kill or two — the magazine is the buffer, the reload is the tax.

2 · It loses the pure DPS race up close

600 RPM is slow for an LMG. The VS Orion and TR CARV both fire 750 RPM — if you and an enemy Heavy trade chest shots face-to-face, their bullets add up faster than yours (0.48 s to your 0.50 s).

Play around it: don't trade fairly. Shield first, aim for the neck, and use corners — your per-bullet damage and accuracy win fights that start on your terms.

3 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 75 m you're at 125 damage: 8 chest shots, 12 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. High Velocity Ammunition pushes the floor out to 95 m, which helps — but you're still a Heavy, and the front line is your address.

4 · The first shot kicks hard

Your first shot in every burst recoils at 1.8× the normal kick — heavier than the GD-22S's 1.5×. The climb is straight up (0° angle, no sideways drift), which is easy to correct, but that first jump can throw your opening round high if you aren't ready for it.

Play around it: start your crosshair a touch low, at the upper chest rather than the neck, and pull straight down as you fire. The Angled Forward Grip cuts that first-shot kick by 60% if it keeps bothering you.

5 · Loose hipfire

4.0° standing, 4.5° moving — looser than the GD-22S (3.0° / 3.75°) and the Orion. At 8 m your standing hipfire ring is already wider than a man's chest, so hipfire is a point-blank tool only, not a general one.

Play around it: hipfire only inside ~8 m, and take the Laser Sight if you build for it — it pulls the ring back inside the chest (0.37 m).

6 · Aiming makes you slow

Like all LMGs, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5x), and the gun takes 1.1 s to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your rocket launcher out.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint, with the first shot jumping highest (1.8× kick). Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5–8, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your shield and movement do the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 60 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 4.7 s reload, doing it in the open is the most expensive mistake this gun lets you make.
