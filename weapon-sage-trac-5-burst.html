01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.10° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — use it to let the sprint settle before any bullet leaves.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.06°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 143 damage up close, fading to 112 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). There are three bands to memorise — full damage to 10 m, a steady slide to 60 m, then minimum:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.24 s up close (5 headshots ≈ 0.32 s once your damage has fallen past 39 m); 7 chest shots ≈ 0.48 s up close, 8 ≈ 0.56 s at mid range, 9 ≈ 0.64 s far out. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun: four head hits end it at any distance inside 39 m. Note the mid band: you need 7 chest shots only inside 10.2 m, and 8 from there to 39.0 m — the switch happens almost as soon as your damage starts falling. Magazine: 42 rounds, with 252 in reserve.

10–25 mSweet spot

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage, a 0.06° starting cone, and recoil that climbs up and to the right. Plant your feet during each burst — you drop to half speed while aiming, so strafe between bursts, not during them. 25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Bursts of 3–6 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding from 134 toward 118 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs up and to the right (23–25°), so pull your mouse gently down-left. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Aim: chest. Single bursts of 3 at exposed targets only.

At 112 damage you need 9 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The TRAC-5 Burst keeps one fire mode — 3-round burst. There is no full-auto and no semi-auto: every trigger pull fires exactly three rounds, and the burst IS the gun.

One more thing worth knowing: one trigger pull is a 3-round burst — shots 80 ms apart inside the burst, 80 ms between bursts — so a held trigger is a steady 750 RPM stream. The cone still grows 0.05° with every shot, so there are no free shots with no cone growth on this gun.

One more number worth knowing: your ADAD window is 1 aimed shot (5 from the hip). Standing still, your first aimed shot lands before you start strafing — after it your cone has grown to 0.11°, and after the second to 0.16°, past the 0.15° moving minimum, so starting to strafe then costs nothing. From the hip the window is 5 shots: 2.0° + 5 × 0.10° = 2.5°, exactly the moving minimum. The cone still grows during those shots; the window is about the switch to moving, not about bloom pausing. In practice the aimed window is so short that the rule is simple: plant your feet for the burst, strafe between bursts.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Take the angle first

    As a Light Assault, your jump jets are the angle: get above the fight — a rooftop, a ledge, a balcony — before you open fire. As an Engineer, use doorways and cover the same way, and keep your ammo pack down for the squad holding with you. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills at any range inside 39 m versus 7–9 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural climb carry shots two and three up and to the right, into the skull. Four head hits end the fight in about a quarter of a second (0.24 s). It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. One trigger pull is 3 rounds; a good hold is two pulls — 6 rounds. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal; release the trigger for a heartbeat between holds — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.60 s; an empty gun takes 3.7 s. That extra 1.1 s is over a second standing defenceless. Count roughly: after two kills or ~30 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 12 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe — between bursts

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. And remember: you drop to half speed while aiming, so strafe between bursts to stay hard to hit, and plant your feet while each burst is firing — a still shooter lands bursts, a moving shooter dodges them.

  6. Pick fights inside 40 metres — near your squad

    Your damage, accuracy and mobility all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. And stay near friendlies: a Light Assault alone in the open is a free kill, but one dropping in behind a squad line is the reason the line holds — and an Engineer beside a MAX is worth two guns.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the TRAC-5 Burst pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It has no full-auto mode — and no semi-auto either

Burst is all it has: every trigger pull fires exactly three rounds and then stops. If you panic and clamp the trigger, it will keep bursting at 750 RPM — but every shot still comes in threes with an 80 ms gap, and you can't spray a doorway the way an automatic can.

Play around it: commit to the burst rhythm (habit 3) instead of fighting it — the gun is at its best when you treat each pull as one deliberate decision.

2 · 42 rounds, but a 3.7 s empty reload

The magazine is generous — 42 rounds — but an empty gun takes 3.7 s to reload, over a second longer than a short reload.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Your 2.60 s short reload is the design, not a failure.

3 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 60 m you're at 112 damage: 9 chest shots, 13 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire single bursts at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. As a Light Assault your jump jets are the shortcut — arrive at 40 m from above instead of walking across their sightline.

4 · The burst climbs up and to the right

Its recoil angle is 23–25° — up and to the right, not straight up — at 0.3–0.5° per shot, and the climb builds inside every burst. In a long hold your aim walks off the top-right of the target's head — you'll see this clearly in the VR drill below.

Play around it: pull your mouse down-left while firing. The good news: the first shot of each burst is the gentlest (0.75×), so a burst that starts on target stays close. The Forward Grip already trims the sideways part by 25%.

5 · It costs 650 certs for the same kill time as the free TRAC-5

Six hundred and fifty certs buys a lot of jump-jet ranks, repair-tool upgrades and suit slots. This gun does not kill faster than the free starter carbine — same 143→112 damage, same 750 RPM, same 0.48 / 0.56 / 0.64 s. It out-handles it: a 0.06° first-shot cone against 0.10°, sideways walk of 0.17° against 0.225°, and a first shot that kicks less instead of 2.35× more — but its hipfire is worse (2.0° standing against 1.5°), and it has no full-auto mode.

Play around it: buy it for the handling — the tighter cone, the gentler burst, the extra 2 rounds — and only after your jump jets (Light Assault) or repair tool (Engineer) and suit are in decent shape. And remember the swap time: 0.55 s to bring it out, so change weapons behind cover, never in the open.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Go to the VR Training area (the green terminal at any warpgate). Five minutes here beats an hour of dying in real fights.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up and to the right at about 24° — no straight-up climb here — and notice the first shot of each burst jumps the least, that 0.75× first-shot kick. That's your gun's fingerprint. Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk through the firing range with your crosshair glued to neck height on every dummy. Don't shoot until the crosshair is already on the neck — the goal is to arrive on target, not to chase it after you start firing.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with holds of 6 rounds — two trigger pulls — releasing the trigger fully between holds. If a hold goes wide, shorten it to a single 3-round burst. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 30 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Remember: there's no extended magazine on this gun, so the 42 you have are all you get.
