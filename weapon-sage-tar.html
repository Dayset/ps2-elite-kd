First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

1 · SprintingYour cone starts at 7.0° — the widest it can ever be. Every hipfire shot widens it further (+0.10° per shot).

→

2 · Trigger held, ADS pressed mid-holdThe sight only changes the picture. The bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running.

→

3 · The sprayAccurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun. Your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and he kills you for it.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Stop — ~0.3 sLet go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint. Wait for the settle.

→

2 · Aim — ~0.15 sPress ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, your Nano-Regen is already running.

→

3 · Fire — from the aimed coneNow fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.10°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero.

The hipfire exception — under ~8 m, skip all of this

Inside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving. The TAR's hipfire cone standing still is just 1.5°, tight enough to land chest shots at arm's length, and you stay at full speed instead of slowing to 0.75×. Stopping is optional too: strafe past him while you fire. The disaster above is a mid-range problem — close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 143 damage up close, fading to 100 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising: 7 chest shots up close, 8 to 30 m, 9 to 47 m, 10 beyond that — and headshots need just 4 out to 30 m, then 5.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and drops you to 0.75× move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The TAR has the tightest hipfire of any TR assault rifle (1.5° standing still). Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let your Nano-Regen tick through the return fire.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage, 800 RPM, and the fastest close-range time to kill of any starter or near-starter rifle. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in under a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; at 0.75× you're faster than almost anyone else aiming back at you.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 112 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil drifts up and to the right (17–20°), so pull your mouse gently down and to the left. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 100 damage you need 10 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover, or go revive a teammate instead. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The TAR has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Stop, aim, then fire

    The section 01 rhythm, every time: release sprint, let the sight come up, then pull the trigger. Firing from a sprint at a 7.0° cone is the single most common way new TAR players lose duels they should have won — and the fix costs nothing.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.97 s; an empty gun takes 4.11 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after one kill or ~30 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: at 0.75× ADS speed you're the fastest strafing rifleman on the field, and a moving medic is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Pick fights inside 30 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and fire rate all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. And remember what you are: a Combat Medic. A dead medic revives nobody — revive from cover, heal between pushes, and let your squad's fire cover your reloads.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the TAR pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · Damage falls off a cliff past 30 m

Full power ends at 10 m; by 60 m you're at 100 damage and need 10 chest shots. The T1 Cycler keeps 112 damage out to 80 m — at long range it out-kills you outright (0.64 s to your 0.68 s).

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a medic — the front line, and the revive, are your address.

2 · Only 40 rounds — and no extended magazine exists

At 800 RPM a held trigger empties the magazine in 3 seconds flat, and there is no extended-magazine option on this gun at all. The T1 Cycler carries the same 40, but fires slower; the NC1 and Pulsar carry 30 and still last longer per trigger pull.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Five-round bursts mean eight kills' worth of trigger pulls per magazine if you land them.

3 · The empty reload is 4.11 s of defencelessness

One of the longer empty reloads among assault rifles. Run dry in the open and you're a spectator for four seconds.

Play around it: reload at 30 rounds, behind cover, every time. The 2.97 s short reload is still slow — make it a habit, not a decision.

4 · The first shot kicks hard, then it walks sideways

First-shot recoil is ×3.0 — a 0.9° jump before the burst even starts — and horizontal recoil is ±0.297° per shot with a tolerance of 1.0, so the gun wanders left and right as the burst goes on. The recoil angle is 17–20° to the right.

Play around it: pull your mouse down-left while firing, in the opposite direction of the climb. The Forward Grip trims the sideways part by 25%, and the Angled Forward Grip tames that first-shot jump by 60%.

5 · Hipfire bloom grows fast, and sprinting maxes the cone

Hipfire bloom is 0.10° per shot — double the aimed bloom — and a sprint starts you at the 7.0° maximum. The gun is a hipfire star only when you respect the setup: stopped or strafing, close range, short bursts.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m (section 01's exception), ADS beyond it, and never fire from a sprint. The Laser Sight + Short Barrel build exists for players who live in the hipfire band.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up-right — that's your gun's fingerprint, with a hard first-shot jump (×3.0). Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 20–30 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — and a magazine that lasts four kills, not one.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending. Try it again with the Laser Sight build when you buy it.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 30 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Never let the counter hit zero.
