01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.12° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — do it behind cover, or while strafing: on this gun, moving costs no accuracy at all (section 04).

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.00°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The NSX Tanto has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun. And this gun breaks the normal rule in one spectacular way: its starting cone is 0.00° in every stance, hip or aimed, still or moving. The only carbine in the game that can say that.

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.06° per shot aimed, 0.12° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. 08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire — and on this gun bloom is the whole game, because the starting cone is already perfect. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets to a perfect dot and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close and at mid-range versus 7–8 to the chest, in 0.30 s instead of 0.60–0.70 s. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Strafe while you shoot

    Most guns punish moving while aiming. This one doesn't: you keep 75% of your move speed while scoped, and your cone starts at 0.00° whether you stand still or strafe. Tap A/D while firing — a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back, and on this gun the movement costs you nothing.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.3 s; an empty gun takes 2.75 s — nearly half a second longer, an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after one or two kills or ~20 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then swap fast

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. And remember your swap speed: this gun equips in 0.5 s, the fastest carbine swap there is — returning to the Tanto from your sidearm takes only 0.75 s (0.25 s to put the pistol away + 0.5 s to bring the Tanto up), so finish a wounded enemy with the pistol instead of reloading in the open.

  6. Pick fights at 8–50 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and strafe speed all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. Past 50 m, tap-fire at exposed targets only — never hold the trigger out there. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Tanto pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It loses the pure DPS race up close

600 RPM at 143 damage is a slow kill on paper: 0.60 s for a chest kill up close, where faster carbines do it in 0.55 s or less. The same-family NS-11C fires the same 143-damage bullet at 652 RPM, so its chest kill up close is 0.55 s against your 0.60 s — the Tanto answers with a 0° first-shot cone and 0.75× ADS move, not raw damage.

Play around it: don't trade fairly. Aim for the neck, use corners, and let your first-shot accuracy do what their fire rate does — 4 headshots in 0.30 s beats any chest-shot trade.

2 · Bloom punishes holding the trigger

Hip bloom is a steep 0.12° per shot — double the aimed bloom — so a 10-round hip burst already reaches a 1.2° cone (0.63 m wide at 30 m), and a full 30-round dump reaches 3.6°. The perfect first shot is a trap if it makes you hold the trigger: the cone you build is the cone you die by.

Play around it: bursts of 5, release, repeat (habit 1) — and the Short Barrel if you build for hipfire.

3 · The magazine is small

30 rounds at 7–9 shots per kill is three or four kills per magazine — then a 2.3 s reload, 2.75 s if you emptied it. Spray at two enemies and the second one kills you while you're reloading.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 1), early reloads (habit 4), and the Extended Magazine — 36 rounds is five close-range kills per magazine instead of four.

4 · Vertical recoil is real

0.4° of climb per shot, with a 1.4× first-shot multiplier — the opening round jumps 0.56° — adds up to 4° of climb per second of fire, drifting up and slightly right (5–7°). And there is no Compensator for this gun, so nothing in the shop flattens the climb for you.

Play around it: start each burst a touch low and let the climb walk into the target, or fit the Angled Forward Grip — it cuts that first-shot kick by 60%.

5 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 60 m you're at 112 damage: 9 chest shots, 13 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only — your first shot is still a perfect dot out here, which most carbines can't say — and spend the rest of the time closing distance. High Velocity Ammunition helps the bullet get there faster and holds minimum damage to 80 m.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up and slightly right — that's your gun's fingerprint, and the first shot jumps hardest (1.4×). Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — even though your cone starts at 0.00°, bloom still moves the later shots.

  • Strafe Shooting 3 minutes · movement

    At 15–25 m, hold ADS and strafe side to side (A/D) while firing bursts of 5 at a dummy. On most guns this is a trade-off; on this one the cone stays 0.00° while you move and you keep 75% speed. Learn to trust it until strafing while shooting feels automatic.

  • Hipfire Dot 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy 5–15 m away and hipfire it down in bursts of 5 without stopping or scoping in. Watch the first shot land exactly on the dot every time — then feel where the ring starts to grow, and release before it does. This is your point-blank survival skill.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 2.3 s short reload against a 2.75 s empty one, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
