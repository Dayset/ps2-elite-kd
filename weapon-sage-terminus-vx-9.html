01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.10° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, you weren't going to win the fight in the first 0.15 s anyway.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.10°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The hipfire exception — under ~8 m, skip all of this Inside about 8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving. The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 143 damage up close, fading to 112 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.23 s, 7 chest shots ≈ 0.47 s up close. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 10 m, then a steady slide to 125 at 39 m and 112 at 60 m. The gold line shows Soft Point Ammunition, which stretches full power to 15 m.

Rule of thumbInside 10 m you are at maximum power. Past 40 m every kill takes one or two extra bullets — close the distance or burst carefully instead of spraying.

No bullet drop. Vanu weapons don't drop under gravity — your rounds fly flat, so aim dead-on at any range. The problem at 50 m+ is damage and bloom, never holdover. (TR and NC rifles have to aim slightly above distant targets; you never do.)

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The Terminus has good hip accuracy for an assault rifle. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let your Nano-Regen Device top you up after.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage, mild recoil, fast handling. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in about a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 125 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil drifts up and slightly to the right (5–6°), so pull your mouse gently down and to the left. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots. And remember: no bullet drop, so hold dead-on — never above the target.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 112 damage you need 9 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The Terminus has a semi-auto mode on the B key, but you'll do nearly all of your fighting in full-auto — "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.05° per shot aimed, 0.10° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot — the cone grows from shot 1, there are no free shots — so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short bursts also leave you rounds for the second enemy — remember, the magazine is only 30.

No zoom. The target at 35 m is small but the view is wide open — you see the crate, the tower, and the next enemy coming. Fast to aim, nothing blocks your edges. Best inside ~40 m.

VHO Reflex — 2x

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  2. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire — the cone grows from shot 1, there are no free shots. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  3. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 1.95 s; an empty gun takes 2.65 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after one kill or ~15 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  4. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving Medic is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  5. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and fire rate all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

  6. Clear the room first, revive second

    You're the Medic — your squad keeps you alive because you bring them back. But you can't revive anyone while you're dead. Win the fight with the habits above, then get the Medical Applicator out. Reviving mid-firefight is how a Medic dies standing over a body.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Terminus VX-9 pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It costs 1,000 certs — the free Pulsar is close behind

The Pulsar VS1 is auto-granted and only 48 ms slower up close (0.52 s to your 0.47 s). You paid 1,000 certs for the fastest TTK in the starter class, a better damage falloff, and faster handling — real gains, but know what they cost.

Play around it: if you haven't bought the Terminus yet, try the Pulsar first — same class, same habits. The Terminus is a refinement, not a revelation.

2 · Only 30 rounds — and no extended magazine exists

Seven to nine shots per kill means three or four kills per magazine at perfect accuracy — fewer in real fights. And unlike the starter rifles, there is no Extended Magazine attachment for this gun at all.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 2) and early reloads (habit 3). Your 1.95 s reload is quick — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

3 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 60 m you're at 112 damage: 9 chest shots, 13 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground. (No bullet drop helps your aim out there — it doesn't help your damage.)

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a Medic — the front line, next to your squad, is your address.

4 · The first shot kicks hard — 2.5×

Your first shot in every burst recoils two and a half times as hard as the rest. In a long burst your aim walks off the target's right shoulder — you'll see this clearly in the VR drill below.

Play around it: pull your mouse down-left while firing, in the opposite direction of the climb. The Forward Grip already trims the sideways part by 25%, and the Angled Forward Grip exists specifically to tame that first-shot kick.

5 · Aiming makes you slow

Like most assault rifles, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5×) — and unlike the H-V45, you don't get the 0.75× fast-strafe trait. The gun takes 0.65 s to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your Medical Applicator out.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up and slightly right (5–6°) — that's your gun's fingerprint, with the first shot jumping hardest at 2.5×. Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — remember, the cone grows from shot 1, so the release is the whole skill.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Never let the 30-round magazine run dry in the open.
