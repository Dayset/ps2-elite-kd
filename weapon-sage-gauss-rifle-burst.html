01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened. And on a burst gun it's worse: every wasted pull is two rounds gone, and you can't hose the trigger to make up for it.

What you did — the spray

Three moments, one bad habit. Read left to right.

Read it this way: the disaster is set before the sight ever comes up. Sprinting puts your cone at 7.0°, hipfiring widens it further (+0.12° per shot), and pressing ADS does not shrink a cone that is already bloomed — the scope only gives you a precise picture. Your bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running.

The fix — three beats, in order

Same courtyard, same enemy. Three beats, in order — never two at once.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

Under ~10 m? Skip the whole dance.

Inside about 10 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving. No sight to raise, no settling to wait for, and stopping is optional: the hipfire ring at 10 m is only 0.44 m wide standing still, so it covers his chest. The three-beat fix is a mid-range habit; up close, your movement is your defence.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.12° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and on a burst gun, those wasted pairs are a quarter of your magazine's killing power gone in one mistake.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, your Nano-Regen Device (F) is already healing you through it.

3 · Trigger. Now fire — in pairs. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.0125°, your first two-round burst lands where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~10 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 167 damage up close, fading to 125 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 3 headshots ≈ 0.20 s, 6 chest shots = 0.50 s up close. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun. And because every pull fires two rounds, a 6-shot chest kill is exactly three pulls.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 10 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 90 m. The gold line shows High Velocity Ammunition, which stretches minimum damage out to 110 m — this gun has no Soft Point Ammunition, so the gold line is your only ammo choice.

Rule of thumbInside 10 m you are at maximum power. Past 55 m every kill takes one or two extra bullets — close the distance or burst carefully instead of spraying.

0–10 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. This gun's hipfire is looser than the NC1 starter's (3.0° moving, 2.5° standing), so it only truly works this close — but inside 10 m it works. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing in pairs so you're hard to track, and let your Nano-Regen Device top you up between duels.

10–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Groups of 5 rounds — two pairs plus one.

This is where the gun earns its keep: near-full 167 damage, mild recoil, fast handling. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 3 head hits end the fight in 0.20 s. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–55 mControlled

ADS, short groups, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Groups of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 143 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a group, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil drifts up and to the right (23–24°), so pull your mouse gently down and to the left. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

55 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Single pulls (two rounds each) at exposed targets only.

At 125 damage you need 8 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover — and as a medic, your squad needs you up close anyway. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you pull the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (every shot grows that cone). The Gauss Rifle Burst has no full-auto mode at all — it fires two-round bursts, and there is no semi-auto mode either. "Firing modes" here are something you do with your finger, on top of the gun's own burst.

Stances — the cone you start with, before the first shot

Hipfire, standing2.5°Your point-blank default. The 0.35 m ring at 8 m in section 05 is this stance drawn out.

One thing about the cone that beginners get wrong: the cone grows by the bloom per shot from shot 1 — 0.06° per aimed shot, 0.12° from the hip. There is no bloom freeze and no free shots: the first shot already widens the cone for the second. A "free" window would be a lie the target dummy would expose in one magazine.

Recoil, for the record: 0.4–0.5° vertical per shot, but the first shot of each burst kicks only ×0.5 — burst guns are built this way, and it is why every group starts flat. The catch: the second shot of each pair kicks harder than the first, so a pair walks a little. Horizontal recoil is ±0.16° with a 0.28° tolerance (at most two kicks in one direction before it must come back), the angle is 23–24° to the right — up and right, not straight up — and it recovers at 18°/s after ~120 ms. Pull down-left, and the gun comes back to you between groups.

Try it: the two-round burst window

A target 30 m away, aiming down sights. Release, let the cone reset, fire again. Aim for groups of 5 rounds; 6–8 is tolerable while you're new. Ammo — one real choice (and two things that don't exist)

High Velocity Ammunition

First buy100 certs

Bullets fly 10% faster (620 → 682 m/s) and minimum damage stretches 20 m farther (90 → 110 m). Cost: full damage ends 2 m sooner (10 → 8 m). This is the gun's only ammo option — there is no Soft Point Ammunition for it, and no Extended Magazine either.

Good: nearly always — flatter, faster bullets mean less lead on moving targets at 25–55 m, and the extra 20 m of damage range helps exactly where this gun fights.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Regen on, then peek

    Your Nano-Regen Device (F key) heals you and nearby allies — pop it before a duel, not during one. You're your squad's medic: staying alive is the job, and a medic who wins her own duel gets to revive the teammate behind her too.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 kills up close versus 6 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Pairs, pause, pairs

    Every shot widens your cone of fire from shot 1. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Groups of 5 rounds are ideal — two full pairs plus one; release the trigger for a heartbeat between groups — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 1.95 s; an empty gun takes 2.6 s — an eternity mid-fight, and there is no extended magazine to save you. Count roughly: after one kill or ~20 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, strafe between groups: a moving medic is dramatically harder to headshot back, and your 0.5× aimed move speed is slow enough already.

  6. Fight near your squad, inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy, heal beam and revive tool all shine at close-mid range. Don't chase a kill away from the people you keep alive — the revive you miss costs more than the kill you gain. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Gauss Rifle Burst pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It loses the pure DPS race up close

600 RPM is unhurried. A faster-firing 750 RPM rifle adds up chest shots faster than yours in a face-to-face trade — 480 ms to your 500 ms.

Play around it: don't trade fairly. Regen first, aim for the neck, and use corners — your per-bullet damage and accuracy win fights that start on your terms, and one headshot erases that whole 20 ms edge.

2 · Only 30 rounds — and no extended magazine exists

At perfect accuracy that's five kills per magazine; at real accuracy it's barely two. A 100-round LMG can suppress a doorway for a minute. You cannot — and unlike the NC1, there is no Extended Magazine to buy your way out of it.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Your 1.95 s short reload is fast for a rifle — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

3 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 90 m you're at 125 damage: 8 chest shots, 12 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a medic — the front line, next to your squad, is your address.

4 · The recoil pulls up-right — and the second shot of each pair kicks harder

Its recoil angle is 23–24° rightward, and the first shot of a burst is gentle (×0.5) — then the second shot of the pair kicks harder. In a long hold-down your aim walks off the target's right shoulder, two rounds at a time — you'll see this clearly in the VR drill below.

Play around it: pull your mouse down-left while firing, in the opposite direction of the climb. The Forward Grip already trims the sideways part by 25%, and short groups mean every pair starts from that gentle first shot.

5 · No full-auto — panic fights are its worst day

An enemy rounds a corner at 3 metres and your finger does the panicked thing: hold the trigger. A 750 RPM automatic just keeps firing; your gun fires two rounds and waits for you to pull again. In a pure button-mash, you lose.

Play around it: don't take fair point-blank trades. Hipfire in pairs while strafing, and let the 3.0° cone do the work — or break line of sight and re-engage at 15 m, where the burst gun is king.

6 · Aiming makes you slow — and you're a priority target

Like all assault rifles, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5x), and the gun takes 0.65 s to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your Medical Applicator out. And every enemy knows a medic keeps the squad alive, so you get shot first.

Play around it: hipfire inside 10 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), swap weapons behind cover, never in the open — and never stand still in the open, reviving or shooting.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up-right at 23–24° — and notice how the first shot of each pair barely moves, then the second jumps. Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with groups of 5 — two pairs plus one — releasing the trigger fully between groups. If a group goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round, and a feel for the re-pull timing that runs the gun at 600 RPM.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down in pairs without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement and Nano-Regen do the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. With a 30-round magazine and no extended magazine, the habit matters more than on any other rifle.
