First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.15° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, a quarter-second of patience is cheaper than a magazine of misses.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.3°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 200 damage up close, fading to 143 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 3 headshots ≈ 0.46 s, 5 chest shots ≈ 0.92 s up close. Headshots save 50% of the kill time at close range — and with a 200 ms first-shot delay added, the close chest kill takes 1.12 s against 0.66 s for headshots, still a 41% saving. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 10 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 50 m. The gold line would show an ammo option — this gun has none, so there is only the one line.

Rule of thumbInside 10 m you are at maximum power. Past 50 m every kill takes one or two extra bullets — close the distance or fire carefully instead of spraying.

0–10 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and drops your speed to 0.75× — at arm's length that gets you killed. Pistols have the tightest hipfire of any weapon class. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track.

10–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident fire

Aim: neck / upper chest. Tap on cadence — one round per 230 ms.

This is where the gun earns its keep: 200 damage at 10 m, still 178.62 at 25 m. Start your crosshair at the neck — the straight-up recoil walks the next tap into the head. Strafe while scoped; at 0.75× you move faster aimed than a scoped Heavy, and a moving target still beats a still one.

25–40 mControlled

ADS, slow taps, let it settle

Aim: chest. Slow taps, one round per 230 ms, with a real pause.

Damage is sliding toward 143 and your cone of fire blooms if you tap too fast. Tap, let the cone settle, tap again. Recoil kicks straight up, vertical 0.8 — firm but honest, and it settles between taps. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

40 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps at exposed targets only.

At 143 damage you need 7 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The U-150 Recall has no fire-mode switch — semi-automatic only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Tap on cadence — 230 ms is the rhythm

    The Recall fires as fast as you click, but no faster than one round per 230 ms. Clicking faster wastes clicks; clicking slower wastes time. Find the rhythm in the trainer in section 04 and make it muscle memory.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 to kill up close versus 5 to the chest, in 0.46 s instead of 0.92 s. Aim at the neck and let the straight-up recoil carry the next tap into the skull.

  3. Use the magazine — it is the biggest here

    Twenty rounds is four kills' worth of ammo if you don't miss. You can afford to tap twice at a second target instead of reloading, and to pre-fire a corner. Spend the ammo; that is what it is for.

  4. Swap, don't reload, mid-fight

    Your primary's reload is the Recall's job. Swapping to the pistol takes about 0.25 s; reloading the primary takes seconds. When the rifle clicks dry, the Recall ends the fight.

  5. Reload early — the penalty is real, the habit is not

    Reloading with rounds left takes 1.8 s; an empty gun takes 2.15 s. Twenty rounds means you reload less often than any other semi-auto pistol — but when you do reload, do it behind cover.

  6. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: your aimed cone is 0.30° standing still and 0.30° strafing — moving costs you nothing in cone size on this gun, so strafe from the first shot.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the U-150 Recall pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · The slowest semi-auto trigger here

261 RPM is the slowest semi-auto rate in this set — a missed tap costs 230 ms, and the kill stretches to 1.38 s at 40 m.

Play around it: Play around it: Make the first taps count. Stop, aim, then tap — the Recall rewards a settled first shot more than any other semi-auto here.

2 · Bloom is heavy for a tap-fire gun

Hip bloom is 0.15° per shot and aimed bloom 0.15° — the highest aimed bloom of any pistol here. Tap too fast and the cone grows faster than your aim can follow.

Play around it: Play around it: Tap on cadence and let the cone settle between taps. If the group walks, slow down — the gun is telling you the rhythm.

3 · It is a jack of all trades

Nothing about the Recall is the best in its class: not the fastest kill, not the hardest hit, not the tightest cone. It wins by never being bad.

Play around it: Play around it: Lean on the magazine and the range. Twenty rounds and 143 damage at 50 m are the edges — use them, and don't ask the gun to be a Pilot.

4 · The long reload punishes a dry magazine

An empty Recall takes 2.15 s to reload — and with 20 rounds, running dry usually means you fired at two enemies and missed too much.

Play around it: Play around it: Reload after every kill, behind cover. The magazine is big enough that topping up early costs you nothing.

5 · No ammo attachment to fix the falloff

There is no ammunition option on this gun — no soft point, no high velocity. The damage curve is what it is.

Play around it: Play around it: The curve is already generous — 143 damage at 50 m. Fight at 10–25 m, where you are at 179–200 damage, and let the range take care of itself.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • Cadence Trainer 4 minutes · tap rhythm

    Use the tap trainer in section 04. Click once per round at the 230 ms rhythm — not faster, not slower. Goal: ten taps in a row inside the on-cadence window.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 15 m away and empty the magazine one tap at a time, without touching the mouse between taps. Watch the pattern climb straight up — that is your gun's fingerprint.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while tapping it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Swap Drill 3 minutes · habit

    Empty your primary into a dummy, swap to the Recall, and finish the dummy with the pistol. Repeat until the swap is automatic — in real fights you will do it without thinking, which is the point.
