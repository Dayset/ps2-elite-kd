First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.2° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

The IA makes this worse in one specific way: every pull fires two pellets, and both pellets share the same bloomed cone. A panicked pull from a sprint cone is two misses at once — and the first-shot recoil multiplier is 2×, so the second pull starts from a muzzle that has jumped twice as far as you expected.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, a quarter-second of patience is cheaper than a magazine of misses.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.2°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

IA note: your hip cone is the tightest of any pistol on this page — 0.5° standing, 0.75° moving. The gun is built for hipfire; the sight is for when you have time. And when you do fire, the next pull is ready in 333 ms.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). On this gun the exception is bigger than on any other pistol: the hip cone is so tight (0.5°) that hipfire works out to 10 m, not just 8. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many pulls
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Each pull fires two pellets of 250 damage each — 500 damage per pull up close, fading to 167 per pellet (334 per pull) at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). Count pulls, not pellets: every number in this guide is per pull, because a pull is what your finger does and what the kill time measures.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Rule of thumbThree pulls kill, everywhere: 3 chest pulls, 2 headshot pulls, 4 against a shielded Heavy (5 at 40 m). The only number that changes with range is how much of each pull actually lands — past 25 m the two pellets spread, and a pull that only lands one pellet does half the damage.

0–10 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and drops your speed to 0.75× — at arm's length that gets you killed. One pull does 500 damage: two pulls kill a fresh target, one pull to the head kills outright. Your hip cone is 0.5° — the tightest on this page — so hipfire is the plan, not the fallback. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track.

10–25 mSweet spot

ADS or hipfire — your call

Aim: neck / upper chest. One pull at a time.

Still ~364 damage per pull at 25 m, and three pulls kill anyone. The two pellets land close together at this range, so a pull is still a pull. Start your crosshair at the neck — the heavy first-shot kick climbs into the head for free damage.

25 m +Fading

ADS, slow deliberate pulls

Aim: chest. One pull, breathe, one pull.

Damage has faded to 334 per pull and the pellets are spreading apart — a pull that lands only one pellet does 167 damage, and the kill costs six pulls, not three. Fire one pull, let the sight settle, fire again. Past 30 m, close the distance or switch to your primary.

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (each pull grows that cone with every round). The IA is semi-automatic only — one click, one pull, two pellets, no fire-mode switch — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun. Tap – pause – tap: the cadence

A semi-automatic fires once per click, and no faster than its refire — one shot every 333 ms on this gun. Clicking faster does nothing; clicking slower hands the enemy time. Same 6 shots, two very different outcomes:

The target pattern is one even tap every 333 ms. Not faster — the gun cannot fire faster, and clicks inside the refire window do nothing. Not slower — every extra 100 ms of hesitation is a tenth of a second added to your kill time, and at 250 damage a round, you cannot afford to give it away. Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's upward climb carry the next shot into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  2. Tap at the cadence, not faster

    This gun fires once per click and no faster than its 333 ms refire. Clicks inside the refire window do nothing — they are wasted motion that shakes your aim. Even taps at the cadence are the whole skill; the simulator in section 04 grades exactly this.

  3. Count pulls, not pellets

    Every number on this page is per pull. Two pulls is 1,000 damage up close — a kill — but only if both pellets of both pulls land. A pull that lands one pellet is 250 damage, not 500. Say 'three pulls' in your head, never 'six shots.'

  4. Count your six pulls

    Six pulls is two kills if every pellet lands — and one kill plus a reload if they don't. Know how many pulls you've fired. After three pulls, ask yourself whether the next target is worth the remaining three or whether you should reload behind cover first.

  5. Reload early — the penalty is real, the habit is not

    Reloading with rounds left takes 2.55 s; an empty gun takes 3.55 s — a full second more. Reload early and only in cover, because two and a half seconds is a long time to stand in a doorway.

  6. Hipfire is a weapon, not a fallback

    Your standing hip cone is 0.5° — the tightest on this page — and the 0.07 m ring at 8 m fits easily inside his chest. Inside ~10 m, don't ADS at all: keep strafing and let the hipfire do the work. Past ~10 m the pellets start splitting, and that's the exact moment to switch to the sight.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the NS-357 IA pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · 500 damage per pull — but only if both pellets land

Two pulls kill up close, one pull to the head kills outright. But a pull that lands one pellet does 250 damage, and past 25 m the pellets spread wide enough to split.

Play around it: Don't trade pulls at range. Inside 10 m both pellets land in the chest — fight there, and let the MPL keep the second pull on target.

2 · The harshest damage falloff on this page

Full damage only reaches 2 m; by 30 m each pellet is down to 167. No other pistol falls off this fast — the Underboss holds 375 out to 8 m.

Play around it: Close the distance. You're a point-blank gun — the doorway and the stairwell are your address. Past 30 m, switch to your primary.

3 · First-shot recoil is doubled

First-shot recoil multiplier 2× on a 1.5° vertical kick — the muzzle jumps hard on every pull, and the second pull starts from a muzzle that has jumped twice as far as you expected.

Play around it: Buy the MPL (100 certs): it cuts hipfire recoil by 60%. And pull down firmly after every pull — the kick is the price of the damage.

4 · Six pulls is two kills — if every pellet lands

The cylinder holds six pulls: two kills if every pellet lands, one kill and a prayer if they don't. A half-landed pull is a sixth of your ammo for half the damage.

Play around it: Count your pulls after every kill, and reload early behind cover. The 2.55 s reload is quicker than the revolvers' — use that.

5 · Long range is a six-pull chore

At 40 m a half-landed pull does 167 damage, so the kill can cost six pulls and 1.67 s — against a target who is shooting back with a rifle.

Play around it: Tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're an eraser — the doorway is your address.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and fire six single pulls without touching the mouse. Watch the pairs climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint (1.5° a shot, doubled on the first). Repeat, pulling down after every pull, until the pairs stay fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Cadence Ladder 4 minutes · pull control

    At 5–10 m, kill dummies with even pulls at the 333 ms cadence — use the simulator in section 04 first, then do it live. If a pull goes wide, slow down. Goal: whole kills without a single half-landed pull.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending. Both pellets should land, every pull.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly three pulls, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Remember: 2.55 s with rounds left, 3.55 s empty.
