01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.4° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, you picked this angle before you fired.

3 · Trigger. Now fire — in bursts of 5, starting from the aimed cone of 0.10°. First shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Your bullet does 112 damage at every range — there is no falloff at all. Headshots count two-and-a-half times (×2.5), leg shots count less (×0.9). There is just one damage number to memorise:

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.24 s at any range, 9 chest shots ≈ 0.64 s. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — nine chest hits is a long trigger pull, which is why the head is where this gun lives: four head hits end it, at any range, with no falloff to worry about.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

No falloff at all — 112 damage from the muzzle to the render distance.

Rule of thumbAt every range you are at maximum power — 9 chest shots, 4 headshots, no falloff to track. The head stays at 4 from arm's length to the render distance. There is no range where this gun gets weaker — only ranges where your cone and the target's size make the shots harder.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and slows you down — at arm's length that gets you killed. Your standing hipfire cone is 3.25° — a 0.45 m ring at 8 m, just about chest-wide. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let your Cloak carry you to the next angle after the trade.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 112 damage at every range — there is no falloff to outrun — and 4 head hits end the fight in 0.24 s. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head.

25–60 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage never falls off — 112 at 60 m is the same 112 as at 10 m — but your cone blooms and the target gets smaller. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil is nearly straight up (−1°/1°), so pull your mouse gently down. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

60 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

Nine chest hits at any range is a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. The ×2.5 headshot is your long-range weapon: 4 head hits still end it. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). This rifle is full-auto only — there is no second fire mode to switch to — so “firing modes” are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun: hold, release, hold.

Stances — the cone you start with, before the first shot

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Kit it out

What to bolt
on — and what not to

Buying attachments is buying a loadout, not a shopping list. Here's every slot for this gun and whether it's actually worth the certs.

Two things to know before you spend a single cert. First, this gun comes bare — iron sights, no rail attachment, no barrel attachment. Second, two things don't exist for this gun at all: no Soft Point and no High Velocity Ammunition — the flat 112 damage has no falloff for either to stretch. Don't go looking for them. The good news: the rail slot holds a Forward Grip, an Angled Grip, a Laser Sight, a Darklight, and an Extended Magazine, and the ammo slot holds a Heavy Magazine.

The standard cost is 30 certs for optics, 100 certs for rail, barrel and ammo parts — the ones here follow that pattern unless a different price is shown.

08

Six habits

Six habits
worth keeping

Six small habits, each worth one real fight. Steal all six and you can win with any scout rifle. Keep only one or two and you'll still notice them.

  1. Cloak first, then shoot

    Your Cloak (F key) gets you to the fight — it doesn't get you through it. Firing breaks cloak, so decloak behind cover, already aimed at the doorway, and let the first burst land before the enemy knows you're there. If the fight goes bad, cloak and move: a scout rifle duels badly from the open, and your best defence is not being where the return fire lands.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal two-and-a-half times damage: 4 kill at every range versus 9 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.10 s; an empty gun takes 2.90 s. That extra 0.8 s is most of a second standing defenceless. Count roughly: after two kills or ~18 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 9 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe — while shooting

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. And remember: you move at half speed while aiming, so keep your bursts short and your strafes between them — plant your feet for the burst, move for the pause.

  6. Pick fights inside 60 metres — near your squad

    Your damage never falls off, but your cone and the target's size do the limiting — and your cloak shines at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you. And stay near friendlies: an Infiltrator alone in the open is a free kill, but one behind a squad line, bursting heads between cloaks, is the reason the line holds.

09

Know your weak spots

Where this rifle
loses — and how to live with it

Know them before someone else shows you. Every weapon here has the same five kinds of problem — a counter weapon, a counter range, a moment that leaves you naked, and a habit that gives you away. Learn the shapes.

1 · Nine chest shots is the longest trigger pull of any scout rifle

112 damage means 9 chest hits to kill — a 0.64 s perfect-accuracy kill time, the slowest chest kill of any scout rifle here. Body shots are a commitment, not a plan.

Play around it: Hunt the head: 4 headshots end it in 0.24 s at any range. The ×2.5 multiplier is the whole gun — aim at the neck and let the climb do the work.

2 · The worst hipfire bloom of the family

Hipfire bloom is 0.4° per shot — four times the automatic scouts' — and the standing hip cone is 3.25°, the widest of any scout rifle here. Hipfire goes wide fast.

Play around it: The Laser Sight (section 06) shrinks the hip cone by a third, and short hipfire bursts stay inside the chest. Past 8 m, aim down sights — the ADS cone is a tight 0.10°.

3 · It doesn't count toward Scout Rifle directives

Kills with the Tomoe count toward Nanite Systems Exports directives instead of the Scout Rifle tree — so it won't help you auraxium a scout rifle. (Kills do still count as Scout Rifle kills for Infiltrator directives.)

Play around it: Buy it for the gun, not the directive. If the auraxium is the goal, the Vandal is the NS scout that counts.

4 · Four headshots, or nothing

The ×2.5 headshot is the whole gun — and it needs all four. A 3-headshot burst leaves the enemy alive with 160 health, and the 9-shot body kill is a 0.64 s exposure.

Play around it: Burst discipline (habit 3): 5-round bursts, release, re-acquire. The 750 RPM fire rate forgives a miss — the cone doesn't.

5 · 1,000 certs for a specialist

A thousand certs buys a lot of Cloak ranks, suit slots and sidearm upgrades. This gun does not out-damage the free SR-100 bolt-action; it out-handles it at mid-range, and only if you hunt heads.

Play around it: Buy it for the flat damage — the only scout rifle that never gets weaker with range — and only after your Cloak and suit are in decent shape. And remember the swap time: 0.65 s to bring it out, so change weapons behind cover, never in the open.

10

Drills

Five drills
to keep

Do one per alert

You don't get better by reading this page. You get better by doing five specific reps, this session, until they're boring. Pick one habit from section 08, do it for a whole alert, and you'll know whether it's working. Don't try all five at once — that doesn't work.

2 minutes · recoil

The Wall Test

Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine in bursts of 5 without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb nearly straight up — and watch the very first shot jump furthest, that ×1.4 first-shot kick. That's your gun's fingerprint. Repeat, this time pulling down, until the group stays fist-sized.

3 minutes · aim height

Neck-Line Walk

Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

4 minutes · burst control

Burst Ladder

At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — and with 112 damage, a wild round costs you a ninth of the kill.

3 minutes · hipfire

Circle Strafe

Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your shield and movement do the defending. Keep the bursts short: 0.4° of hip bloom per shot goes wide fast.

3 minutes · habit

Reload Discipline

Fire exactly 15 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Remember: the Extended Magazine takes you to 24 and the Heavy Magazine to 27, but the habit is the upgrade.

Pocket rule: NSX Tomoe · 1,000 certs — NOT a starter (the NSO starter is the SR-100) · fight at 10–60 m · aim neck, burst 5, release, repeat · four headshots kill at any range — no falloff · reload early behind cover (2.1 s, never on empty) · buy the NiCO XR 2x (30) + Forward Grip (100) + Extended Magazine (100) · hipfire under 8 m · past 60 m, hunt the head or close in.
