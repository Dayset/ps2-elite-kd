01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.6° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, you picked this angle before you fired.

3 · Trigger. Now fire — one aimed tap at a time, starting from the aimed cone of 0°. First shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → tap. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

02

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 334 damage up close, fading to 280 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). There are only two damage tiers to memorise — no middle step:

RangeDamageHeadChestLegsChest vs shielded Heavy
Close · 0–15 m3342345
Far · 15–75 m+334→2802446

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 2 headshots ≈ 0.24 s at any range, 3 chest shots ≈ 0.47 s up close and 4 chest shots ≈ 0.71 s past 15 m. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun: 2 head hits end it at any distance.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 15 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 75 m.

Rule of thumbInside 15 m you are at maximum power — 3 chest shots, 2 headshots. Past 15 m every kill takes one extra chest bullet (4 instead of 3), and the head stays at 2 all the way out. Close the distance or tap carefully instead of spraying.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and slows you down — at arm's length that gets you killed. Your standing hipfire cone is 3.0° — a 0.42 m ring at 8 m, about chest-wide. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let your Cloak carry you to the next angle after the trade.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, tap-fire

Aim: neck / upper chest. Tap — one shot at a time.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 334 damage, a 0° aimed cone, and two headshots end the fight in 0.24 s. Start your crosshair at the neck — the second tap walks into the head. Strafe between taps, plant your feet during each one.

25–75 mControlled

ADS, taps, hunt the head

Aim: chest. Taps of 1–2, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 280 and your cone blooms 0.1° with every aimed shot if you rush. Tap, let the cone recover (~0.3 s), tap again. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots. The head is still 2 taps all the way out.

75 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Single taps at exposed targets only.

At 280 damage you need 4 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and tap-fire

How you stand and
how you pull the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). This rifle has no fire-mode switch — semi-auto only — so “firing modes” are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun: tap, pause, tap.

One more number worth knowing: your ADAD window is 2 aimed shots (0 from the hip). Those first shots of a tap are the ones you can fire while snapping left-right between directions — but the cone still grows 0.1° with every one of them, so they are a movement window, not free accuracy. There are no shots with no cone growth on this gun.

Tap — pause — tap: the rhythm

Every shot widens your cone of fire (“bloom”). Releasing the trigger for a heartbeat resets it. Same 6 rounds, two very different outcomes:

The target pattern is taps of 1–2 rounds — that's the ideal. Three in one pull is fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.1° per shot aimed) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. Short taps also leave you rounds for the second enemy — and with 12 in the magazine, three to four kills per mag is a realistic goal, not a dream.

RTA Reflex Sight (1x)

No zoom. The target at 35 m is small but the view is wide open — you see the crate, the tower, and the next enemy coming. Fast to aim, nothing blocks your edges. Best inside ~40 m.

MH2 Reflex Sight (2x)

Same target, same 35 m — but he looks twice as tall, so placing shots on his chest and head gets much easier. The price: your view narrows to the round window, the crate and tower are gone, and close targets feel fast and huge. Best at 25–75 m.

That's the whole 30-cert decision from section 06 in one picture: 1x for awareness, 2x for precision. Most veterans carry both and swap per base — indoors 1x, open ground 2x.

06

Kit it out

What to bolt
on — and what not to

Buying attachments is buying a loadout, not a shopping list. Here's every slot for this gun and whether it's actually worth the certs.

Two things to know before you spend a single cert. First, this gun comes bare — iron sights, no rail attachment, no barrel attachment. Second, two things don't exist for this gun at all: no Forward Grip and no Extended Magazine. Don't go looking for them — the 12-round magazine is what you get, so tap discipline (section 04) matters more here than on guns you can pad out. The good news: the rail slot holds a Comfort Grip, a Laser Sight, a Darklight, or a Weighted Receiver — and the ammo slot holds the faction's Explosive Ammunition plus a Heavy Magazine.

The standard cost is 30 certs for optics, 100 certs for rail, barrel and ammo parts — the ones here follow that pattern unless a different price is shown.

08

Six habits

Six habits
worth keeping

Six small habits, each worth one real fight. Steal all six and you can win with any scout rifle. Keep only one or two and you'll still notice them.

  1. Cloak first, then shoot

    Your Cloak (F key) gets you to the fight — it doesn't get you through it. Firing breaks cloak, so decloak behind cover, already aimed at the doorway, and let the first tap land before the enemy knows you're there. If the fight goes bad, cloak and move: a scout rifle duels badly from the open, and your best defence is not being where the return fire lands.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 2 kill at every range versus 3–4 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the second tap walk into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Tap, pause, tap

    Every shot widens your cone of fire by 0.1° while aiming. Holding the trigger past ~3 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. One or two shots per pull is the ideal; release the trigger for a heartbeat between taps — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 1.95 s; an empty gun takes 2.40 s. That extra 0.45 s is nearly half a second standing defenceless. Count roughly: after two kills or ~7 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 4 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe — between shots

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. And remember: you move at half speed while aiming, so strafe between your taps, not during them — plant your feet for the shot, move for the pause.

  6. Pick fights inside 75 metres — near your squad

    Your damage, accuracy and cloak all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you. And stay near friendlies: an Infiltrator alone in the open is a free kill, but one behind a squad line, tapping heads between cloaks, is the reason the line holds.

09

Know your weak spots

Where this rifle
loses — and how to live with it

Know them before someone else shows you. Every weapon here has the same five kinds of problem — a counter weapon, a counter range, a moment that leaves you naked, and a habit that gives you away. Learn the shapes.

1 · Only 12 rounds — and no way to buy more than 15

The Heavy Magazine takes you to 15, but there is no Extended Magazine on this gun at all — it simply doesn't exist.

Play around it: Tap discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Your 1.95 s reload is fast — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

2 · A missed tap costs a quarter-second

At 255 RPM the gun only fires every 235 ms. Miss a headshot and the next chance comes a quarter-second later — an eternity against a 652 RPM rifle.

Play around it: Slow down. One aimed tap beats two hopeful ones, and the pause between taps is where your accuracy lives.

3 · It moves at half speed while aiming

Your aimed strafe is 0.5× — every other scout rifle in this guide is the same, but the Vandal keeps 0.75×, and every automatic rifle you meet strafes faster than you do while shooting.

Play around it: Strafe between taps, not during them. The Weighted Receiver (section 06) trades even more speed for a 0° cone while moving — a lane-holding tool, not a duelling one.

4 · Long range is a tap-fest

Past 75 m you're at 280 damage: 4 chest shots, 6 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: Tap at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're an Infiltrator — the squad line is your address, and it's rarely 75 m from the enemy.

5 · It costs 1,000 certs — and it's not stronger on paper

A thousand certs buys a lot of Cloak ranks, suit slots and sidearm upgrades. This gun does not out-damage the free M77-B bolt-action; it out-handles it at mid-range. If you buy it expecting a bigger hammer, you'll feel robbed.

Play around it: Buy it for the handling — the 0° aimed cone, the two-tap headshot kill, the fast reload — and only after your Cloak and suit are in decent shape. And remember the swap time: 0.85 s to bring it out, so change weapons behind cover, never in the open.

10

Drills

Five drills
to keep

Do one per alert

You don't get better by reading this page. You get better by doing five specific reps, this session, until they're boring. Pick one habit from section 08, do it for a whole alert, and you'll know whether it's working. Don't try all five at once — that doesn't work.

2 minutes · recoil

The Wall Test

Aim at a wall 20 m away and fire single taps without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — and watch how little it moves between single taps, because the recoil is only 1.0 and it recovers before your next tap. Now hold the trigger for 6 rounds: watch the group walk. That's the bloom you're paying for when you rush.

3 minutes · aim height

Neck-Line Walk

Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

3 minutes · tap control

Tap Ladder

At 30–50 m, kill dummies with taps of 2, releasing the trigger fully between taps. If a tap goes wide, slow down. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round, at the range where most rifles start missing.

3 minutes · survival

Cloak Relocate

Fire two taps at a dummy, cloak, sprint 20 m to new cover, fire again. This is your survival skill — the one that keeps you alive after the minimap gives you away. Remember: an unsuppressed shot reveals you out to 100 m.

3 minutes · habit

Reload Discipline

Fire exactly 8 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Remember: there's no extended magazine on this gun, so the 12 you have are all you get.

Pocket rule: HSR-1 · 1,000 certs — NOT a starter (the TR starter is the M77-B) · fight at 15–75 m · aim neck, tap, pause, tap — 1–2 shots per pull · two headshots kill at any range · reload early behind cover (1.95 s, never on empty) · buy the MH2 Reflex Sight 2x (30) + Heavy Magazine (100) + Comfort Grip (100) · hipfire under 8 m · past 75 m, tap or close in.
