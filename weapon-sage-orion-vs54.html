01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.1° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and at 750 RPM, that's six wasted rounds before you even start aiming.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, your overshield (F) covers it.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.1°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The Orion's hipfire is the best of any starter LMG, so this exception is wider for you than for anyone else. The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The Orion has the best hipfire of any starter LMG (a 2.5° cone standing still), so trust it. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let the overshield soak the return fire.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage, 750 RPM, fast handling. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull straight down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 125 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (0° angle — no sideways drift at all), so pull your mouse gently down. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 112 damage you need 9 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position — and your bullets are the slowest of the starter LMGs (540 m/s), so moving targets need real lead. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

How you stand and

The Orion has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Shield first, then shoot

    Your overshield (F key) is extra health that only helps if it's on before the bullets arrive. Hear footsteps or expect a corner? Shield up, then peek. A shielded Heavy needs 11 chest shots to drop up close instead of 7 — that gap wins duels.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest, in 0.24 s instead of 0.48 s. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 3.0 s; an empty gun takes 3.44 s — nearly half a second longer, an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after two kills or ~30 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving Heavy is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and shield all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Orion pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · The first shot kicks hardest

Your first-shot recoil multiplier is 2.25× — the biggest jump of any faction starter except the XMG-100, and far above the GD-22S's gentle 1.5×. Every burst opens with a jolt, so a lazy first trigger pull throws your second shot high.

Play around it: expect the jump and pull down as the burst starts, or fit the Angled Forward Grip (section 06) — it cuts that first kick by 60%. Short bursts of 5 keep the kick from compounding.

2 · Only 50 rounds — and no way to get more

The CARV carries 100 and the XMG-100 carries 80. Your 50 vanish fast when suppressing a doorway, and no extended-magazine option exists for the Orion at all — Census lists none for this gun.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Count your kills: two enemies is roughly a magazine, so reload after the second one, behind cover.

3 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 65 m you're at 112 damage: 9 chest shots, 13 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way — and your bullets are the slowest of the faction starters at 540 m/s, so runners need real lead.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a Heavy — the front line is your address.

4 · The loosest aimed first shot of the starters

Your aimed cone starts at 0.1° — more than three times wider than the GD-22S's 0.03°. Your opening round is simply less precise than an NC player's, so a single hopeful long-range tap is a worse bet for you than it is for them.

Play around it: don't snipe with it — fight inside 40 m, where 0.1° is plenty tight, and let your 750 RPM do the talking instead of one perfect shot.

5 · Aiming makes you slow

Like all LMGs, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5x), and the gun takes 0.8 s to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your rocket launcher out.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea, and your hipfire is the best of the starters), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

GD-22S · NC

The hammer. 167 damage per bullet — the hardest-hitting starter — with the tightest first-shot cone (0.03°) and the fastest reload (2.3 s). But it fires at only 577 RPM, so it kills slower than you in every band.

How you beat it: don't let it dictate a slow, aimed duel at 40 m+, where its precision and damage shine. Close in, where your 750 RPM and 0.48 s kill time rule, and punish its misses — a missed GD-22S shot costs its owner 104 ms, more than your 80 ms.

T9 CARV · TR

Your mirror, with a drum. Identical damage, fire rate and time to kill to yours in every band — the difference is the 100-round magazine and a 5.4 s reload.

How you beat it: you can't out-shoot it bullet for bullet, so out-play it. Its hipfire is the worst here (4.0° standing vs your 2.5°), so hipfire-duel it under 10 m. And punish the reload — at 5.4 s it's defenceless for an age. Push the moment you hear it click dry.

XMG-100 · NSO starter

The robot's gun. What every Nanite Systems Operative is handed on day one: 143 damage at 714 RPM with a big 80-round magazine and hipfire tied with yours (2.5°) — but the slowest bullets of the four (500 m/s) and a long 4.5 s reload.

How you beat it: you're faster in every band — 20 ms up close, 30 ms at range — and it kicks harder per shot (2.5× first-shot recoil) with slower bullets that need more lead on runners. Punish the reload: at 4.5 s, an XMG that just emptied its drum is defenceless — push it.

Orion VS54 · you

The duellist. Tied for the fastest time to kill of any starter, the best hipfire of the faction guns, and a reload that beats the CARV's by 2.4 seconds.

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint (0° angle, no sideways drift). Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — and your hipfire is the best of the starters, so lean on it.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 30 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
