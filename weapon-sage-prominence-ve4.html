01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.06° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, use cover for it.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.10°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The Prominence has tight hipfire for a carbine (1.25° standing). Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and keep moving — a still target at this range is a dead one.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage to 10 m, a tight 0.10° aimed cone, and mild recoil. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural up-and-right climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in about a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 125 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil drifts up and to the right (14–17°), so pull your mouse gently down and to the left. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 125 damage you need 8 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The Prominence VE4 has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

Stances — the cone you start with, before the first shot

StanceStarting coneWhat it means for you
Sprinting7.0°Maxed out — the widest the cone can be. This is the section 01 disaster: never start firing from a sprint.
Hipfire, moving1.75°Walking or strafing from the hip. Fine inside ~8 m, leaks rounds past the target beyond it.
Hipfire, standing1.25°Your point-blank default. The 0.17 m ring at 8 m in section 05 is this stance drawn out.
Hipfire, crouched0.75°The tightest hipfire you have (1.25° if you crouch-walk). Crouch when holding a doorway from the hip.
Aimed (ADS), feet planted0.10°Standing or crouched makes no difference here — about twelve times tighter than hipfire. This is why section 01's order matters.
Aimed (ADS), strafing0.25°Two and a half times the planted cone, and you move at half speed (0.5×). Strafe between bursts to stay hard to hit; plant your feet during each burst.

The strafe window. Standing still and aimed, your cone starts at 0.10° and grows 0.05° with every shot — so after 1 shot it sits at 0.15°, after 2 at 0.20°, and after 3 it has reached the 0.25° a strafing shot starts at. That 3-shot window is the ADAD window: it is not a freeze, the cone grows from the very first shot. From the hip the window is wider — 8 shots (1.25° to 1.75° at 0.06° per shot) — because the hip cone has further to climb.

06

Attachments: what to buy, what to skip

Every slot, judged
for a new player

The Prominence VE4 costs 1,000 certs to unlock, and it arrives bare — iron sights, no rail, no barrel, standard ammo. That's fine: the gun is already good. Everything below is an optional sidegrade, not an upgrade. Buy in the order shown and stop whenever the gun feels right.

If you already own the free Solstice VE3, know what the 1,000 certs buy: the same 143 damage at 652 RPM instead of 698, with an underbarrel rail the starter can't take — a toolbelt, not a damage upgrade.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats attachments

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's up-and-right climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  2. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double. And resist the urge to hold the trigger: the magazine is only 30 rounds, and a long spray is still a spray.

  3. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 1.9 s; an empty gun takes longer — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after one kill or ~15 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  4. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  5. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and handling all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

  6. Let the underbarrel do the awkward jobs

    Grenade the doorway before you push, shotgun the corner panic, smoke the revive — that's what the rail is for. Switch with the Gear Slot 1 key, and remember the rail holds one tool at a time: pick the job before the fight, not during it.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them, play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Prominence VE4 pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · The 7-shot kill dies at 10 metres

Full damage ends at 10 m, and the 7-shot chest kill ends at about 10.4 m — one step further out and every kill takes 8 shots. Play around it: fight inside 10 m when you can, or accept the extra bullet and burst cleanly — the head still only takes 4.

2 · The first shot kicks hardest

The first round of every burst jumps with a 2.3× multiplier, climbing up and to the right before the burst even settles. Yank the trigger and your opening shot climbs off the head. Play around it: start each burst a touch low and left and let the climb walk into the target, or fit the Angled Forward Grip — it cuts that first-shot kick by 60%.

3 · The magazine is only 30 rounds

Two clean kills per magazine if you burst well, one if you spray — and the Extended Magazine only buys you 5 more. A second enemy rounding the corner while you're dry is the classic Prominence death. Play around it: burst discipline (habit 2) and early reloads (habit 3) — and count your rounds out loud until it's automatic.

4 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 60 m you're at 125 damage: 8 chest shots, 12 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground. Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. At 50 m your time to kill is already 0.64 s — range is where this gun stops winning.

5 · Aiming makes you slow and wide

While strafing aimed, your cone opens to 0.25° — two and a half times the planted 0.10° — and you move at half speed. A scoped strafe is a slow, wide target. Play around it: plant your feet during each burst and strafe between them, and hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea).

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test2 minutes · recoil
    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up and to the right — that's your gun's fingerprint, and the first shot jumps hardest (2.3×). Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.
  • Neck-Line Walk3 minutes · aim height
    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.
  • Burst Ladder4 minutes · burst control
    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — even though your aimed cone caps at 3.0°, bloom still moves the later shots.
  • Circle Strafe3 minutes · hipfire
    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.
  • Reload Discipline3 minutes · habit
    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 1.9 s reload and a 30-round magazine, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
