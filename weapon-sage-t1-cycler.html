First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

Three moments, one bad habit. Read left to right.

Read it this way: the disaster is set before the sight ever comes up. Sprinting puts your cone at 7.0°, hipfiring widens it further (+0.10° per shot), and pressing ADS does not shrink a cone that is already bloomed — the scope only gives you a precise picture. Your bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running.

The fix — three beats, in order

Same courtyard, same enemy. Three beats, in order — never two at once.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

Under ~8 m? Skip the whole dance.

Inside about 8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving. No sight to raise, no settling to wait for, and stopping is optional: the hipfire ring at 8 m is only 0.28 m wide standing still, so it covers his chest. The three-beat fix is a mid-range habit; up close, your movement is your defence.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.10° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — take it behind cover if you can. A Medic has no overshield to hide behind, so the cover does that job instead.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.10°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The T1 Cycler has good hip accuracy for an assault rifle. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let your 750 RPM do the talking.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage, fast handling, 750 RPM. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in under a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull straight down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 125 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs straight up (0° angle, no sideways drift), so pull your mouse gently down. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 112 damage you need 9 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too — and a Medic who arrives alive revives the squad.

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The T1 Cycler has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). No zoom. The target at 35 m is small but the view is wide open — you see the crate, the tower, and the next enemy coming. Fast to aim, nothing blocks your edges. Best inside ~40 m.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Heal between fights, not during them

    Your Nano-Regen Device (F key) restores health over seconds — it will not out-heal incoming bullets. Use it after a kill, behind cover, while you reload. And remember what comes after the shooting stops: the revive. A Medic who survives the fight revives the squad; a dead hero revives nobody.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.755 s; an empty gun takes 3.55 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after a kill or ~25 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving Medic is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and fire rate all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the T1 Cycler pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · No damage edge — the middle of the road

143 damage is the baseline every other starter is measured against, not an advantage. The NC1 Gauss Rifle hits for 167 a bullet: it needs one fewer chest shot up close (6 to your 7) and kills in 3 headshots to your 4.

Play around it: don't trade single shots — trade bursts. Your 750 RPM and 40-round magazine win the longer fight; the Gauss Rifle's 600 RPM and 30-round magazine don't.

2 · The first shot jumps

The first round of every burst kicks 2.5× as hard as the rest — enough to throw your second round high if you don't expect it. At 750 RPM that jump comes fast, and it comes at the start of every burst you fire.

Play around it: pull down as the burst starts, not after — or fit the Angled Forward Grip, which cuts first-shot kick by 60%. Neck-height aim absorbs the rest: a high first shot still lands on the head.

3 · Only 40 rounds — and no way to buy more

There is no extended magazine for this gun at all. At 750 RPM a held trigger empties the magazine in 3.2 seconds, and a mag dump plus a reload is the easiest way to die to the second enemy in the room.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Your 2.755 s reload with rounds left is quick — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

4 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 80 m you're at 112 damage: 9 chest shots, 13 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Snipers and battle rifles own that ground.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a Medic — the front line, next to your squad, is your address.

5 · Aiming makes you slow

Like all assault rifles, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5×), and the gun takes 650 ms to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your medical applicator out.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

Pulsar VS1 · VS

The duellist. Same damage as you, 698 RPM, the gentlest vertical recoil of the four (0.22), the fastest reload (1.75 s), and the fastest bullets (615 m/s).

How you beat it: your edge is thin — 750 vs 698 RPM — so win it on the magazine: 40 rounds to its 30 means you finish the second enemy while it's reloading. Its first-shot kick (2.75×) is harsher than yours, so its bursts open worse than yours do.

AR-100 · NSO starter

The robot's rifle. What every Nanite Systems Operative is handed on day one: 143 damage at 667 RPM with the tightest horizontal walk here (0.2°) and full damage out to 15 m — but the slowest bullets of the four (550 m/s) and the slowest time to kill in every band.

How you beat it: it needs the same shots as you in every band (7/8/9) but fires them slower — your time-to-kill edge is 60–80 ms everywhere. Its slow bullets need more lead on runners, so keep moving and it keeps missing.

Rule of thumbOn paper you kill 4–13% faster than the other starters, shot for shot. In a real duel, after reaction and ping, it's 3–10% — and one headshot from you (0.24 s for four, up close) beats every number on this page. Fight on your terms: neck-height aim, inside 40 m, and reload before the magazine runs dry.

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint: 0.27 vertical, no sideways drift. Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5–8, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 25 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.

Pocket summary — screenshot this T1 Cycler · fight at 10–40 m · aim neck, burst ~5, release, repeat · heal between fights, revive after the shooting stops · reload early behind cover (never on empty) · buy the 2x (5 certs) and Soft Point (100) · hipfire under 8 m · past 50 m, tap or close in.
