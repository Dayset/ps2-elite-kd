The NSO bolt-action — 600 damage, a 1.4-second rechamber, and a one-shot headshot kill to 276 m. The slowest gun here, and the one that ends fights with a single click.

Read this first — this gun is NOT a starter. The ADVX // Mako costs 1,000 certs (or 699 Daybreak Cash). Your NSO starter sniper rifle is the semi-automatic SR-100 — the Mako is a purchase you make later, because you want the one-shot headshot, not a gun you start with.

01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — clicking as fast as your finger allows while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still working. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+1.0° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes 0.3 s with a 7x scope (0.2 s with a 4x). Trigger working through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti — and on this gun, with only 6 rounds and 1.4 s between shots, two wasted rounds are nearly half your magazine gone for nothing.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine. You have a cloak, not an overshield, to hide behind — and the cloak won't save a shot you already wasted. A clean first shot is worth more than a rushed spray.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0°, your first shot lands where the dot points — and the 1.4 s rechamber gives the cone time to reset fully before the next one.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.
02

Know your damage

How many bullets to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 600 damage up close, fading to 400 at long range — ADVX // Mako damage. Headshots count ×2.25, leg shots count less (×0.9). Sniper rifles use a separate Sniper Rifles resistance type: body and leg shots are reduced against Nanoweave wearers (about 20% — implied by the wiki's effectiveness tables), so the chest count rises in the far bands. Headshots ignore it.

Perfect-accuracy times: 1 headshot = 0 ms to 276 m; 2 headshots = 1,570 ms beyond it; 2 body shots = 1,570 ms to 183 m, 3 body shots = 3,140 ms beyond it. 1 headshot + 1 body shot = 2 shots = 1,570 ms at 350 m (900 + 400 = 1,300). Against a shielded Heavy Assault (1,450 health): 2 headshots at any range, or 3 chest shots up close and 4 at range. In real fights you will land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun: one clean headshot ends it, and no semi-auto can do that.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 15 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 350 m. The headshot count is what matters — see the table above.

Rule of thumbInside 276 m one headshot kills — the longest one-shot headshot range of any NSO rifle, and longer than the starters' 246 m. Past 276 m every kill takes a second shot (1,570 ms), and the chest goes from 2 shots to 3 at 183 m. That is the gun's signature: the headshot is the whole kill, and the rechamber is the whole price.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (how you work the trigger). The ADVX // Mako is a bolt-action sniper rifle, so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger and the bolt — not a setting on the gun. And with a 6x scope or higher, a third thing matters: scope sway — the crosshair drifts, more the higher you zoom. Hold Breath (Shift by default) almost completely suppresses it; the Target Focus implant extends how long you can hold it.

Fire – rechamber – re-acquire: the bolt rhythm

Every shot costs 1.4 s of rechambering. The cone resets fully while you work the bolt — the skill is putting the crosshair back on the target, not managing bloom. Same 2 shots, two very different outcomes:

The target pattern is one aimed shot, a full rechamber, and one more aimed shot. The cone resets while you work the bolt, so the extra bullets don't punish you — the lost aim does. The rechamber kicks you out of the scope on this gun (no straight-pull bolt), so use the 1.4 s to find the target again instead of firing blind. Two aimed shots beat four rushed ones — and your magazine is only 6 rounds.

7x scope — 7x

At 150 m through the 7x scope, the target is big enough to place a headshot — and you still see the ground around him. Fast to aim, nothing blocks your edges. Scope-in is 0.3 s; hold your breath (Shift) for the shot.

12x scope — 12x

Same target, same 150 m — but through the 12x scope he looks nearly twice as tall again, so placing shots on his head gets much easier. The price: your view narrows to the round window, the world outside the glass is gone, and the sway looks twice as big. Best past 100 m.

That's the whole 30-cert decision from section 06 in one picture: 7x for the fight you are in, 12x for the shot you are planning. Most veterans carry both and swap per base — 7x for open ground, 12x for the long lane.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats attachments

The gun is fine — your habits decide the fights. Six things to do on every engagement, in order.

  1. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal 2.25× damage: 1 kills to 276 m versus 2–3 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gentle recoil climb into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free. At 0 ms for one head, no semi-auto out-kills you anywhere inside 276 m.

  2. One shot, rechamber, re-acquire

    The cycle is 1.57 s: 1.4 s of chambering, 0.17 s of refire. Use the rechamber to re-aim, not to admire the hit. The cone resets fully while you work the bolt, so every shot starts at 0.00° — the skill is putting the crosshair back on the target, not managing bloom.

  3. Hold your breath for the shot

    Rifles with 7x scopes and above have scope sway, and higher magnification magnifies it. Hold Shift to almost completely suppress the sway — and release Shift while rechambering, so one breath stretches across several shots. Without the Target Focus implant your breath is short; make the shot count.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.5 s; an empty gun takes 3.9 s. That extra 1.4 s is nearly a second and a half standing defenceless. Count roughly: after three kills or ~5 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last round as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then peek — don't strafe-duel

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. And you move at 0.5× while aiming, same as everyone else — so peek, fire your one shot, and break line of sight instead of standing in the open.

  6. Cloak is your rechamber

    You're an Infiltrator, and the cloak is your escape — but a cloaked Infiltrator still has to rechamber. When you fire, cloak during the 1.4 s rechamber, reposition, and reappear somewhere else. The Mako's cycle is an eternity in the open; behind a cloak it is free. Never fire twice from the same window.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them, play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the ADVX // Mako pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · 1.57 s between shots — a miss is a lost duel

1.4 s of chambering plus 0.17 s of refire. A missed first shot costs you 1.57 s of kill time, and two misses in a duel hand it to the enemy. Against a semi-auto that fires every 300 ms, you get one chance per duel.

Play around it: hold your breath and re-acquire properly (habits 2 and 3). This gun forgives a bad position; it does not forgive a rushed shot.

2 · Only 6 rounds — and a 3.9 s empty reload

Six rounds is three kills (two body shots each) and a spare. A sprayed pair of shots can leave you dry mid-fight, and the empty reload is the longest of any NSO rifle.

Play around it: reload early and only in cover (habit 4). Your 2.5 s short reload is still slow — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

3 · No straight-pull bolt — the rechamber kicks you out of the scope

Every other bolt-action here can equip a straight-pull bolt and stay scoped while rechambering. The Mako cannot — the attachment table says no. Every shot costs you the scope picture, and you have to find the target again.

Play around it: re-acquire after every shot (habit 2), and cloak during the rechamber (habit 6). The 1.4 s is free time if you use it to move.

4 · It never one-shots past 276 m

Two headshots, 1,570 ms apart, past 276 m — the slowest follow-up here. Every semi-auto here fires its second headshot in 275–451 ms. Your edge is the first shot, not the second.

Play around it: don't take the 276 m+ shot at a moving target. The first shot has to land, or the duel is already lost.

5 · Hipfire is its worst fight

5.0° standing and 6.0° moving from the hip — wide for a sniper rifle, and the bloom is 1.0° per shot. A hipfire duel under 10 m against a fast-firing rifle is a fight you're built to lose.

Play around it: keep your distance (habit 5), ADS even at 10 m, and if someone does close on you, backpedal while hipfiring rather than standing still — or cloak and reposition.

6 · It costs 1,000 certs — and the SR-100 is free

A thousand certs buys a lot of Infiltrator tool ranks, suit slots and abilities. What you're buying over the free SR-100 is the one-shot headshot to 276 m — and a 1.57 s cycle that punishes every miss.

Play around it: buy it for the headshot — the 0 ms one-shot kill inside 276 m — and only after your cloak and suit are in decent shape. If you mostly fight inside 100 m, the free SR-100 is the better buy.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area ~15 minutes a session

Five drills, all in the VR Training room — no certs, no enemies, no pressure. About 15 minutes a session; do one set, then go fight.

  • Rechamber Rhythm 4 minutes · the 1.57 s cycle

    At 50–100 m, kill dummies with one shot, a full rechamber, and one more shot — no rushing. Goal: every second shot lands, because you re-acquired instead of firing blind. Count the cycle out loud: shot, one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand, shot. The rechamber is the drill.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb gently — that's your gun's fingerprint: a 0° angle, 0.4° per shot, the gentlest climb here. Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own. One headshot is the whole gun — make the neck your default.

  • Hold-Breath Drill 3 minutes · sway

    At 150 m with the 7x scope, hold Shift, shoot, release Shift while rechambering, hold Shift again for the next shot. Train the breath cycle until it is one motion: breathe out, hold, shoot, breathe in while the bolt works.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 5 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point. Remember: a short reload is 2.5 s and an empty one is 3.9 s, so the last round is your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.
