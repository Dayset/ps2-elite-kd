The Nanite of the Living Dead event pistol — a plasma hose that fires 125-damage bolts at every range, with no damage falloff, no aim-down-sights, no attachments, and no reload. It runs on heat instead of bullets, and it overheats if you get greedy.

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you hold the trigger down while you're still running. There is no sight to snap into on this gun — no ADS at all — so the cone you start with is the cone you keep, and the heat meter is the only thing that stops you. Your bolts spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair, then the gun overheats and locks you out. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip on most guns widens it further still — but not this one: the Ectoblaster's hip bloom is 0, so the cone you start with is the cone you keep.

Then you held the trigger — and the gun overheated. There is no aim-down-sights to reach for, so the mistake here is different: a full hold dumps 20 shots into the heat pool and locks the gun out for about 4.2 s. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking fire, then suddenly no gun at all.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and the heat bleeds off only after a 0.75 s delay. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is confetti, and your last half-second is a lockout.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Steady. Let the hip cone settle at 1.5° standing (1.0° crouched). There is no sight to raise — the settle is the aim.

3 · Fire in strings. Now fire — 8–10 shots, release, let the heat bleed off, fire again. Starting from the settled hip cone, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the heat from zero instead of inheriting a lockout.

Stop → steady → fire in strings. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Headshots save 29% of the kill time at close range — and with a 200 ms first-shot delay added, the close chest kill takes 1.75 s against 1.31 s for headshots, still a 25% saving. Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Flat 125 damage from 0 to 100 m and beyond. There is no range where this gun gets weaker — only ranges where the slow plasma takes too long to arrive.

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (on most guns, holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot — on this gun it does not: hip bloom is 0, so the cone never grows). The Ectoblaster has no fire-mode switch and no aim-down-sights — full-auto hipfire only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger and your heat meter, not a setting on the gun.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Fire in strings, not one long hold

    Heat builds 100 per shot against a 1999 pool — about 20 shots before it overheats. Fire 8–10 shots, let the heat bleed off for a second (750 per second after a 0.75 s delay), then fire again. An overheated gun locks you out for about 4.2 s.

  2. Aim at the chest — headshots pay half

    The headshot multiplier is 1.5x, not the usual 2x: 6 headshots to kill versus 8 to the chest. Headshots still help (1.11 s instead of 1.55 s), but the gap is smaller than on any other pistol — don't chase the head at the cost of missing.

  3. Hipfire is the only fire — so master it

    There is no aim-down-sights on this gun at all. Your standing hip cone is 1.5° and it never grows: the 0.21 m ring at 8 m stays 0.21 m on shot 1 and shot 19. Crouch to tighten it to 1.0° (a 0.14 m ring) when you can.

  4. Use the splash

    Every bolt also deals splash damage — 75 within 0.5 m, fading to 25 at 1.5 m. Aim at the floor by a wounded enemy's feet, or into a doorway, and the splash finishes what the direct hit started.

  5. Swap, don't cool down, mid-fight

    When the heat meter is high, swapping to your primary is faster than waiting for the bleed-off. The Ectoblaster is a finisher: it comes out when the primary is dry and goes away the moment it gets hot.

  6. Keep moving — you cannot scope in

    Without ADS you have no reason to stand still. Strafe while you fire: your hip cone is the same moving as standing still at the start, and a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back.

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Ectoblaster pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · The slowest kill here by far

Eight chest shots in 1.55 s is the slowest time to kill of these six pistols — nearly twice the Emissary's. Anything that trades shots with you wins.

Play around it: Play around it: Don't trade. The Ectoblaster finishes wounded enemies and punishes stationary ones; it does not win fair duels.

2 · No aim-down-sights at all

Every other pistol tightens to a 0.15–0.30° cone when scoped. Yours stays at 1.5° standing, 1.0° crouched, forever.

Play around it: Play around it: Fight inside 10 m, where the 0.21 m ring still fits inside his chest, and crouch when you hold a doorway.

3 · Heat, not a magazine

About 20 shots and the gun locks out for roughly 4.2 s — the longest forced pause of any sidearm here.

Play around it: Play around it: Fire in strings of 8–10 and let it bleed off between them. Never hold the trigger until it overheats in a fight you are losing.

4 · Headshots pay less

A 1.5x multiplier instead of 2x means headshots save you only 29% of the kill time, the smallest headshot payoff of any pistol here.

Play around it: Play around it: Aim centre-chest and let the volume do the work. Chase the head only on stationary targets.

5 · Slow plasma — 150 m/s

The slowest projectile of these six pistols by a wide margin. At 25 m your bolt takes a sixth of a second to arrive, and a strafing target has moved.

Play around it: Play around it: Fight close, lead moving targets, and don't take the shot past 25 m unless the target is standing still.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • Heat Strings 4 minutes · heat management

    At 10 m, fire exactly 10 shots, stop, count to two, fire 10 more. Watch the heat meter — never let it overheat. Goal: three full strings without a single overheat lockout.

  • Crouch Ring 3 minutes · hipfire

    Crouch 8 m from a dummy and hold the trigger. The cone never grows — watch the group stay the same size from the first shot to the last. Stand up and repeat: feel the ring widen to 0.21 m.

  • Splash the Feet 3 minutes · splash

    Aim at the floor at a dummy's feet and kill it with splash-assisted hits. Learn how close the bolt has to land — inside 0.5 m for full splash, 1.5 m for the last of it.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · movement

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while firing. You cannot scope in, so movement is your only defence — make it automatic.

  • Swap Drill 2 minutes · habit

    Empty your primary into a dummy, swap to the Ectoblaster, and finish the dummy before the heat runs out. Repeat until the swap is automatic.
