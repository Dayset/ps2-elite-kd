01

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 167 damage up close, fading to 100 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising — and remember what this gun is for: it finishes fights your primary started, so the kill usually costs fewer of these bullets than the table says.

Perfect-accuracy times to kill (chest): 0.67 s at 5 m, 0.67 s at 10 m, 0.80 s at 25 m, 0.93 s at 40 m. All headshots: 0.27 s at 5 m. In real fights you’ll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun. A 16-round magazine holds 2.7 full chest-kills at close range, fewer if you miss.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 15 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 60 m.

Rule of thumbInside 15 m you are at maximum power. Past 60 m every kill takes an extra bullet or two — and past 25 m a pistol is a peashooter: close the distance, or let your primary do the talking and keep the pistol for the finish.

0–10 mClose

Hipfire — don’t aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving — and tap, don’t mash: one click per shot, at the gun’s own 133 ms rhythm.

Scoping in costs you time and drops your speed to 0.75× — at arm’s length that gets you killed. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you’re hard to track. This is the pistol’s home ground: the fight your primary couldn’t finish, at the range where a sidearm is fastest to bring to bear.

10–25 mMid

ADS, upper chest, disciplined fire

Aim: neck / upper chest. Tap steadily — one click per shot, 133 ms apart. Start your crosshair at the neck: the recoil climbs into the head for free damage.

This is the longest range a pistol should be your first choice. Full damage holds to 15 m, and the aimed cone is a fraction of the hipfire cone. Strafe while scoped; at 0.75× you move faster aimed than a scoped Heavy, and a moving target still beats a still one.

25 m +Far

Tap-fire only — or don’t engage

Aim: chest. Slow, deliberate taps with a real pause between them. Past 25 m every round has to count, because the magazine only holds 16.

At 100 damage you need 8 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets only. Otherwise, your better play is to reload your primary, or close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot your pistol is a skill too.

03

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you work the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (every shot grows that cone). The The Immortal is semi-automatic — one click, one shot, so “firing modes” are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

Aimed (ADS), strafing0.30°The moving floor. The moving cone equals the standing cone from the very first shot (nStill ADS = 0), so strafing costs you nothing in cone size on this gun. You still move at 0.75× while scoped. Strafe between shots to stay hard to hit; plant your feet during each shot.

Hipfire, the numbers behind it: standing still you get 4 shots from the standing cone before bloom pushes it past the moving cone — after that, standing still buys you nothing over strafing, so keep moving. Aimed, that number is 0: the ADS cone is already at its moving floor, so strafe from the first ADS shot.

Try it: the tap-fire rhythm

A target 10 m away, aiming down sights. Click TAP TO FIRE once per shot — one click, one bullet, at the gun’s own 133 ms rhythm. Clicks faster than that are wasted: the gun can’t fire yet, and your aim still moves. Watch the cone grow with each shot and shrink when you pause. Steady taps at the refire rhythm are the whole skill. Tap — pause — tap: the tap-fire rhythm

Every shot widens your cone of fire (“bloom”). Pausing — or re-aiming between bursts — resets it. Same 18 rounds, two very different outcomes:

The target pattern is steady taps at the 133 ms refire rhythm — one click per shot, no faster. Clicks faster than the gun can fire are wasted, and they still move your aim. Tapping at the rhythm keeps every shot inside the cone you actually have; mashing just decorates the wall behind your enemy. Short, steady taps also leave you rounds for the second enemy — and with a 16-round magazine, you can’t afford to waste them.

Iron sights — what ships with the gun

No zoom, no dot — a front post inside a rear ring. The target at 25 m is small and the post covers his chest at longer ranges. Usable, but it eats a lot of your view — the first thing to replace.

1x Reflex Sight — 30 certs

Same target, same 25 m — but the post is gone and a red dot sits on his chest. Nothing blocks your edges, and the dot is faster to place than a post. Best everywhere a pistol fights. Pistols take 1x sights only — there is no magnified optic for a sidearm.

That’s the whole 30-cert decision from section 05 in one picture: iron sights for your first hour, the 1x reflex for everything after.

07

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Swap, don’t reload

    Your primary just ran dry mid-fight. Drawing your sidearm is faster than reloading it — that is the pistol’s entire reason to exist. Train the swap until it’s one motion: primary clicks empty, pistol is already out. The kill you finish with the The Immortal is a kill your reload would have lost.

  2. Tap at the rhythm, never faster

    Your gun fires one shot per 133 ms — clicks faster than that do nothing but move your aim. One click, one shot, at the rhythm. The simulator in section 03 grades your taps: steady taps at 133 ms keep every shot inside the cone you actually have.

  3. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 kills up close versus 6 to the chest, in 0.27 s instead of 0.67 s. Don’t aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun’s natural upward climb carry shots into the skull. It’s the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it’s free.

  4. Reload early — the penalty is real, the habit is not

    Reloading takes 1.4 s, and a 16-round magazine empties fast when you’re finishing fights. Reload after every kill, behind cover, while your primary is back in your hands — never in the open, never mid-duel. A pistol with a full magazine is a second life; an empty one is a paperweight.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy’s head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: your ADS cone is already at its moving floor (nStill ADS = 0), so strafing costs you nothing and makes you dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. Hipfire is a weapon, not a fallback

    Your standing hip cone is 1.00°, and the 0.14 m ring at 8 m fits inside his chest. Inside ~8 m, don’t ADS at all: keep strafing and let the hipfire do the work. Past ~10 m the rings start leaking past him, and that’s the exact moment to switch to the sight.

08

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here’s where the The Immortal pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · 167 damage, 451 RPM — the middle of everything

Six chest shots in 0.67 s up close: faster than the Executive, slower than the President, and with no single trick to lean on.

Play around it: Win on consistency, not gimmicks. Steady taps at the 133 ms rhythm, neck-high aim, and the headshots that come free with the climb.

2 · 16 rounds and NO extended magazine

The Immortal cannot mount an Extended Magazine — 16 rounds is all you get, fewer than three full chest-kills per magazine.

Play around it: Reload after every kill (1.45 s) and count your taps. There is no attachment that fixes this; the habit has to.

3 · The longest full-damage reach of the directives

Full damage holds to 15 m — longer than the Executive or President — but by 40 m you’re at 100 damage needing 8 chest shots.

Play around it: Use the 15 m window: fight at 10–15 m, where you’re at full power and the other directive pistols have started to fall off.

4 · The barest attachment rail of the four

Suppressor, Flash Suppressor, Darklight, Laser Sight — no Compensator, no Extended Magazine, no special ammunition.

Play around it: That’s fine: the stock round is the fastest-killing one, and the recoil is mild. Spend the certs on the optic and the Laser Sight.

5 · The slowest directive bullet

325 m/s — the President and Executive both fly at 375 m/s. At 25 m you’re already leading a strafing target.

Play around it: Fight inside 15 m, where the lead is negligible, and aim at the chest of a moving target rather than chasing his head.

09

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Swap Drill 3 minutes · the pistol’s real job

    Empty your primary into a dummy, then swap to the The Immortal and finish it. Repeat until the swap is one motion and the first pistol shot lands within a second of the last primary shot.

  • Tap Rhythm 4 minutes · trigger discipline

    At 10 m, kill dummies with steady taps at the 133 ms rhythm — one click per shot, no faster. Count hits per magazine. Goal: whole kills without a single wasted click.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly half your magazine, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it’s automatic — in real fights you’ll do it without thinking, which is the point.
