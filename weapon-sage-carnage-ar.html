First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

What you did — the spray

Three moments, one bad habit. Read left to right.

Read it this way: the disaster is set before the sight ever comes up. Sprinting puts your cone at 7.0°, hipfiring widens it further (+0.10° per shot), and pressing ADS does not shrink a cone that is already bloomed — the scope only gives you a precise picture. Your bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running.

The fix — three beats, in order

Same courtyard, same enemy. Three beats, in order — never two at once.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

Under ~8 m? Skip the whole dance.

Inside about 8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving. No sight to raise, no settling to wait for, and stopping is optional: the hipfire ring at 8 m is only 0.21 m wide standing still, so it fits well inside his chest. The three-beat fix is a mid-range habit; up close, your movement is your defence.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.10° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, a quarter-second of patience is cheaper than a magazine of misses.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.10°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

The one exceptionInside ~8 metres, don't ADS at all — hipfire and keep moving (section 03). The disaster above is a mid-range problem: close enough that you reached for the sight, far enough that the spray missed. When in doubt: release everything, aim, then shoot.

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 143 damage up close, fading to 112 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

RangeDamageHeadChestLegsChest vs shielded Heavy
Close · 0–10 m14347811
Mid · 11–39 m143→12548912
Far · 40 m+125→112591013

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.24 s, 7 chest shots ≈ 0.48 s up close. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun.

Damage falloff — where your bullets get weaker

Full power to 10 m, then a steady slide to minimum at 60 m. There is no Soft Point Ammunition on this gun — no second line, no way to stretch the 143 damage further. The 125 mark at 39 m is the last range where 8 chest shots still kill.

Rule of thumbInside 10 m you are at maximum power. Past 40 m every kill takes one extra bullet — close the distance or burst carefully instead of spraying.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and drops your speed to 0.75× — at arm's length that gets you killed. The Carnage has the best hip accuracy of any NC rifle. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let your Nano-Regen Device (F) top you up between duels.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage to 10 m, still 138 at 18 m, mild recoil, fast handling. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural upward climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in 0.24 s. Strafe while scoped; at 0.75× you move faster aimed than a scoped Heavy or a scoped Gauss Rifle, and a moving target still beats a still one.

25–60 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull straight down

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding from ~134 toward 112 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs dead vertical (0° angle, no sideways drift), so pull your mouse straight down. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

60 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 112 damage you need 9 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The Carnage AR has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

First buy100 certs

Cuts side-to-side recoil by 25%, keeping your bursts on a single target. Tiny cost: weapon swap is 0.15 s slower.

Good: the default for aimed, mid-range play — this gun's horizontal recoil (0.275–0.30) is its worst trait, and the grip is the fix.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Nano-Regen on, then peek

    Your Nano-Regen Device (F) starts healing you the moment you switch it on. Pop it before you round the corner, not after you've already lost half your health — it won't out-heal incoming fire, but it tops you back up between duels so you walk into the next one at full strength.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural upward climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Reload early — the penalty is small, the habit is not

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.285 s; an empty gun takes 2.6 s — only ~0.3 s more, so a dry magazine won't doom you. But 30 rounds still vanish fast: after one kill and a few misses you're at half a mag. Duck behind cover and reload early anyway — the habit is what saves you on the gun that doesn't forgive.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: at 0.75× aimed speed you're faster than almost anyone scoped in against you, and a moving Medic is dramatically harder to headshot back.

  6. After the kill, revive before you chase

    You're a Medic — the fight doesn't end when the enemy dies. A revived teammate is a second gun, and your medical applicator wins fights your rifle can't. Check for downed friendlies before you chase the next red dot, top up your own health, then move. And pick fights inside 40 metres: your damage, accuracy and strafe speed all shine there.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Carnage AR pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · Your own starter out-damages you

The NC1 Gauss Rifle — the free rifle you started with — kills in 6 chest shots to your 7, and 3 headshots to your 4. It beats your time to kill by 20–60 ms in every band, and its damage holds out to 90 m.

Play around it: don't trade chest shots. Your 750 RPM forgives a miss (80 ms) better than its 600 RPM (100 ms), your aimed strafe is faster, and your headshots close the gap entirely — 4 to kill up close, 0.24 s.

2 · Only 30 rounds — small for 750 RPM

At 750 RPM a held trigger eats the whole magazine in 2.4 seconds. Two kills and a few misses and you're dry — and the Heavy Magazine only buys you 5 more rounds.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Your 2.285 s reload is among the fastest of any assault rifle — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

3 · The first shot kicks hardest

The first shot of every burst kicks 2.5× as hard as the rest — 0.62° of vertical jump before bloom even starts. In a burst, shot one is the wild one.

Play around it: the Angled Forward Grip cuts that first-shot kick by 60%, or start your aim a touch low and let the first round climb into the chest.

4 · The sideways walk

Horizontal recoil of 0.275–0.30 with a tolerance of 0.8625 is the worst sideways wander of any rifle on this page — the gun drifts left and right with no pattern to learn, and long bursts walk off the target's shoulders.

Play around it: the Forward Grip trims the sideways part by 25%, and short bursts (habit 3) never give the wander time to build.

5 · Long range is a pea-shooter, and no ammo fixes it

Past 60 m you're at 112 damage: 9 chest shots, 13 against a shielded Heavy. And unlike the TAR or GR-22, you can't buy Soft Point Ammunition to stretch your full damage to 15 m — 143 ends at 10 m, full stop.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a Medic — the front line is your address, and your revive is worth more than a 60 m plink.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb straight up — that's your gun's fingerprint (0° angle, no sideways drift). Repeat, this time pulling straight down, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement and Nano-Regen do the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
