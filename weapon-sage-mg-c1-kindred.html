MG-C1 Kindred — the fast opener, mastered

A 1,000-cert carbine for the TR Light Assault and Engineer — hard-hitting 167-damage rounds with a trick up its sleeve: the first seven rounds of every burst leave the barrel at 698 RPM before the gun settles to 550. This page teaches you to win fights with it today.

The one-line verdict: a hard-hitting carbine that wins fights in the first half-second. Every fresh burst opens at 698 RPM, so short bursts are faster as well as more accurate — and the 2.5× first-shot kick punishes you if you yank the trigger. Fight between 10 and 40 metres and it will carry you.

01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.12° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — take it behind cover, or while the enemy is still turning toward you.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.03°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

02

Know your damage

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 167 damage up close, fading to 125 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising — and one twist that makes this gun special:

The fast opening — read this firstThe Kindred does not fire at one speed. The first seven rounds of every fresh burst leave the barrel at 698 RPM (one shot every 86 ms), and only then does the gun settle to its sustained 550 RPM (one shot every 109 ms). Every kill in the table below lands inside that fast opening — so the times to kill here are the fast ones, not the 550 RPM ones. Release the trigger and your next burst starts fast again. Short bursts aren't just more accurate on this gun — they're quicker, too.

Perfect-accuracy times to kill, using the fast opening: 3 headshots ≈ 0.17 s, 6 chest shots ≈ 0.43 s up close; 7 chest shots ≈ 0.52 s at mid range, 8 ≈ 0.63 s far. At the sustained 550 RPM, full-damage output is 1,531 damage per second — while the opening lasts, it's about 1,943. In real fights you'll land maybe half your rounds — still, this is why the head matters so much on this gun. Two footnotes on the mid band: legs take 7 shots out to about 22 m and 8 past it, and a shielded Heavy takes 9 out to about 41 m before the count climbs to 11 at the far end of the band. One sentence for the shopper: the free TRAC-5 fires 750 RPM at 143 damage with a 40-round magazine, so the Kindred's 1,000 certs buy you harder-hitting 167-damage bullets and the 698 RPM opening — but a smaller 30-round magazine.

How to fight hereAim down sights at the upper chest and neck. Fire in bursts of 5 — every fresh burst opens at 698 RPM, and your recoil climbs up-left into the head for free damage.

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The Kindred has genuinely tight hipfire for a carbine (1.75° standing), so trust it. Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let the 698 RPM opening do the killing before he can answer.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: near-full 167 damage, a tight 0.03° aimed cone, and every burst opening at 698 RPM. Start your crosshair at the neck — the natural up-left climb walks your shots into the head, and 3 head hits end the fight in under a fifth of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down-right

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding toward 143 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset — and to restart the 698 RPM opening — fire again. The recoil drifts up and to the left (11–12°), so pull your mouse gently down and to the right. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 125 damage you need 8 chest hits, and your bullets are slow (480 m/s) — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover — as a Light Assault, your jump jets are made for exactly this. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The Kindred has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun. And your finger also controls the fire rate: every fresh burst starts at 698 RPM, so how you hold the trigger decides how fast the gun shoots, not just how straight.

The ADAD window — 4 shotsYour aimed cone grows 0.06° per shot from 0.03°, and the strafing cone starts at 0.30°. The gap (0.27°) divided by the bloom (0.06°) gives an ADAD window of 4 shots: the first 4 rounds of a planted burst are fired from a cone tighter than anything strafing gives you. From the hip the window is also 4 shots (1.75° growing 0.12° per shot against a 2.25° strafing cone). That window is not a freeze — the cone grows from the very first shot — it's the reason the ideal burst is 5: plant, fire, strafe, repeat.

Hold – release – hold – release: the burst rhythm

Every shot widens your cone of fire ("bloom"). Releasing the trigger for a heartbeat resets it — and restarts the 698 RPM opening. Same 18 rounds, two very different outcomes:

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.06° per shot aimed, 0.12° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him — and past round seven you're firing at the slow 550 RPM as well. Short bursts also leave you rounds for the second enemy — remember, the magazine is only 30.

RTA Reflex Sight — 1x

No zoom. The target at 35 m is small but the view is wide open — you see the crate, the tower, and the next enemy coming. Fast to aim, nothing blocks your edges. Best inside ~40 m.

MH2 Reflex Sight — 2x

Same target, same 35 m — but he looks twice as tall, so placing shots on his chest and head gets much easier. The price: your view narrows to the round window, the crate and tower are gone, and close targets feel fast and huge. Best at 25–50 m.

That's the whole 30-cert decision from section 06 in one picture: 1x for awareness, 2x for precision. Most veterans carry both and swap per base — indoors 1x, open ground 2x.

Attachments: what to buy, what to skip

Every slot, judged

Good news first: the list is short, and the gun is nearly complete as it ships. Two gaps to know before you spend: there is no Suppressor, no Compensator, no High Velocity Ammunition and no Extended Magazine for the Kindred — you will always show on the minimap when firing, the 2.5× first-shot kick can't be bought down, and 30 rounds is all you ever get. Everything below is an optional sidegrade, not an upgrade. Buy in the order shown and stop whenever the gun feels right.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Fresh bursts fire fastest

    Every burst you start opens at 698 RPM for seven rounds. That makes the release between bursts do double duty: it resets your cone of fire and restarts the fast rate. A player who holds the trigger is firing a 550 RPM gun; a player who bursts is firing a 698 RPM gun. Same weapon, different fire rate — decided by your finger.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 3 kills up close versus 6 to the chest, in about 0.17 s. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's natural up-left climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it — and past round seven they're slow, too. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets, your fire rate resets, and your hits per magazine roughly double.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.25 s; an empty gun takes 2.9 s — and with a 30-round magazine you can't afford either mid-fight. Count roughly: after one kill, or about 15 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe between bursts: a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back, and your planted bursts stay tighter than your strafing cone for the first 4 rounds anyway.

  6. Pick fights inside 40 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and fast opening all shine at close-mid range. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones — and as a Light Assault, use your jump jets to arrive above or behind your target at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the Kindred pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · Only 30 rounds — and no extended magazine exists

Thirty rounds with a 698 RPM opening burns fast: one clean kill is 6 rounds, a messy one is 15, and there's no extended magazine to buy your way out of it. Two kills per magazine if you're clean, one if you're not.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Your 2.25 s reload is quick for a carbine — reloading often is the design, not a failure.

2 · The first shot kicks hardest

The first round of every burst jumps with a 2.5× multiplier — about a 0.8° hop, the harshest first-shot kick in its class. Yank the trigger and your opening shot climbs off the head before the burst even starts. And unlike most guns, there's no Compensator or Angled Forward Grip to buy it down — the attachment list simply doesn't offer one.

Play around it: start each burst a touch low and left, and let the up-left climb walk into the target. The Forward Grip trims the sideways part by 25% — it's the only help on offer, so take it.

3 · The fast rate only lasts seven rounds

Hold the trigger past round seven and the Kindred is just a 550 RPM gun — its whole reason to exist, gone. A long spray here is worse than on other guns: slower and wilder at the same time.

Play around it: never hold past 8 rounds at range (habit 1). The release is what makes this gun special — use it.

4 · Slow bullets, short reach

480 m/s is slow for a carbine, and Soft Point makes it 432 m/s — moving targets past 40 m need real lead. Past 70 m you're at 125 damage: 8 chest shots, 12 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, lead runners by a body width, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. You're a Light Assault or an Engineer — the front line is your address.

5 · Aiming makes you slow

Like most 167-damage carbines, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5×), and the gun takes 0.55 s to swap to — slow if a fight surprises you with your rocket launcher or repair tool out.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up-left — that's your gun's fingerprint, and the first shot jumps hardest (2.5×, about 0.8°). Repeat, this time pulling down-right, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — and feel the rhythm: every fresh burst reopens at 698 RPM, so the release is doing two jobs at once.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending. Your 1.75° hip cone is tight enough that this actually works.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 20 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 30-round magazine and a 2.25 s reload, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
