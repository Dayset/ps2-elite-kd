01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.05° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, your first aimed shot is worth more than three sprayed ones.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.30°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

How many bullets
to kill, by range

Every enemy infantry has 1,000 health (500 shield + 500 health). Your bullet does 125 damage up close, fading to 91 at long range. Headshots count double, leg shots count less (×0.9). That gives you three numbers worth memorising:

0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and slows you to 0.75× speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The MKV Suppressed has excellent hipfire for an SMG (1.00° standing, a 0.14 m ring at 8 m). Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track. You're off the minimap even while firing — use the confusion, and reposition after the kill.

8–20 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: 125–119 damage across the band (8–9 chest shots to kill), a up and to the right (8–14° angle) climb, and a 30-round magazine behind it. Start your crosshair at the neck — the climb walks your shots into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in about 0.23 s. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one — and on this gun, strafing while aimed doesn't widen your cone at all.

20–40 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5–8 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding from 119 toward 94 (9–11 chest shots) and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs up and to the right (8–14° angle), so pull your mouse gently down-left — one direction only. Strafing while aimed costs you nothing in cone size here, so keep moving between bursts.

40 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 91 damage you need 11 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The MKV Suppressed has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Fight at your range

    Your damage, accuracy and 30-round magazine all shine inside 20 metres. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 8 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's up-and-to-the-right climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double. And resist the 30-round magazine: it tempts you to hold the trigger, and a long spray is still a spray.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.6 s; an empty gun takes 2.8 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after two or three kills or ~15 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 5 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back — and on this gun, strafing while aimed costs you nothing in cone size (0.30° either way).

  6. Stay off the map — and stay moving

    You are never on the enemy minimap, not even while firing. Use that: kill one enemy, reposition before the next one arrives, and never fight from the same doorway twice. The MKV's whole advantage is that the enemy doesn't know where you are — the moment you stand still and trade fairly, you've thrown it away for nothing.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the MKV Suppressed pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · It loses the pure DPS race up close

779 RPM at 125 damage kills with chest shots in 0.54 s. The SMG-46 Armistice does it in 0.47 s — 13.0% faster, 70 ms — and even its unsuppressed twin the NS-7 is 3.9% slower than you, not faster. The suppressor costs you the race against every unsuppressed gun.

Play around it: don't trade fairly. Your edge is silence, not speed: flank, kill from behind, and be gone before the return fire arrives.

2 · The first shot kicks hardest

The first round of every burst jumps with a 2.0× multiplier. Yank the trigger and your opening shot climbs off the head before the burst even starts.

Play around it: start each burst a touch low and let the climb walk into the target — your recoil climbs up and to the right at 8–14°, so pull down-left.

3 · Reloads are quick — and the gap is tiny

2.6 s with rounds left, 2.84 s on empty — the gap between a partial and an empty reload is only 0.24 s, the smallest in the class. That's the good news; the bad news is 2.6 s is still a long time when a second enemy rounds the corner.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4) — and remember, on this gun an empty reload barely costs more than a partial one.

4 · Long range is a pea-shooter

Past 42 m you're at 91 damage: 11 chest shots, 16 against a shielded Heavy, with bloom fighting you the whole way. Carbines and battle rifles own that ground — and your own twin the NS-7 still deals 100 damage out there.

Play around it: tap-fire at exposed targets only, and spend the rest of the time closing distance. That said, at 42 m your time to kill is 0.77 s — range is where this gun stops losing.

5 · Aiming makes you slow

Like all SMGs, you move at 0.75× speed while aiming down sights — a quarter slower than walking — and the gun takes 0.65 s to swap to, slow if a fight surprises you with your sidearm out.

Play around it: hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea), and swap weapons behind cover, never in the open.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up and to the right at 8–14° — that's your gun's fingerprint, and the first shot jumps hardest (2.0×). Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 20–30 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — even though your aimed cone starts at just 0.30°, bloom still moves the later shots.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 15 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 2.6 s reload, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
