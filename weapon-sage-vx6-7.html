01

First lesson: stop, aim, then fire

The mistake almost
every new player makes

Picture the scene, because you've probably lived it: you're sprinting across a courtyard, an enemy appears, and you do everything at once — hold the trigger down while you're still running, then press aim-down-sights (ADS) a split-second later, finger still down. The sight picture snaps in, it looks precise… and your bullets spray everywhere except the man in your crosshair. He kills you. It feels like the game cheated. It didn't — here's what actually happened.

✗ What you did — the spray

You started firing from a sprint, from the hip. Sprinting is the least accurate state in the game: your cone of fire starts at 7.0° — fully bloomed, the widest it can ever be. Every shot you fire from the hip widens it further still (+0.10° per shot).

Then you pressed ADS mid-hold — and nothing reset. Aiming down sights does not shrink a cone of fire that is already bloomed. The scope only gives you a precise picture; the bullets keep flying out of the wide cone you built while running and hipfiring. That's the whole mystery: accurate-looking aim, wildly inaccurate gun.

Two timers stack the deck further: your gun needs about 0.3 s to settle after a sprint, and raising the sight itself takes ~0.15 s. Trigger held through all of it means your first half-second of fire is pure confetti.

✓ The fix — three beats, in order

1 · Feet. Let go of sprint (and the trigger, if you're already holding it). Stop, or drop into a strafe — anything but a full sprint.

2 · Sight. Press ADS and let the sight come up. This takes a heartbeat — that's fine, the sight is up in 0.15 s.

3 · Trigger. Now fire. Starting from the aimed cone of 0.10°, your first shots land where the dot points, and you control the bloom from zero instead of inheriting a maxed-out cone.

Stop → aim → fire. If you take one habit from this entire page, take this one. You'll practise exactly this rhythm in the simulator in section 04.

Perfect-accuracy times to kill: 4 headshots ≈ 0.23 s, 7 chest shots ≈ 0.45 s up close. At 800 RPM, full-damage output is 1,907 damage per second. 0–8 mPoint blank

Hipfire — don't aim down sights

Aim: centre of the chest, from the hip. Keep moving.

Scoping in costs you time and halves your move speed — at arm's length that gets you killed. The VX6-7 has tight hipfire for a carbine (1.25° standing, 1.75° on the move). Strafe side to side (A/D) while firing so you're hard to track, and let your jump jets (Light Assault) or your position (Engineer) do the defending.

8–25 mSweet spot

ADS, upper chest, confident bursts

Aim: neck / upper chest. Bursts of 5 rounds.

This is where the gun earns its keep: full 143 damage to 10 m, an 8-shot kill out to about 31 m, and 800 RPM behind it. Start your crosshair at the neck — the climb walks your shots up and to the right into the head, and 4 head hits end the fight in about a quarter of a second. Strafe while scoped; you're slower, but a moving target still beats a still one.

25–50 mControlled

ADS, short bursts, pull down-left

Aim: chest. Bursts of 5 rounds, pause between them.

Damage is sliding from 143 toward 100 and your cone of fire blooms if you hold the trigger. Fire a burst, release for a split second to let accuracy reset, fire again. The recoil climbs up and to the right (20–30° off vertical), so pull your mouse gently down-left — two directions, not one. Crouch or stand still when you can — both tighten your shots.

50 m +Plinking

Tap-fire only — or don't engage

Aim: chest. Taps of 3–5 rounds at exposed targets only.

At 100 damage you need 10 chest hits — a long, loud advertisement of your position, and at 465 m/s your bullets take 0.13 s just to reach 60 m. Take these shots at stationary or fleeing targets, medics reviving, and engineers on turrets. Otherwise, your better play is to close the distance behind cover. Knowing when not to shoot is a skill too.

04

Stances and firing modes

How you stand and
how you hold the trigger

Your accuracy is decided by two things before skill even enters it: what your body is doing (your stance sets the starting cone of fire) and what your finger is doing (holding the trigger grows that cone with every shot). The VX6-7 has no fire-mode switch — full-auto only — so "firing modes" are something you do with your finger, not a setting on the gun.

Stances — the cone you start with, before the first shot

StanceStarting coneWhat it means for you
Sprinting7.0°Maxed out — the widest the cone can be. This is the section 01 disaster: never start firing from a sprint.
Hipfire, moving1.75°Walking or strafing from the hip. Fine inside ~8 m, leaks rounds past the target beyond it.
Hipfire, standing1.25°Your point-blank default. The 0.17 m ring at 8 m in section 05 is this stance drawn out.
Hipfire, crouched1.0°The tightest hipfire you have (1.25° if you crouch-walk). Crouch when holding a doorway from the hip.
Aimed (ADS), feet planted0.10°Standing or crouched makes no difference here — twelve and a half times tighter than hipfire. This is why section 01's order matters.
Aimed (ADS), strafing0.35°Three and a half times the planted cone, and you move at half speed (0.5×). Strafe between bursts to stay hard to hit; plant your feet during each burst.

The strafe window: strafing while aimed starts at 0.35°, and your planted cone is 0.10° with 0.05° of bloom per shot — so the first 5 shots of a planted burst grow your cone to exactly the strafing floor (0.10° + 5 × 0.05° = 0.35°). In other words, the first 5 shots cost you nothing extra if you strafe instead of planting — but past 5, the planted burst pulls ahead. Hipfire works the same way: 5 shots before a planted burst grows past the 1.75° moving floor (1.25° + 5 × 0.10°). Crouched and aimed, the window is 2 shots (0.20° crouch-walk floor, 0.10° start). This is the ADAD window, not a freeze: the cone grows from shot 1, it just hasn't passed the moving floor yet.

Recoil, by the numbers: vertical 0.30° per shot, horizontal ±0.25° with a 0.7° tolerance, drifting 20–30° up and to the right, recovering at 18°/s after 75 ms — and the first shot of every burst kicks 3× (0.90°). That first-shot kick is why the Angled Forward Grip (section 06) earns its keep here. The gun takes 0.55 s to swap to, ~0.15 s to raise the sight, and 0.3 s to settle after a sprint.

The target pattern is bursts of about 5 rounds — that's the ideal. Bursts of 6–8 are fine while you're new (call it newbie tolerance). Past that, the bloom (0.05° per shot aimed, 0.10° from the hip) keeps growing with every shot, so the extra bullets mostly decorate the wall behind your enemy instead of hitting him. 08

Kill efficiently: six habits

Technique beats
attachments

  1. Stop before you shoot

    The whole of section 01 in one habit: feet, then sight, then trigger. The 0.45 s kill only exists if the first shots land — and from a sprint your cone starts at 7.0°, so the first half-second of a running burst is confetti. Arrive at your range, stop, then open fire.

  2. Aim at the neck, harvest the head

    Headshots deal double damage: 4 kills up close versus 7 to the chest. Don't aim at the head directly — aim at the neck and let the gun's up-and-to-the-right climb carry shots two and three into the skull. It's the single biggest damage upgrade you own, and it's free.

  3. Burst, release, burst

    Every shot widens your cone of fire. Holding the trigger past ~8 rounds at range means later bullets fly around your target, not into it. Five rounds is the ideal burst; release the trigger for a heartbeat between bursts — your accuracy resets and your hits per magazine roughly double. And respect the 30-round magazine: at 800 RPM it empties in 2.18 s, and a long spray is still a spray.

  4. Never reload on empty

    Reloading with even 1 bullet left takes 2.15 s; an empty gun takes 2.76 s — an eternity mid-fight. Count roughly: after two kills or ~15 rounds, duck behind cover and reload early. Treat the last 10 rounds as your emergency reserve, not your working magazine.

  5. Pre-aim the doorway, then strafe

    Walk with your crosshair at head height, pointed where an enemy's head will appear — a doorway edge, a corner, stairs. Most beginners lose fights in the half-second spent dragging the crosshair onto target. While firing, tap A/D to strafe: a moving target is dramatically harder to headshot back — but strafe between bursts, since your aimed cone jumps to 0.35° on the move.

  6. Pick fights inside 30 metres

    Your damage, accuracy and fire rate all shine at close-mid range, and your 8-shot kill holds out to about 31 m. Sprint between cover, use buildings and rocks as stepping stones, and arrive at your range before opening fire. The player who chooses the range usually wins the duel — make it you.

09

The honest weak spots

Know them,
play around them

Every gun has a price. Here's where the VX6-7 pays — and the habit that covers each hole.

1 · The magazine is small for the fire rate

30 rounds at 800 RPM is 2.18 s of trigger — four close-range chest kills per magazine, then a 2.15 s reload. A second enemy rounding the corner while you reload is the classic VX6-7 death.

Play around it: burst discipline (habit 3) and early reloads (habit 4). Never take a 1-vs-2 with fewer than 15 rounds in the mag.

2 · The first shot kicks hardest

The first round of every burst jumps with a 3× multiplier — a 0.90° kick up and to the right before the burst even starts. Yank the trigger and your opening shot climbs off the head.

Play around it: start each burst a touch low and left and let the climb walk into the target, or fit the Angled Forward Grip — it cuts that first-shot kick by 60%.

3 · Damage falls off fast

The 7-shot kill lasts only to about 10 m. By 31 m it's 8 shots, by 47 m it's 9, and past that it's 10 — and the chest time to kill stretches from 0.45 s to 0.675 s on the way out.

Play around it: Soft Point Ammunition stretches full damage to 15 m, and habit 6 keeps you inside 30 m where the gun is honest.

4 · Aiming makes you slow and sloppy on the move

Like all carbines, you move at half speed while aiming down sights (0.5×), and your aimed cone jumps from 0.10° planted to 0.35° strafing — three and a half times wider.

Play around it: plant your feet during each burst and strafe between them; hipfire inside 8 m instead of scoping (that's the Point Hold build's whole idea).

5 · Slow bullets

465 m/s is unhurried — a bullet takes 0.13 s to reach 60 m, and with Soft Point (419 m/s) or a Suppressor (395 m/s) it's slower still. Leading a sprinting target past 50 m is guesswork, and the guess usually misses.

Play around it: tap-fire at runners or don't engage at all; close the distance instead. At your real fighting ranges, 8–30 m, the flight time is 0.02–0.06 s and barely matters.

10

Training: five VR drills

VR Training area
~15 minutes a session

Open the world map, travel to VR Training, and run these on the target dummies. Five minutes before a play session beats an hour of dying confused.

  • The Wall Test 2 minutes · recoil

    Aim at a wall 20 m away and empty a full magazine without touching the mouse. Watch the bullet pattern climb up and to the right — that's your gun's fingerprint, and the first shot jumps hardest (3×). Repeat, this time pulling down-left, until the group stays fist-sized.

  • Neck-Line Walk 3 minutes · aim height

    Walk the dummy range keeping your crosshair glued to neck height the whole time, snapping to each new target without dipping to the floor or sky. Slow and clean first; speed comes on its own.

  • Burst Ladder 4 minutes · burst control

    At 30–40 m, kill dummies with bursts of 5, releasing the trigger fully between bursts. If a burst goes wide, shorten it. Goal: whole kills without a single wild round — even though your aimed cone caps at just 3.0°, bloom still moves the later shots.

  • Circle Strafe 3 minutes · hipfire

    Pick a dummy, stand 5 m away, and circle it with A/D while hipfiring it down without stopping. This is your point-blank survival skill — the one that fires while your movement does the defending.

  • Reload Discipline 3 minutes · habit

    Fire exactly 15 rounds, step behind a pillar, reload, repeat. Train your hands to reload early and only in cover until it's automatic — with a 2.15 s reload, in real fights you'll do it without thinking, which is the point.
